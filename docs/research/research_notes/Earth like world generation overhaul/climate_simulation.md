## 1. Insolation and temperature (insolation formula, EBMs, continentality, lapse, lag, ice-albedo)

### Takeaway
Daily-mean top-of-atmosphere insolation is a closed-form function of latitude and solar declination, so a
monthly forcing table per latitude row costs nothing. The cheapest physically grounded way to turn it into
monthly temperature is a seasonal diffusive energy-balance model (EBM) with a per-cell heat capacity (land
shallow, ocean deep): it produces seasonal amplitude, lag and continentality from one equation; every hobby
generator that has seasons instead hard-codes "annual mean by latitude ± amplitude by latitude, then nudge by
distance inland".

### Cited Findings
- Instantaneous flux: `Q = S0·(R0/RE)²·cosΘ` for cosΘ > 0 else 0, with `cosΘ = sinφ·sinδ + cosφ·cosδ·cos h`. Daily mean: `Q_day = (S0/π)·(R0/RE)²·[h0·sinφ·sinδ + cosφ·cosδ·sin h0]`, where the sunrise hour angle solves `cos h0 = −tanφ·tanδ`; if `tanφ·tanδ > 1` the sun never sets (h0 = π), if `< −1` it never rises (Q_day = 0). — [Wikipedia: Solar irradiance / Insolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolation)
- Declination: `sinδ = sinε·sinθ` (ε obliquity, θ orbital angle from the March equinox; `δ = ε·sinθ` is the small-tilt approximation). Distance factor: `R0/RE = (1 + e·cos(θ − ϖ)) / (1 − e²)`, e eccentricity, ϖ longitude of perihelion. Solar constant ≈ 1361 W/m² (1367 in older figures); simplified distance term `1 + 0.034·cos(2πn/365.25)`. — [Wikipedia: Solar irradiance / Insolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolation)
- 1-D seasonal diffusive EBM (the Budyko–Sellers–North family as packaged in climlab): `C ∂T/∂t = (1−α)·S(φ,t) − [A + B·T] + (1/cosφ) ∂/∂φ [cosφ · D · ∂T/∂φ]`. climlab defaults: A = 210 W/m², B = 2 W/m²/°C, D = 0.555 W/m²/°C, S0 = 1365.2 W/m², 90 latitude points, 90 time steps per year, heat capacity as a 10 m water-equivalent mixed layer; annual model albedo `a0 = 0.3, a2 = 0.078` (α = a0 + a2·P2(sinφ)), ice albedo `ai = 0.62` below a freezing threshold `Tf = −10 °C`; the seasonal model uses `a0 = 0.33, a2 = 0.25` and no ice feedback unless `ai` is supplied. — [climlab API: climlab.model.ebm](https://climlab.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/climlab.model.ebm.html)
- climlab's seasonal-cycle notebook runs that EBM with 2 m, 10 m and 50 m water depths and concludes the observed zonal-mean seasonal cycle is best matched by ~10 m effective depth (2 m too extreme, 50 m too flat). — [climlab courseware: Seasonal cycle and heat capacity](https://climlab.readthedocs.io/en/latest/courseware/Seasonal_cycle_and_heat_capacity.html)
- Observed seasonal lag: continental interiors/polar regions as little as 15–20 days in summer; mid-latitude continental climates 20–25 days (winter) and 25–35 days (summer); oceanic locations 2–3 months; western Europe about one month; examples — Fairbanks warmest early July, San Francisco warmest in September, Japan/Korea warmest in August, coldest January. — [Wikipedia: Seasonal lag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_lag)
- Azgaar (annual only): sea-level temperature is piecewise linear in latitude — inside the "tropics" band [−20°, +16°] it falls 0.15 °C per degree from the user's equator temperature, outside it falls linearly to the user's north/south pole temperatures; altitude drop is `6.5 °C per km` applied to `(h−18)^exponent` metres; stored as `Int8` °C. No seasons, no continentality in the generator. — [Azgaar temperature-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/model/physical/temperature-generator.ts)
- WorldEngine (annual only): `latitude_factor = interp(y, [tilt−0.5, tilt, tilt+0.5], [0,1,0])` (a triangle peaking at a randomly shifted "equator", axial tilt drawn from a normal with HWHM 0.07 of map height), `temp = (latitude_factor·12 + simplex_noise·1)/13 / distance_to_sun²`, then above `mountain_level` multiplied by `1 − (elev − mountain_level)/30` (floor 0.033). Unitless, no seasons. — [WorldEngine temperature.py](https://github.com/Mindwerks/worldengine/blob/master/worldengine/simulations/temperature.py)
- Undiscovered Worlds (January + July fields), actual code: equator–pole difference `tempdiff = −0.045·tilt² + 6.872·tilt − 231.7` (plus `3.5·(22.5−tilt)` if tilt < 22.5); `poleT = avg + 0.55·tempdiff`, `equatorT = avg − 0.36·tempdiff`; base temperature linear in latitude between them; sea cells +2 °C; ±4 °C fractal noise; seasonal swing `polarvariation = tilt·1.5555·1.5` °C at the poles scaling linearly to 0 at the equator (applied ± half to the two seasons, hemispheres mirrored); a high-obliquity "two summers" term `tilt·0.2946 − 2.454`; an eccentricity term `e²·100 + e·30` °C, stronger at the equator; then lapse `world.tempdecrease()` °C/km (default 6.5) above sea level. — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp `createtemperaturemap`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp), [misc.cpp `tempelevadd`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/misc.cpp)
- Undiscovered Worlds continentality, actual code (`adjustcontinentaltemperatures`): with `inland` = distance from sea in cells and `contstrength = tilt/22.5`, winter is lowered by `(Tmax−Tmin)·0.6·inland·contstrength` and summer raised with factor 0.05 (i.e. continentality mostly deepens winters, barely raises summers), tapering outside −10…30 °C. A second pass (`adjusttemperatures`) couples temperature to rain: winter rain warms winters (factor 0.08, cap ~10–15 °C), summer rain cools summers (cap 10 °C), rainless cells get hotter summers (×1.3) and colder winters (×1.2). Maritime winter warming is carried by the same wind-march that carries rain (`heatpickuprate 0.008`, `heatdepositrate 0.15`, `maxwinterheatfactor 3.5`, summer effect `tilt/75` of the winter one). — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- Frozen Fractal ("Around the World" game, January + July): two latitude curves per month — an oceanic one and a continental one — blended by a 0..1 continentality factor obtained by tracing the wind vector backward from each cell until it reaches sea and counting the travel time. Quoted check values at ~51°N: Antwerp 4.0 °C winter / 18.9 °C summer vs Astana −14.5 / 20.6. Annual version: curve of |lat|/90 crossing 0 °C near 60°, minus 6.5 °C/km, plus simplex noise. — [Frozen Fractal part 8: Seasons](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/19/around-the-world-8-seasons), [part 7: Temperature and rain](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/15/around-the-world-7-temperature-and-rain/)
- Tectonics.js: sea-level temperature from radiative equilibrium of absorbed insolation plus a guessed meridional heat flow (`T = ((absorbed + heat_flow)/ε / σ)^¼`, emission coefficient 0.83, uniform albedo 0.2 hard-coded with the albedo-from-snow/ocean/plants call commented out), then `− lapse_rate·height` with a default lapse of **3.5 K/km**; precipitation is `guess_precipitation_fluxes(lat)` — a function of latitude only. — [Tectonics.js Atmosphere.js](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/noncompiled/models/atmosphere/Atmosphere.js)
- Worldbuilding Pasta rules of thumb: lapse 6.5 °C/km in open air, lower on large plateaus, higher on isolated peaks (its Clima-Sim based workflow uses 4.46 °C/km); 1 km of altitude ≈ 8° of latitude poleward; interiors of plateaus above 4 km should average below 10 °C; warm-current west coasts at ~50–70° up to about +15 °C, cold-current east coasts about −10 °C, minor adjustments near 20°. — [Worldbuilding Pasta, An Apple Pie from Scratch part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)

### Inferences
- **Monthly forcing table.** For month m take θ = 2π(m + 0.5)/12 measured from the March equinox (ignoring Kepler's equation is fine for e = 0.0167), compute δ and R0/RE, then Q_day(φ) per latitude row. 12 × rows evaluations — free. With the equator on the map's centre line, φ = 90°·(1 − 2y/H).
- **Lag and amplitude from a slab, analytically** (my derivation from the linearised EBM `C dT/dt = F·cos ωt − B·T`): amplitude `= F / √(B² + (Cω)²)`, phase lag `= atan(Cω/B)`, ω = 2π/yr = 1.99e-7 s⁻¹, C = 4.2e6·h J/m²/K for h metres of water. With B = 2: h = 2 m → lag ≈ 40 days; h = 10 m → ≈ 78 days; h = 50 m → ≈ 89 days (a slab can never exceed 91 days). Diffusion (heat exchange with neighbours) raises the effective B and shortens these. This reproduces the cited "land ~1 month, ocean 2–3 months" if land uses an effective depth of roughly 1–2 m and ocean 40–70 m. It also shows amplitude and lag are not independent knobs: a generator that hard-codes them separately can pick values no slab could produce.
- **Recommended temperature model (tier 2, see section 6):** a 2-D seasonal EBM on a coarse grid (e.g. 256×128 or 512×256): per cell `C(x) ∂T/∂t = (1−α(x,T))·Q(φ,t) − (A + B·T) + ∇·(D∇T)`, with C from land/sea (and sea-ice), α from surface type with a snow/ice ramp, explicit or ADI diffusion, ~50–100 steps per year, spun up 20–40 years (ocean slab e-folding time C/B ≈ 3 years at 50 m... so ~10–15 years is already within a fraction of a degree). Output 12 monthly means of sea-level temperature, bilinearly upsampled to the full grid, then lapse-corrected per fine cell with the fine heightmap (`T − Γ·z`, Γ = 6.5 °C/km default, or 4.5–5.5 for a "moist/plateau" option). Cost is trivially small (256×128×100 steps×30 years ≈ 1e8 cell updates). Advection by the prevailing wind (upwind term `−u·∇T` with the rule-based wind field of section 2) is what makes west coasts maritime and east coasts continental; without it continentality is symmetric about the coast, which is wrong for Earth.
- **Tier 1 alternative (no time stepping):** `T(m) = T_ann(φ) + A(φ, c)·cos(2π(m − m_peak(c))/12) − Γ·z + current_offset`, where c ∈ [0,1] is continentality from an upwind distance-to-sea trace (Frozen Fractal's method), `A = lerp(A_ocean(φ), A_land(φ), c)`, `m_peak = lerp(Aug/Feb, mid-July/mid-Jan, c)`. This is what Undiscovered Worlds and Frozen Fractal effectively do, with A linear in latitude.
- **Ice–albedo feedback** in an offline generator is safest as a *diagnosed* albedo ramp (e.g. α rises from surface value to 0.6 between 0 and −10 °C) iterated to convergence inside the EBM; expose the solar constant / greenhouse offset (the A term) as the "mean temperature" knob and solve for A so the global mean hits the requested 15 °C — otherwise a user-drawn map with much polar land can tip into snowball (Worldbuilding Pasta flags the same risk beyond ~40° of flat land, cited above).
- **[unverified background]** Dry adiabatic lapse 9.8 °C/km, moist adiabatic ~4–7 °C/km depending on temperature, environmental (ISA) 6.5 °C/km; Earth annual-mean sea-level temperature is roughly 27 °C at the equator, about −20 °C at the North Pole and far colder over Antarctica largely because of its elevation; typical annual range (warmest minus coldest month) ~1–3 °C over tropical ocean, ~5–8 °C over mid-latitude ocean, 25–45 °C in mid/high-latitude continental interiors (Siberia > 60 °C). I did not re-fetch a source for these numbers in this session.

## 2. Atmospheric circulation rules (cells, ITCZ migration, pressure centres, monsoons, prevailing winds)

### Takeaway
Every rule-based generator reduces the circulation to latitude bands whose boundaries shift with the season
(ITCZ following the thermal equator, subtropical highs at ~25° in winter and ~35° in summer), then bends the
bands with land/sea thermal contrasts (winter highs over cold continents, summer lows over hot ones). That is
enough to get monsoons and Mediterranean summers; none of the generators I read simulates pressure or wind
dynamically.

### Cited Findings
- Worldbuilding Pasta's pressure/wind recipe: ocean subtropical highs sit over the cold (eastern) leg of each gyre, centred ~25° latitude in that hemisphere's winter and ~35° in its summer; winter-only highs form over landmasses at least ~5–10 °C colder than ocean at the same latitude, centred on the coldest part and biased toward cold plateaus, slightly poleward of the ocean highs; lows form over continents in summer and over high-latitude oceans in winter. Winds spiral out of highs clockwise in the northern hemisphere, anticlockwise in the southern; tropical easterlies blow toward the ITCZ; fronts between neighbouring anticyclones slant (more equatorward in the west, poleward in the east); polar easterlies may appear in winter. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- ITCZ in the same recipe: it follows the thermal equator — the warmest point at each longitude in that season — smoothed so it does not zigzag through mountains; its zone of influence extends ~15° north and south, wider on western ocean shores, thinner near subtropical highs. Monsoon = onshore wind in summer (wet), offshore in winter (dry) where the ITCZ sweeps across. Polar-front influence reaches from the poles to the poleward edge of the subtropical highs: ~40° in summer, ~30° in winter. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- The ITCZ roughly follows the thermal equator; its seasonal migration is more prominent over land and subtle over oceans; it moves farther from the equator in northern summer than in southern summer because of the north-heavy continents; merged with a monsoon circulation it is called a monsoon trough; double ITCZs occur (a southern one between 3° and 10°S in the east Pacific). — [Wikipedia: Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone)
- Azgaar: six 30° latitude tiers each with a user-editable wind angle; a tier is "westerly" if its angle is in (40°, 140°), "easterly" in (220°, 320°), counted as northerly/southerly otherwise. Winds are therefore straight rows/columns, no deflection by terrain, no seasons. — [Azgaar precipitation-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/model/physical/precipitation-generator.ts)
- Undiscovered Worlds wind map, actual code: band borders (on a 0–180 scale from the north pole) at 28/32 (polar easterlies | westerlies, i.e. ~60° lat), 58/64 ("horse latitudes", ~26–32° lat), 86/94 (equatorial gap, ±4°), mirrored in the south; the border lines are perturbed along longitude with random offsets (nodes every 20 cells, max offset 25 cells) so the belts wobble; wind direction is then a signed east/west value per cell plus an "outside horse latitudes" flag. One wind map, not per season. — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp `createwindmap`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- Undiscovered Worlds monsoons are a separate pass, actual code: seeded from coasts, spread inland tick by tick (max 1500 ticks); require annual mean ≥ 15 °C and a summer–winter difference ≥ 1 °C; strength `= 2.5·(Tsummer − Twinter) + 400·Tmean` scaled by `monstrength = 2 − |32.5 − tilt|/10`; none within 10° of the equator and reduced within 30°; blocked/boosted by slope above 200 m; adds up to 410 units of summer rain and suppresses winter rain by the same strength. — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp `createmonsoons`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- Frozen Fractal: prevailing wind bearing and speed are curves of latitude fitted to 10 years of ERA5 reanalysis, one curve for January and its mirror for July; the Hadley-cell boundary (ITCZ) sits north of the equator in July and south in January, and "monsoon-like" seasonal wind reversal appears wherever that boundary sweeps across a region. No land/sea pressure effect. — [Frozen Fractal part 8](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/19/around-the-world-8-seasons), [analysis notebook](https://github.com/ttencate/global_climate/blob/main/global_climate.ipynb)
- Tectonics.js derives surface pressure from surface temperature and latitude (`guess_surface_air_pressures`) and wind from the pressure field plus rotation (`guess_surface_air_velocities(pos, pressure, angular_speed)`) — i.e. a geostrophic-style diagnosis from a guessed pressure field, the only generator I read that goes pressure → wind. — [Tectonics.js Atmosphere.js](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/noncompiled/models/atmosphere/Atmosphere.js)

### Inferences
- **A pressure-field wind model is the right tier for this project** (per month m):
  1. Zonal base pressure from latitude relative to the *seasonal* ITCZ latitude φ_I(x, m): low at φ_I, high at φ_I ± ~30° (shifted: 25° winter hemisphere, 35° summer hemisphere per Pasta), low at ~60°, high at the poles — e.g. a sum of cosines in `(φ − φ_I)`.
  2. φ_I(x, m) = latitude of the maximum of the month's (smoothed) sea-level temperature along each column, clamped (suggest ±5° over ocean, up to ±20–25° over large land) and low-pass filtered along x.
  3. Thermal anomaly: `p' = −k·(T_sea-level(x,m) − zonal mean T(φ,m))`, smoothed over ~10–15° — gives winter continental highs and summer continental lows (monsoon lows) automatically.
  4. Wind = geostrophic-with-friction: rotate `−∇p` by an angle that depends on hemisphere and latitude (≈ 90° geostrophic far from the equator, turning toward straight down-gradient flow as |φ| → 0 where Coriolis vanishes; a common game trick is rotation angle = sign(φ)·(90° − friction 20–30°)·min(1, |φ|/15°)).
  5. Deflect/block by terrain: subtract the component of wind into slopes higher than a threshold or solve a cheap 2-D potential-flow correction.
  This is my synthesis of the Pasta hand-rules and the Tectonics.js approach, not something I found implemented in one place.
- With a flat, compressed map, Coriolis is a rule (`sign(lat)` and a ramp near the equator), not a physical quantity; nothing in this section depends on real distances except the smoothing radii, which should be expressed in latitude degrees.
- **[unverified background]** Hadley cells ~0–30°, Ferrel ~30–60°, polar 60–90°; trade winds NE/SE, westerlies, polar easterlies; ITCZ seasonal range roughly 5°S–10°N over the oceans and up to ~25–30°N over South Asia in July, ~15–20°S over Africa/Australia/South America in January; named semi-permanent centres (Azores/Bermuda, Pacific, Siberian and Canadian highs; Icelandic and Aleutian lows; South Asian summer low).

## 3. Ocean currents (gyres, boundary currents, upwelling, coastal effects)

### Takeaway
No generator I read simulates ocean currents; the usable algorithm is Worldbuilding Pasta's hand-rule set,
which can be mechanised as "classify each coastal ocean cell as warm/cold/neutral from latitude band and
which side of the basin it is on, then apply a sea-surface-temperature offset and let the wind carry it
onshore".

### Cited Findings
- Gyre construction rules: an eastward equatorial counter-current near the equator; where it meets a coast it splits into two westward equatorial currents 5–10° north and south; on reaching the western side of the basin these turn poleward and hug the coast through the Hadley latitudes (warm), separate from the coast at ~40–50° and cross the ocean eastward; on the eastern side they split, the equatorward branch running down the coast as a cold current; water that reaches ~80° curves west under the polar easterlies and returns equatorward (blocked by sea ice or land). Gyres turn clockwise in the northern hemisphere, anticlockwise in the southern. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- A current only matters thermally if it has travelled ≥ ~30° of latitude; the effect persists until it is ~10° of latitude away from where it left its source band. Magnitudes: up to about +15 °C on coasts warmed by poleward currents and about −10 °C on coasts chilled by equatorward ones at ~50–70°; small offsets near 20° (slight warming on the warm-current side, cooling on the cold-current side). — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- Upwelling occurs where the wind blows parallel to a coast with the coast on its left in the northern hemisphere (right in the southern). — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- Rainfall coupling in the same recipe: coasts with warm currents and onshore wind are "wet"; cold-current coasts only add wet areas inside the ITCZ zone or along fronts; cold-current hot-desert coasts are cool (~20 °C in summer) and foggy with almost no rain; cold-current Mediterranean coasts have morning fog. Deserts near 20° should reach the west coast. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- Undiscovered Worlds has no currents: sea is a flat +2 °C, and maritime moderation comes from heat picked up by the prevailing wind over sea and deposited over land; its evaporation pickup scales with sea temperature (`tempfactor 80`, floor 0.15). — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- ExoPlaSim also has no ocean currents (50 m slab ocean) and is consequently too cold at high-latitude maritime locations such as northern Europe. — [Worldbuilding Pasta ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/11/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vi.html)

### Inferences
- **Mechanised current rule (suggested):** for every ocean cell compute (a) `west_dist`, `east_dist` = distance along the row to the nearest land to the west/east (basin position), (b) latitude band. SST anomaly `ΔT_sst(x) = w_west(φ)·f(west_dist) − w_east(φ)·f(east_dist)` with `f` decaying over ~10–15° of longitude; sign convention on Earth: in the subtropics (10–40°) the *western* side of a basin (east coast of a continent) is warm and the *eastern* side (west coast of a continent) is cold; poleward of ~45° it flips — the eastern side of the basin (west coast of the continent, e.g. NW Europe) is warm and the western side (e.g. Labrador, Kamchatka) is cold. Then diffuse the anomaly and feed it into the EBM as an ocean heat-flux term (Q-flux) or simply add it to ocean-cell temperature before wind advection carries it onshore.
- **Slightly more physical alternative, still cheap:** a steady wind-driven barotropic stream function (Stommel/Munk): solve `r∇²ψ + β ∂ψ/∂x = curl τ / ρH` on the ocean mask with ψ = 0 on coasts, using wind stress from section 2. It is one sparse linear solve (or a few thousand SOR sweeps on a 256×128 grid) and produces gyres with western intensification on arbitrary coastlines without hand rules; SST is then advected by `u = ẑ×∇ψ` with relaxation to the local radiative equilibrium. I know this from oceanography textbooks, not from a source fetched here — **[unverified background]**; on a "compressed" planet β must be defined per latitude degree, not per metre.
- Upwelling index per coastal cell: `max(0, wind · alongshore_tangent_with_coast_on_left(NH))` → extra −2…−6 °C SST and a precipitation-suppression flag (stable marine layer) → fog deserts (Atacama/Namib analogues) emerge when this coincides with the subtropical high.
- **[unverified background]** Western boundary currents (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio, Brazil, Agulhas, East Australian) are narrow, fast and warm; eastern boundary currents (California, Canary, Humboldt, Benguela) broad, slow, cold with coastal upwelling; the Antarctic Circumpolar Current exists because a latitude band (~55–60°S) has no land — a generator should detect land-free latitude bands and close a circumpolar current there, which thermally isolates the pole.

### Gaps
- I found no open-source generator or paper implementing rule-based or stream-function ocean currents for procedural worlds; the mechanised algorithms above are my proposal.
- No verified numbers for the SST anomaly of specific currents (the +15 / −10 °C figures are Worldbuilding Pasta's air-temperature rules of thumb).

## 4. Precipitation (moisture advection, orographic, convective, frontal, seasonal regimes)

### Takeaway
All working generators use the same core: march moisture along the prevailing wind, gain over sea as a
function of temperature, lose a fraction per step over land, lose extra on upslope. Seasonal regimes
(Mediterranean dry summer, monsoon wet summer, savanna) then fall out of running that march once per
season with shifted belts — though Undiscovered Worlds needed explicit "encourage Mediterranean /
rainforest / savanna" correction passes to get Earth-like results. Smith & Barstad's linear model is an
FFT-based drop-in for the orographic part.

### Cited Findings
- Azgaar, actual code: each wind row starts with humidity `maxPrec = 120·modifier·latMod` (60 for meridional winds), where `latMod` comes from a per-5° table `[4,2,2,2,1,1,2,2,2,2,3,3,2,2,1,1,1,0.5]` (wet 0–5°, 5–20° wet-summer, 20–30° dry, 30–50° wet-winter, 50–60° wet, 60–70°, dry poleward of 70°). Over water: `humidity += 5·modifier·step` (capped at maxPrec). First land cell gets `humidity/rand(10,20)` (coastal rain). On land: `precip = clamp(max(humidity/(10·modifier), 1)·step + max(Δh,0)·(h_next/70)², step, humidity)`; if `precip > 1.5·step` one step-unit re-evaporates; cells with temperature < −5 °C are skipped ("no flux in permafrost"); if the next cell's height > 85 (of 100) all remaining humidity is dumped and the wind is dry beyond. Stored as `Uint8`. — [Azgaar precipitation-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/model/physical/precipitation-generator.ts)
- WorldEngine: precipitation is pure simplex noise (6 octaves) multiplied by a temperature gamma curve `(t^γ·(1−bonus)) + bonus` so cold areas are drier; no wind, no orography. — [WorldEngine precipitation.py](https://github.com/Mindwerks/worldengine/blob/master/worldengine/simulations/precipitation.py)
- Undiscovered Worlds prevailing-wind rain, actual code: rain seeds start at coastal tiles and are fed by the `seamult = 80`·wind-strength sea tiles upwind; pickup `= waterpickup·350` scaled down for cold sea; deposition divisor `dumprate = 100`; land re-evaporation `landpickuprate = 40`; slope effect above 300 m with `slopefactor = 160 − 20·lapse`; lateral spreading with 0.85 retention (0.95 into horse latitudes); seasonal rain variation `tilt/3750` (tropics `tilt/2250`) scaled by distance inland up to 50 cells; winter rain ×0.05 behind seasonal sea ice; tropical rain ×0.4 correction. Separate passes then add ocean rain, monsoons, mountain precipitation, smoothing and caps. — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp `createprevailinglandrain`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- Undiscovered Worlds seasonal-regime corrections, actual code (`adjustseasonalrainfall`): a "Mediterranean" mask (strength `1.8 − |31.5 − tilt|/10`, peaking where summer max ≈ 19 °C, winter min ≥ 2 °C, rain ≤ 500, ≤ 40 cells inland, just poleward of the horse latitudes) shifts rain from summer to winter; near the equator rain is boosted in both seasons (+0.3/+0.4 × strength) to "encourage rainforest"; in an 8°-wide strip around ±12° the winter-hemisphere rain is cut (×−1.2 strength) to "encourage savannah". — [Undiscovered Worlds globalclimate.cpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- Frozen Fractal: evaporation only over water with a hand-tuned temperature curve; moisture advected with Jos Stam's semi-Lagrangian backward step (bilinear sample upstream), wind jittered by simplex noise (more jitter when warm); each step a temperature-dependent fraction rains out (~30% at −10 °C); no explicit slope term — rain shadows emerge only because altitude lowers temperature. Converged in 25–50 steps (≥ 95–97% of moisture rained out), ~1 s for the whole globe after optimisation; the seasonal version runs it twice and uses 10 full-resolution steps then half resolution. Known artefact: a spurious rain band near 50°. — [Frozen Fractal part 7](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/15/around-the-world-7-temperature-and-rain/), [part 8](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/19/around-the-world-8-seasons)
- Smith & Barstad linear orographic model (verified from the authors' extended abstract): two steady advection equations for vertically integrated cloud water and hydrometeors with source `S = C_w·U·∇h` (the classical upslope model) give the Fourier-space transfer function `P̂(k,l) = C_w·i·σ·ĥ(k,l) / ([1 − i·m·H_w]·[1 + i·σ·τ_c]·[1 + i·σ·τ_f])`, with intrinsic frequency `σ = U·k + V·l`, vertical wavenumber `m = [((N_m² − σ²)/σ²)·(k² + l²)]^½`, uplift sensitivity `C_w = ρ_v0·Γ_m/γ`; precipitation is `max(P + background, 0)`; with `m = τ_c = τ_f = 0` it reduces to the upslope model. Example parameters: T0 = 280 K, γ = −5.8 °C/km, Γ_m = −6.5 °C/km, N_m = 0.005 s⁻¹, ρ_v,ref = 7.4 g/m³, H_w = 2500 m, τ_c = τ_f = 1000 s, U = 15 m/s. — [Smith & Barstad, AMS 10th Mesoscale conf. extended abstract](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/62756.pdf); full paper: Smith & Barstad 2004, J. Atmos. Sci. 61, 1377–1391 ([catalogue record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_236444084))
- A maintained Python implementation exists (`orographic_precipitation`), with example defaults: uniform background `precip_base = 7`, wind 10 m/s from 270°, `conv_time = fall_time = 1000` s, `nm = 0.005`, `hw = 5000` m, `cw = ref_density·lapse_rate_m/lapse_rate = 7.4e-3·(−6.5/−5.8) ≈ 8.3e-3`. Barstad & Smith 2005 give typical ranges τ = 200–2000 s, H_w = 1–5 km. — [PyPI: orographic-precipitation](https://pypi.org/project/orographic-precipitation/), [Barstad & Smith 2005 (archive copy)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916123113/http://www.nssl.noaa.gov/projects/ipex/pubs/barstadandsmith2005.pdf)
- Worldbuilding Pasta's seasonal rain rules: onshore wind from a warm-current coast is wet and stays wet downwind until a front/ITCZ, relief ≥ ~1000 m (rain shadow; gradual slopes can be followed to ~4000 m) or ~2000 km from the coast; inside the ITCZ zone (±15°) wet becomes very wet and even cold-current coasts get rain; orographic rain within a couple of hundred km upwind of relief ≥ 1000 m if the air has travelled ≤ ~3000 km from sea; polar-front zone (poleward of ~40° summer / ~30° winter) wets land ~2000 km downwind, 1000 km upwind and 1500 km across-wind from any coast; desert belt ~15–30°; onshore east coasts wet 1000–1500 km inland; deserts 2000–3000 km inland from onshore coasts or ~1000 km from offshore coasts; rainfall peaks at 1–1.5 km elevation, rain-shadow deserts need ~2 km relief (> 3 km near the equator, 1 km far inland at high latitude), anything above 4 km is dry. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- Dwarf Fortress: rainfall is a fractal field later biased by rain shadow and orographic precipitation once elevations are final (an `OROGRAPHIC_PRECIPITATION` toggle exists); temperature is biased by latitude and elevation along the Y axis; pole configuration sets which end is cold and the alternating wind bands. — search-result summaries of the [DF wiki "World" page](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:World) and [DF2014 Advanced world generation](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Advanced_world_generation) (pages themselves returned HTTP 429 — not read directly)

### Inferences
- **Recommended monthly precipitation model:** a steady-state column-moisture advection per month on the coarse grid: `u·∇W = E − P`, `E = e0·sat(T_sst)·(1 − ice)` over sea (plus a small land recycling term proportional to soil moisture), `P = W/τ(φ, m) + P_oro + P_conv`. Solve by semi-Lagrangian backward steps (Stam) for ~50 iterations to steady state — per Frozen Fractal about 1 s per field on a full globe, so 12 months is seconds. Use Clausius–Clapeyron for `sat(T)` (≈ +7%/°C) instead of hand-tuned curves. Components:
  - *Convergence/convective (ITCZ)*: `P_conv ∝ W·max(0, −∇·u)` plus a Gaussian in `(φ − φ_ITCZ(x,m))` of half-width ~7–10°.
  - *Subsidence suppression*: multiply rain-out rate `1/τ` by a factor < 1 under the subtropical-high pressure anomaly from section 2 (this is what makes the desert belt and the Mediterranean dry summer when the high shifts to 35° in summer).
  - *Frontal*: raise `1/τ` in the westerlies belt (40–65°), more in the winter hemisphere.
  - *Orographic*: either the upslope term `P_oro = C_w'·W·max(0, u·∇h)` evaluated on the **fine** heightmap (cheap, local, gives sharp rain shadows because W is depleted), or Smith–Barstad by FFT per wind sector on the fine grid: one 2048² FFT pair is ~0.2–0.5 s, so 12 months × a couple of wind sectors is still well under a minute. Smith–Barstad assumes a uniform wind per FFT domain, so it must be tiled/blended over windows of roughly constant wind; and it works in real metres and seconds, which a "compressed planet" does not have — choose a nominal km-per-cell for the orographic step only (τ·U ≈ 10–15 km is the smearing length it adds; if a cell is larger than that, the upslope model is equivalent and simpler).
- Azgaar's latitude table and Undiscovered Worlds' Mediterranean/rainforest/savanna "encouragement" passes are evidence that a pure wind-march does **not** produce Earth's seasonal regimes without a subsidence term tied to the seasonally shifting subtropical high. Build that term in from the start rather than patching.
- Rain-out length scales from Pasta (wet ≤ 1000–2000 km inland, desert beyond 2000–3000 km) translate, at 111 km per degree, to e-folding distances of roughly 10–20° of arc; in a compressed world express τ in degrees so continental interiors dry out at Earth-like angular scales.
- Dwarf Fortress today (per the project brief: one pole, annual rain with rain shadow) is at Azgaar's level; DF itself has no monthly climate, so "DF-like" does not constrain the seasonal model.

### Gaps
- The full Smith & Barstad 2004 paper was not opened (paywall); the transfer function is verified from the authors' own extended abstract only. Units of the PyPI defaults are not stated on that page.
- Dwarf Fortress's actual rain-shadow algorithm is undocumented (closed source; wiki pages rate-limited); Songs of Syx's world climate was not researched; RimWorld's wiki gives only qualitative statements (see section 5).
- No fetched source for observed zonal-mean precipitation by latitude and month.

## 5. Existing implementations and write-ups (what each actually does)

### Takeaway
Only three hobby/game sources produce seasonal fields: Undiscovered Worlds (January/July, heavily hand-tuned,
C++ source available), Frozen Fractal's "Around the World" (January/July from ERA5-fitted latitude curves),
and the ExoPlaSim workflow (a real GCM). Everything else (Azgaar, WorldEngine, Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld,
Tectonics.js) is annual. I found no computer-graphics paper that generates a planetary monthly climate.

### Cited Findings
- **Undiscovered Worlds** (Jonathan Coates, C++; cube-sphere of 6 faces): pipeline order in `generateglobalclimate` is wind map → temperature map (Jan/Jul) → sea ice → tides → rainfall (ocean rain, prevailing land rain, monsoons, seasonal adjustment, smoothing, caps) → temperature adjusted by rain and continentality → rivers/lakes → climate (Köppen) map → coastal temperature correction. A code comment records the whole climate stage at ~8–9 s. Stores `jantemp`, `jultemp`, `janrain`, `julrain` per cell and derives months on demand. — [globalclimate.cpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)
- Undiscovered Worlds monthly reconstruction, actual code: month temperatures interpolate between the January and July values and an "equinox temperature" using the sun's per-month distance/latitude table, **with a one-month lag** (`thismonth − 1`, "because temperature lags behind the sun's position"); monthly rain interpolates January↔July by the sun's latitude for each month and is then blended with a more complex monsoon-shaped curve (faster onset than decay); monthly river flow adds freeze storage (flow ×1.2 per preceding frozen month, released as thaw floods). — [planet.hpp `monthtemp`, `monthlyrain`, `monthlyflow`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/planet.hpp)
- The author describes that maps are drawn from annual averages but the program tracks month-to-month variation, with snow creeping down mountains toward winter; worlds can be given the same parameters as Earth or varied (size, gravity, tilt, eccentricity). — [Undiscovered Worlds blog, Feb 2024](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2024/02), [Nov 2022 "Variations"](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2022/11/variations.html)
- **Frozen Fractal "Around the World"**: computes January and July only, "assumed to cover the extremes in each location", sine interpolation between; runs Köppen on the reconstructed months. Validation on real Earth topography (Earth2014, 4320×2160): coasts roughly right, but Asia's interior comes out as cold desert with continental (D) climates missing entirely — the author could not tell whether it was a bug. — [part 8](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/19/around-the-world-8-seasons), [part 9: Climates](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/29/around-the-world-9-climates/)
- **Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator**: annual temperature (Int8) and precipitation (Uint8) only, as detailed in sections 1 and 4; biomes come from a temperature × moisture matrix (12 land biomes: hot desert, cold desert, savanna, grassland, tropical seasonal forest, temperate deciduous forest, tropical rainforest, …). — [temperature-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/model/physical/temperature-generator.ts), [precipitation-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/model/physical/precipitation-generator.ts), [biomes-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/model/physical/biomes-generator.ts)
- **WorldEngine**: noise-based, annual, unitless (sections 1 and 4); biome thresholds are quantiles of the land distribution (e.g. precipitation low/medium thresholds at the 0.75 / 0.3 quantiles) — i.e. it *forces* fixed area shares rather than simulating them. — [temperature.py](https://github.com/Mindwerks/worldengine/blob/master/worldengine/simulations/temperature.py), [precipitation.py](https://github.com/Mindwerks/worldengine/blob/master/worldengine/simulations/precipitation.py)
- **Tectonics.js**: radiative-equilibrium temperature with entropic heat-flow guess, pressure→wind diagnosis, latitude-only precipitation; has `CelestialCycle`/`Orbit`/`Spin` models for insolation averaging over cycles. — [Atmosphere.js](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/noncompiled/models/atmosphere/Atmosphere.js)
- **RimWorld**: each tile has an average temperature (colder with elevation and distance from the equator) and annual rainfall in mm; seasons are reversed between hemispheres, with "permanent summer" near the equator and "permanent winter" near the poles; no formulas published on the wiki. — [RimWorld wiki: World generation](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/World_generation)
- **Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb** is a complete manual rule set (currents → temperature bands → pressure → winds → precipitation → Köppen) designed for hand-drawing on January and July maps; the temperature step leans on "Clima-Sim" (imports a 720×420 map, at most 1,296 cells; presets 1951–1980 ≈ 15 °C global mean; coastal high-latitude errors of 10 °C or more). — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- **Computer-graphics literature**: searches for procedural planetary climate found only adjacent work — Paris et al. 2019 "Desertscape Simulation" (wind over relief + sand transport, no precipitation), Schott et al. 2023 "Large-scale Terrain Authoring through Interactive Erosion Simulation" (stream-power erosion in the uplift domain, no climate), Yang et al. 2024 (erosion reversal; notes drainage area ignores precipitation variation). — [Desertscapes](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/aparis/public_html/projects/paris2019_Deserts.html), [Schott 2023](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/schott2023_Uplift.html), [Yang 2024](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Yang24CGF.pdf)

### Inferences
- Undiscovered Worlds is the best single code base to mine: ~14,500 lines of climate C++ with every tuning constant commented. But its structure (one wind map, rain marched in straight east/west lines, then five correction passes that key on axial tilt) is a pile of Earth-fitted patches; the constants do not transfer to a different grid or map shape. Take its *list of effects that needed patching* (Mediterranean, rainforest, savanna, rain-warms-winter, subpolar streaks, coastal temperature correction) as the acceptance checklist for a cleaner model.
- Frozen Fractal's failed Asia (no D climates, too much cold desert) is the predictable result of (a) continentality that only blends two latitude curves and (b) a rain model with no land moisture recycling and no summer continental low drawing moisture inland. A design for this project should test exactly that case.
- With `tilt = 23.44` the Undiscovered Worlds formulas give (my arithmetic) tempdiff ≈ −95.3, so pole ≈ avg − 52 °C, equator ≈ avg + 34 °C before sea/altitude/rain corrections, and a polar January–July swing of ≈ 55 °C — these raw values are far from the final fields, confirming the later passes do much of the work.

### Gaps
- Songs of Syx: not researched (no source found in the searches run). "Clima"/"Simon's Climate Simulator": only seen through Worldbuilding Pasta's description of Clima-Sim; its internals were not found. Geoff's Climate Cookbook and Artifexian: see section 2 gaps.
- "Procedural Generation of Planetary-Scale Natural Phenomena" was not located by my searches; I cannot confirm its content.
- Azgaar's default equator/pole temperatures and the biome matrix values were not extracted (only the first ~90 lines of the biome file were read).

## 6. Intermediate-complexity options (ExoPlaSim/PlaSim, MITgcm, SPEEDY, shallow-water / advection–diffusion)

### Takeaway
A real GCM is not affordable inside a "minutes, offline, CPU" generator: ExoPlaSim at its coarsest (T21,
32×64 cells) needs minutes per model year and tens of model years to equilibrate, and still lacks ocean
currents. The sweet spot is a 2-D seasonal energy-balance + moisture advection–diffusion model with
rule-based circulation — a few hundred to ~2,000 lines, seconds to run.

### Cited Findings
- ExoPlaSim speed: T21 (32 lat × 64 lon), 10 levels, 45-minute step: 30–60 s of wall time per model year on HPC nodes; ~3.75 min/year on a consumer machine with 4 MPI threads (5.5 min with a 30-minute step); the official tutorial's 4-core laptop takes just over 6 min/year; T21 aquaplanets need 50–200 model years to reach energy balance. — [ExoPlaSim paper (Paradise et al.), arXiv 2107.07685](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07685), [ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://exoplasim.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial.html)
- Worldbuilding Pasta's ExoPlaSim workflow: T21 default, T42 (64×128) is "the practical minimum for mapping", up to T170 (256×512) supported; a 10-year test run takes "under 10 minutes to over an hour"; `runtobalance` defaults 50-year baseline / 75 min / 300 max years (the author uses ~10); inputs are a land mask and topography (geopotential) from an equirectangular heightmap; outputs monthly means per model year in netCDF (`tas` 2 m air temperature, `pr` precipitation, `ts`, `wnd`, …) fed to a Köppen script. Defaults: obliquity 23.441°, eccentricity 0.016715, year 365.25 d, g = 9.80665, pCO2 = 0.0003 bar. — [Worldbuilding Pasta ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/11/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vi.html)
- Its documented biases: no ocean currents (50 m slab) so high-latitude maritime regions like northern Europe are too cold; ice sheets form poorly (no glacial flow); East Asian monsoon too weak; over-production of dry-summer classes (As, Cs, Ds); small islands/peninsulas too dry at this resolution. — [Worldbuilding Pasta ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/11/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vi.html)
- The 1-D seasonal EBM is tiny: 90 latitude points, 90 steps per year. — [climlab API](https://climlab.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/climlab.model.ebm.html)

### Inferences
- **Ranking from simplest to most physical, with rough cost for a 2048² target (my estimates, not measured):**
  1. *Latitude lookup + noise* (WorldEngine, RimWorld): no seasons. Milliseconds. Not sufficient.
  2. *Latitude bands + wind-march rain, annual* (Azgaar, DF, the project today): O(N). < 1 s.
  3. *Two-season rule model* (Undiscovered Worlds, Frozen Fractal): January/July temperature = latitude curve ± amplitude × continentality; rain marched per season. Seconds. Gets seasons; regimes need hand patches.
  4. *Recommended: 12-month diagnostic model* — insolation table → 2-D seasonal EBM with land/sea heat capacity, diffusion and wind advection (coarse grid) → monthly pressure from thermal + zonal terms → monthly wind → optional stream-function ocean gyres/SST anomaly → monthly moisture advection with convergence, subsidence, frontal and orographic rain → downscale to the fine grid with lapse rate and fine-grid orographic term. All steady-state or periodic solves on ~256×128…512×256, 12 months; I estimate tens of seconds single-threaded plus the fine-grid passes (12 × O(N) with N = 4M cells: a few seconds). ~1,500–3,000 lines of Rust. Deterministic (fixed iteration counts, no threads needed) — fits the project's determinism rules if `libm` is used for transcendentals.
  5. *Shallow-water / one-layer dynamical atmosphere* (time-stepped winds from pressure gradients + Coriolis on the coarse grid): a few hundred extra lines; needs thousands of small time steps (gravity-wave CFL) and careful damping; gives self-consistent monsoon flows and lee effects but is fragile on a flat rectangular "compressed" map where Coriolis and the metric are fictions. Minutes. Marginal gain over 4 for a game.
  6. *Simplified GCM* (SPEEDY-class: 8 layers, T30; PlaSim/ExoPlaSim: 10 layers, T21–T42; MITgcm): tens of thousands of lines of Fortran with spectral transforms and full physics packages; not re-implementable in "a few thousand lines"; ExoPlaSim's cited speed means ≥ 30–60 min for a 10-year T21 run and it needs a sphere, not a flat rectangle. Could only be an optional external tool whose monthly output is imported and downscaled.
- What a GCM buys over tier 4: emergent storm tracks, real monsoon dynamics, self-consistent humidity/cloud/radiation — at 500–1,000 km cells. What it does *not* buy: ocean currents (slab), fine rain shadows, and determinism across platforms. For Köppen-level realism the tier-4 model plus explicit current rules can match or beat a T21 slab-ocean GCM on exactly the features players notice (mild west coasts, fog deserts, rain shadows).
- **[unverified background]** SPEEDY (Molteni 2003; T30, 8 levels) and its speed, MITgcm's capabilities, and PlaSim's code size were not looked up in this session.

### Gaps
- No measured runtime for a tier-4 model; the estimates are back-of-envelope.
- SPEEDY, MITgcm and plain PlaSim were not researched beyond ExoPlaSim's figures.
- No source found describing a shallow-water atmosphere used for worldbuilding climate.

## 7. Compact per-cell representation (12 normals vs Jan/Jul vs harmonics) and Köppen accuracy

### Takeaway
A single annual sinusoid (mean, amplitude, phase) is published as adequate for "the vast majority" of
monthly temperature and precipitation climates, with known failure in bimodal tropical rainfall; that
supports storing temperature as one harmonic and precipitation as one or two harmonics. But 12 monthly
values quantised to a byte each are only 24 bytes per cell and remove all doubt — the choice is about
storage, not accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Berghuijs & Woods 2016 (Int. J. Climatol. 36(9), 3161–3174): "a sinusoidal function with an annual period can adequately describe the vast majority" of monthly precipitation and temperature climates worldwide, using five indices — mean precipitation rate, mean temperature, seasonal precipitation amplitude, seasonal temperature amplitude, and the phase difference between the precipitation and temperature regimes. — [VU research portal record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-simple-framework-to-quantitatively-describe-monthly-precipitati-3/)
- A 2017 EGU abstract fits 12-month and 6-month cycles to CRU TS data and reports that a simple sine summarises climate variables for many locations but is expected to be inadequate in parts of the tropics with bimodal (two-peak) regimes. — [EGU2017-7968](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2017/EGU2017-7968.pdf?pdf) (seen through search summary, PDF not opened)
- January + July with sine interpolation is what Frozen Fractal uses and it suffices to evaluate month-count rules such as "at least four months above 10 °C". — [Frozen Fractal part 9](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/29/around-the-world-9-climates/)
- Undiscovered Worlds stores January/July and reconstructs months with a one-month lag for temperature and a non-sinusoidal, monsoon-aware blend for rain (fast onset, slow decay). — [planet.hpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/planet.hpp)
- Köppen–Geiger criteria as used by Peel et al. 2007 (temperatures °C, precipitation mm): **A** Tcold ≥ 18 (Af: Pdry ≥ 60; Am: not Af and Pdry ≥ 100 − MAP/25; Aw: Pdry < 100 − MAP/25). **B** MAP < 10·Pth, where Pth = 2·MAT if ≥ 70% of MAP falls in winter, 2·MAT + 28 if ≥ 70% falls in summer, else 2·MAT + 14 (BW: MAP < 5·Pth; BS otherwise; h: MAT ≥ 18; k: MAT < 18). **C** Thot > 10 and 0 < Tcold < 18. **D** Thot > 10 and Tcold ≤ 0. For C and D — s: Psdry < 40 and Psdry < Pwwet/3; w: Pwdry < Pswet/10; f: neither; a: Thot ≥ 22; b: not a and ≥ 4 months above 10; c: not a/b and 1–3 months above 10; d: not a/b and Tcold < −38. **E** Thot < 10 (ET: Thot > 0; EF: Thot ≤ 0). Summer/winter are the warmer/cooler six-month halves (ONDJFM vs AMJJAS); B is tested first; where both s and w apply Peel adds a rule based on which half-year is wetter. Peel uses 0 °C rather than −3 °C for the C/D boundary. — [Peel, Finlayson & McMahon 2007, HESS 11, 1633–1644](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/11/1633/2007/hess-11-1633-2007.pdf)
- Worldbuilding Pasta gives the same thresholds in mm/day form and notes variants (−3 °C C/D boundary; `h/k` by coldest month above/below 0 °C instead of MAT 18 °C). — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)

### Inferences
- What Köppen needs from the representation: Thot, Tcold, MAT, count of months > 10 °C, MAP, Pdry, summer/winter precipitation totals and each half's wettest/driest month. Sensitivity by scheme:
  - *Temperature, one harmonic (mean, amplitude, phase = 3 values)*: Thot/Tcold/MAT exact by construction if fitted to them; the months-above-10 count is right to ±1 except where the cycle is strongly asymmetric. Adequate. A second (semi-annual) harmonic only matters near the equator (two solar maxima) where amplitudes are ≤ 1–2 °C and no Köppen threshold depends on it.
  - *Precipitation, one harmonic*: cannot represent (a) double rainy seasons near the equator — the semi-annual harmonic dominates there, and Af vs Am vs Aw hinges on Pdry; (b) short sharp monsoons (clipped sinusoid goes negative → must clamp, which distorts MAP); (c) the `s` test "Psdry < 40 and < Pwwet/3" when the dry season is short. Use at least annual + semi-annual harmonics (5 values), clamp at zero and renormalise to MAP — or just store 12 values.
  - *January/July + sine*: equivalent to a one-harmonic fit with phase pinned to the solstice + fixed lag; additionally wrong wherever extremes are not in January/July (oceanic lag puts them in February/August; monsoon regions peak before the rains, in May). It is the cause-neutral explanation for why both Jan/Jul generators need special monsoon handling.
- **Recommendation:** store 12 monthly means: temperature as i8 in 0.5 °C steps offset per cell (or i16 ×0.1), precipitation as u8 on a square-root or log scale (0–1,500 mm/month). 24 bytes/cell → 100 MB at 2048², ~25 MB at 1024², before compression; spatially smooth so it compresses well, and it can be stored at half resolution with the lapse/orographic downscaling re-applied on read. If storage is tight: T as (mean, amplitude, phase) + P as mean + 2 harmonics = 8 values. For runtime in-game interpolation between months, a periodic cubic or the harmonic form avoids kinks at month boundaries.

### Gaps
- I found no study that quantifies Köppen misclassification rates for harmonic or two-month reconstructions; the sensitivity analysis above is my reasoning. This is cheap to measure offline against WorldClim/CRU monthly normals if the team wants a number.
- The explicit functional form in Berghuijs & Woods was not visible on the fetched page.

## 8. Derived fields (snow, sea ice, freeze, GDD, PET, aridity, soil moisture)

### Takeaway
Once monthly T and P exist, every derived field is a one-pass per-cell computation over 12 months;
Thornthwaite needs only monthly temperature and day length, so it is the natural default PET for a generator.

### Cited Findings
- Thornthwaite (1948) monthly PET: `PET = 16·(L/12)·(N/30)·(10·T/I)^α` mm/month, T = monthly mean °C (0 if negative), L = mean day length in hours, N = days in month, heat index `I = Σ_12 (T_i/5)^1.514` over months with T_i > 0, `α = 6.75e-7·I³ − 7.71e-5·I² + 1.792e-2·I + 0.49239`. — [Wikipedia: Potential evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_evapotranspiration)
- FAO-56 Penman–Monteith: `ET0 = [0.408·Δ·(Rn − G) + γ·(900/T)·u2·δe] / [Δ + γ·(1 + 0.34·u2)]` (needs radiation, wind, humidity); Priestley–Taylor drops the aerodynamic term and scales the radiation term by α = 1.26. — [Wikipedia: Potential evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_evapotranspiration)
- Sea ice / snow thresholds used by world-builders: permanent sea ice where the warmest month's sea temperature ≤ −2 °C, seasonal sea ice where the coldest month < −2 °C; ice cap where the warmest month < 0 °C; tundra where it is 0–10 °C; coral reefs where the coldest month ≥ 18 °C in shallow water. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)
- Undiscovered Worlds couples sea ice back into climate (winter rain ×0.05 downwind of seasonal sea ice) and models freeze storage in rivers (flow multiplied by 1.2 per preceding frozen month, released at thaw); its sea-ice pass limits adjustment to 12 °C and weighs nearby land (`landfactor 20`) but is noted in the source as "effectively turned off for now". — [globalclimate.cpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp), [planet.hpp `monthlyflow`](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/planet.hpp)
- ExoPlaSim's glacier option uses a minimum snow depth of 2 m to mark glaciation and has no glacial flow, so ice sheets come out too small. — [Worldbuilding Pasta ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/11/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vi.html)

### Inferences
- Day length for Thornthwaite comes free from the insolation step: `L = 24·h0/π` hours with the same h0.
- Suggested per-cell monthly bookkeeping (all **my proposal**, standard hydrology practice, formulas **[unverified background]** unless cited above):
  - *Snowfall fraction*: 1 below −1 °C, 0 above +3 °C, linear between. *Melt*: degree-day, `melt = k·max(T, 0)·days`, k ≈ 3–5 mm/°C/day. Iterate the 12-month cycle 2–3 times to a periodic state → snow water equivalent per month → monthly snow-cover flag (SWE > ~10 mm) and *permanent snow/glacier* where SWE never returns to zero (cap accumulation). Snow line per month falls out per cell from the lapse-corrected fine temperature.
  - *Sea ice*: ice where monthly SST < −1.8 °C; better, a slab: ice thickness grows when the mixed layer would cool below freezing, with concentration fed back into the EBM albedo and heat capacity. *Lakes/rivers*: frozen in months with mean air T < 0 °C (large lakes with a one-month lag).
  - *Growing degree days*: `GDD_b = Σ_months days·max(T_m − b, 0)` with b = 5 °C (or 0, 10); adding ±½ diurnal range refinement is optional.
  - *Hargreaves PET* (if a diurnal range field exists): `ET0 = 0.0023·Ra·(Tmean + 17.8)·√(Tmax − Tmin)` with Ra extraterrestrial radiation in mm/day equivalent. *Priestley–Taylor* if net radiation is kept from the EBM: `PET = 1.26·Δ/(Δ+γ)·Rn/λ`.
  - *Aridity index* `AI = MAP/PET_annual` (UNEP classes: < 0.05 hyper-arid, 0.05–0.2 arid, 0.2–0.5 semi-arid, 0.5–0.65 dry sub-humid).
  - *Soil-moisture bucket* (Thornthwaite–Mather / Manabe): `S ← clamp(S + P_rain + melt − AET, 0, S_max)`, `AET = PET·min(1, S/(0.75·S_max))`, S_max ≈ 150 mm (vary with soil), excess → runoff per month (gives seasonal river discharge). Iterate the year to periodicity.
- These fields are also the right inputs for biome/vegetation classification beyond Köppen (e.g. a Holdridge- or Whittaker-style scheme on GDD, coldest-month T and AI), and for gameplay (growing season length, frozen rivers).

- No source consulted on diurnal temperature range modelling.

### Inferences

## 10. Validation (does it look like Earth?)

### Takeaway
The quickest objective check is the Köppen major-class area shares on land — Earth: B 30.2%, D 24.6%,
A 19.0%, C 13.4%, E 12.8% — plus spot checks of known regime positions and a regression test of the model
run on Earth's real topography.

### Cited Findings
- Global land-area shares (Peel et al. 2007): arid B 30.2%, cold D 24.6%, tropical A 19.0%, temperate C 13.4%, polar E 12.8%. Most common single types: BWh 14.2%, Aw 11.5%. Csc never occurs and Cwc is 0.002% of land. — [Peel et al. 2007](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/11/1633/2007/hess-11-1633-2007.pdf)
- By continent (Peel et al. 2007): Africa B 57.2 / A 31.0 / C 11.8; Asia D 43.8 / B 23.9 / A 16.3 / C 12.3 / E 3.8; North America D 54.5 / B 15.3 / C 13.4 / E 11.0 / A 5.9; South America A 60.1 / C 24.1 / B 15.0 / E 0.8; Europe D 44.4 / B 36.3 / C 17.0 / E 2.3; Australia B 77.8 / C 13.9 / A 8.3. Greenland was classed ET below 1000 m and EF above; all of Antarctica EF. — [Peel et al. 2007](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/11/1633/2007/hess-11-1633-2007.pdf)
- Peel notes the second-letter (s/w/f) criteria for C and D still misbehave at high latitudes and suggests seasonal-total thresholds would be more robust — a caution against over-fitting a generator to s/w/f boundaries. — [Peel et al. 2007](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/11/1633/2007/hess-11-1633-2007.pdf)
- Beck et al. provide 1-km Köppen maps (2018: present 1980–2016; 2023: 1901–2099 in 30-year windows, ~5% of land changed major class between 1901–1930 and 1991–2020), downloadable from gloh2o.org/koppen for computing reference statistics. — [Beck et al. 2018, Sci. Data](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2018.214), [Beck et al. 2023 record](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64819)
- A known-failure benchmark: Frozen Fractal's model run on real Earth topography got coasts roughly right but replaced Asia's continental (D) climates with cold desert. ExoPlaSim's known failures: too-cold NW Europe, weak East Asian monsoon, too much Cs/Ds/As. — [Frozen Fractal part 9](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/29/around-the-world-9-climates/), [Worldbuilding Pasta ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/11/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vi.html)
- Expected placement rules to assert on generated worlds (from Pasta's synthesis): deserts ~15–30° reaching the west coast near 20°; Mediterranean climates rarely extend far inland and sit on west coasts poleward of the deserts; Ds near Mediterranean zones and Dw near strong monsoon regions; steppe fringes ~100–300 km wide in the tropics and about double at higher latitudes; Af near the equator grading to Am/Aw toward the tropics' edges. — [Worldbuilding Pasta part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)

### Inferences
- **Validation suite proposal:**
  1. *Earth regression*: feed Earth's real heightmap (downsampled ETOPO, resampled to the game's flat lat-linear rectangle) through the generator; compare the Köppen map against Beck's raster (confusion matrix at major-class level; target ≥ 70% agreement by area) and the five area shares against Peel within ±5 percentage points. Note Peel's shares include Antarctica (≈ 9% of land, all EF); excluding it gives roughly B 33, D 27, A 21, C 15, E 4 (my arithmetic from Peel's figures assuming Antarctica ≈ 9.2% of land).
  2. *Named-regime checks on Earth input*: Cs on the west coast at 30–40° (California, Chile, Mediterranean, SW Australia, Cape); BWh/BWk on west coasts at 15–30° with cool summers (Namib, Atacama); Cfb on the west coast at 45–60° (NW Europe, BC, southern Chile); Dfb/Dfc/Dwc across the interior and east at 45–65°N; Am/Aw/Cwa under the South/East Asian monsoon; Af on the equator.
  3. *Zonal profiles*: zonal-mean annual temperature (should fall from ~27 °C at the equator to well below zero at the poles, flat within the tropics) and zonal-mean precipitation (peak near the ITCZ slightly north of the equator on Earth, minima at ~20–30°, secondary maxima at ~40–55°, very low at the poles); seasonal amplitude vs. latitude and vs. continentality. **[unverified background]** for the specific Earth numbers.
  4. *Synthetic sanity worlds*: all-ocean (zonal bands only, no monsoon), one rectangular continent pole-to-pole (classic "idealised continent" textbook pattern: east/west asymmetry, deserts on the west at 20–30°, Cs poleward of them, Cfa on the east), a single N–S mountain wall (rain shadow flips side between trade-wind and westerly latitudes).
  5. On generated (non-Earth) worlds only the area shares and placement rules apply; shares legitimately vary with land distribution, so gate on ranges (e.g. each major class 5–45% of ice-free land for default settings) rather than Earth's exact numbers.
- Since the game map is not equal-area, weight every area statistic by cos(latitude) if the intent is "Earth-like as a planet", or leave unweighted if the intent is "Earth-like share of playable map" — pick one and document it.
