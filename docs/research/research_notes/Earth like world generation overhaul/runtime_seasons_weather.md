# Runtime seasons and weather for a deterministic, multi-region simulation driven by a pre-generated climate

Reading guide. "Cited Findings" are facts read from the linked source (wiki text, source code, paper) during
this research. Where a fact came only from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened, it is
marked *(search summary, page not opened)*. Everything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning or design
proposal, not something a source states. Sources that could not be reached are listed under "Gaps" so the
writer does not mistake silence for a negative result.

Project facts used below (read from the checkout, not from the web): the world calendar already exists in
`crates/game/src/history/calendar.rs` — `DAY_MS = 10_000` (one game day = 10 s of play), `TICKS_PER_DAY =
DAY_MS / TICK_RATE` = 200 ticks at the 50 ms tick, `DAYS_PER_MONTH = 28`, `MONTHS_PER_YEAR = 12`, so 336 days
and 67,200 ticks (56 real minutes) per year, with Dwarf Fortress month names. Stateless randomness is
`roll(at: CalendarTick, subject, salt)` in `crates/game/src/history/rng.rs` (two splitmix64 finaliser rounds).
The forge's climate (`crates/worldgen/src/forge/climate.rs`) currently produces annual temperature from
latitude and height, precipitation in mm/yr, and wind bands (trades / westerlies / polar easterlies) by
latitude row.

## 1. How notable games model calendar, seasons, temperature and weather

### Takeaway
Two families exist. Most colony and survival games (Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld, Don't Starve, Timberborn,
Serene Seasons) use a calendar plus per-biome tables and an occasional random event; the weather itself is a
small state machine with stored state. Only Vintage Story and TerraFirmaCraft compute climate as a function of
position and date — and Vintage Story's precipitation is already the design this project needs: a pure
3D-noise function of (x, z, time) offset by the worldgen rainfall value, with no stored state.

### Cited Findings

**Dwarf Fortress**
- Calendar: 12 months of exactly 28 days = 336 days, 4 seasons of 3 months; the year starts on the 1st of Granite, the first day of spring. Granite corresponds to March in the northern hemisphere table and September in the southern one. — [DF wiki: Calendar](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Calendar)
- Fortress mode: 1 tick = 72 s of game time; 1 day = 1,200 ticks, 1 month = 33,600, 1 season = 100,800, 1 year = 403,200 ticks. Adventure mode: 1 tick = 0.5 s, 1 day = 172,800 ticks, 1 year = 58,060,800 ticks — the same calendar runs 144× slower per tick. The wiki calls the difference a design choice so fortress players can watch a fort over years. — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- At the default 100 FPS cap a fortress year takes 1 h 7 min 12 s of unpaused play (1 day = 12 s); at 30 FPS, 3 h 44 min. — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- Update cadence: "Every ten ticks it advances the seasons, which actually does all kinds of things with the weather and map (both locally and in the world)"; temperature is updated every tick with optimisation flags and is described as "still an unfortunately slow process". — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- Biome-dependent season names: the announced season can differ from the calendar season; "in some biomes 'wet' and 'dry' seasons replace spring or summer", a few biomes have no weather change at all, and plants and caravans always follow the calendar season regardless. — [DF wiki: Calendar](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Calendar)
- Plant growth is tick-ranged against the calendar: `GROWDUR` units are 100 ticks (1,008 per season; crops usually 300 or 500), and growths such as flowers carry start/end ticks within the year (a flower blooming on the first day of summer starts at tick 100,800). — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- Full moons fall on fixed calendar dates each year (25th Granite, 23rd Slate, 21st Felsite, … stepping back about two days a month); werebeasts transform on them. — [DF wiki: Calendar](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Calendar)
- Temperature is a 16-bit unsigned integer in "degrees Urist": °U = °F + 9968 = °C × 9/5 + 10000. Water freezes at 10000 °U, boils at 10180 °U; underground is a constant 10015 °U (8.3 °C); lowest survivable 9900 °U (−55.6 °C), highest survivable 10078 °U (43.3 °C); magma 12000 °U. No fractions are used in calculations. — [DF wiki: Temperature](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Temperature)
- Climate is a six-step rating by peak summer temperature (Scorching ≥ 50 °C, Hot 41–49, Warm 28–41, Temperate 8–27, Cold 1–8, Freezing ≤ 0 °C) that decides surface water behaviour: scorching dries murky pools at once; hot dry biomes lose pools in the dry season; temperate pools and rivers freeze for part of the year (from about a month to most of winter); cold ones thaw only a few months; freezing ones never thaw. — [DF wiki: Climate](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Climate)
- Weather is rain or snow by temperature; rain refills murky pools by 1/7 per hit and washes contaminants; a dammed dry riverbed refills when it rains. Snowfall gradually covers ground and trees. Clouds (cumulus / cirrus / stratus layers) and three fog levels exist only in adventure mode and have no fortress-mode effect. — [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather), [DF wiki: River](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/River)
- Freezing is a threshold switch, not a thickness model: outdoor water becomes an ice wall if at least 4/7 deep, else an ice floor; walls always thaw to 7/7 water, anything inside freezing water dies instantly, a thawing pool drops whoever stands on it, and only above-ground water freezes. Oceans in a freezing climate freeze too. — [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather), [DF wiki: Ice](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Ice), [DF wiki: Ocean](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Ocean)
- Wind: three values per embark-tile biome (east–west, north–south, strength = sum of absolute values), uniform over the embark, with the east–west base set by latitude after atmospheric circulation cells (polar −15…−11, Ferrel +11…+25, by rows counted from the pole). — [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather)
- Evil weather is per-biome generated content: at most one effect per evil biome, either evil rain (blood, or a generated substance with mild syndromes) or evil clouds (a generated gas/dust that spreads from one tile like miasma and can carry severe syndromes such as thralling). It ignores the "weather off" setting. — [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather)

**RimWorld**
- 2,500 ticks per hour, 60,000 per day, 3,600,000 per year; 15 days per quadrum, 4 quadrums = 60 days. One day is about 16 min 40 s at normal speed, one year about 16 h 40 min. — [RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time)
- Quadrums (Aprimay, Jugust, Septober, Decembary) map to spring/summer/fall/winter in the north and the opposite in the south; near the equator it is temperate all year. Version 0.13 doubled day length and halved days per year (same real-time year); 0.17 renamed seasons to quadrums because season names could not be applied to the whole globe. — [RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time)
- Outdoor temperature "varies by latitude, time of day, day of the year, and biome", shifted by events (cold snap, heat wave, volcanic winter); the whole outdoors of a map is one uniform-temperature room. — [RimWorld wiki: Temperature](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Temperature)
- Plant thresholds: growth stops at 0 °C and at 58 °C, slows below 6 °C and above 42 °C, most plants die at −10 °C. Each biome has a growing period; outside it crops die and colonists do not sow. — [RimWorld wiki: Temperature](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Temperature)
- Weather is a weighted pick per biome with temperature gates: rain types 0…100 °C, snow types below −0.5 °C, sandstorm ≥ 10 °C; a running weather may continue after temperature leaves its range. Example weights: tropical rainforest clear 49 % / rain 5.5 % / rainy thunderstorm 4.7 %; ice sheet clear 15 % / soft snow 25 % / hard snow 51 %. Fire danger above a threshold forces rain. — [RimWorld wiki: Weather](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Weather)

**Vintage Story** (wiki plus the published source of the essentials mod)
- Temperature formula: yearly sea-level average 30 °C at the equator, about −20 °C at the poles, 10 °C in temperate zones; monthly swing ±0 °C at the equator, ±16.25 °C temperate, ±32.5 °C at the poles; northern hemisphere coldest mid-January and hottest mid-July, reversed in the south; daily cycle hottest at 4 pm and coldest at 4 am with swing ±9 °C at 0 % rainfall down to ±2.5 °C at 100 % rainfall; about −1.5 °C per 10 blocks of elevation; a climate noise map adds up to 3 °C to yearly and 1 °C to daily values. — [Vintage Story wiki: Temperature](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Temperature)
- The API's elevation rule in code: `GetScaledAdjustedTemperature = clamp((unscaledTemp − distToSealevel / 1.5) / (255/60) − 20, −20, 40)`; rainfall is also altitude-adjusted (`rainfall + (y − sealevel)/2 + …`). Climate values are stored as bytes (0–255). — [vsapi: Common/Climate.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsapi/blob/master/Common/Climate.cs)
- **Precipitation is a stateless function of position and date.** `GetPrecipitation(x, y, z, totalDays) = max(0, rainCloudness − 0.5)`, with `rainCloudness = max(0, noise(x/18 + days·18, z/18, days·4)·1.6 − clamp(sub(x/8 + days·24, z/8, days·6)·5 − 1 − w, 0, 1) + w)` and `w = clamp((worldgenRainfall − 0.6)·2, −1, 1)`. `noise` is 4-octave simplex (base frequency 0.02/3, persistence 0.95) and `sub` 3-octave (0.004/3), both seeded from the world seed. The `days·18` term slides the field along x (about 324 blocks per game day); the third coordinate makes it evolve. — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSystemBase.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSystemBase.cs)
- Because it is a pure function, the same code answers history queries: `GetEnvironmentWetness(pos, days, hourResolution = 2)` sums the rainfall function over the past N days at 2-hour steps. — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSystemBase.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSystemBase.cs)
- **Cloud, wind and event patterns are stateful.** Each 512×512-block map region runs its own simulation choosing among weighted weather patterns (clear sky, cumulus, overcast, stratus, haze, cumulonimbus…), wind patterns and weather events; the value at a position is interpolated between the 4 nearest regions. The per-region `LCGRandom` is position-seeded and its state (`LcgCurrentSeed` etc.) is saved and synced along with the transition weight; a transition takes `30 + rand·3600/SpeedOfTime` seconds. — [Vintage Story wiki: Weather](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Weather), [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSimulationRegion.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationRegion.cs)
- Rain turns to snow below 4 °C; snow accumulates below 0 °C (or 0–1.5 °C while it snows) up to 3 layers. Events are gated by climate: light/heavy thunder need ≥ 12 °C and rainfall ≥ 0.2/0.3; large hail needs ≥ 18 °C. Wind patterns: still / light / medium / strong breeze at 22.7 % each, storm 9.1 %, each with a duration range in hours; wind speed gains 1 % per block above sea level + 10 (cap 150 %). — [Vintage Story wiki: Weather](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Weather)
- **Snow catch-up for unloaded chunks** (the one piece of accumulated state): each weather region records an hourly snow delta at only the 8 corners of its cuboid (2×2×2: two heights, sea level and map top) and keeps one game year of them in a ring buffer. Per hour and corner: if `T > 1.5 °C` or (`rain < 0.05` and `T > 0`) the delta is `−T/15`, else `+rainfall/3`. A chunk column stores `lastSnowAccumUpdateTotalHours`; on load it sums the snapshots newer than that, interpolates between corners, and clamps to `[−1, layers + 0.6]`. If more than a year passed, the old accumulation is ignored. A region catches up at most 480 hours per 25 ms tick. The comment in the code gives the reason: "We don't want to simulate 512x512 blocks at all times". — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSimulationRegion.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationRegion.cs), [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs)
- Gameplay hooks: all trees and crops stop growing at 0 °C; cultivated crops take cold/heat damage outside their range (harvest halved); wild and unfed animals lose weight in cold months; bees hibernate at freezing; player wetness lowers felt temperature by up to 13.5 °C. — [Vintage Story wiki: Temperature](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Temperature)

**TerraFirmaCraft** (1.20 source)
- Temperature = `averageTemperature + monthTemperature − elevationTemperature + dailyTemperature`. The average comes from stored chunk data. `monthTemperature = monthModifier · triangle(−3, 15, 1/(2·temperatureScale), z)` — a triangle wave of latitude (default `temperatureScale = 20,000` blocks) scaling a per-month modifier that is interpolated linearly between the current and next month. Elevation: `clamp((y − seaLevel)·0.16225, 0, 17.822)` °C. — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Daily temperature is stateless: a random source seeded by the day number (plus a salt) gives `((rand − rand) + 0.3·hourModifier)·3` with `hourModifier = hour/6 − 1` folded around noon — range −3.9…+3.9 °C, the same for the whole world on a given day. — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Below ground the seasonal and daily terms fade out with depth and the result blends toward a fixed deep temperature (15 °C at the lava level). — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Hysteresis constants: snow freezes at −2 °C and melts at +2 °C; ice freezes at −4 °C and melts at +2 °C. Snow and ice placement is patchy by 2D noise: snow where `clampedMap(T, −10, 2, −1, 1) + noise < 0`; ice depends on temperature, water depth (0–5 blocks) and a patch-noise threshold; salt water makes sea ice. — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Fog is a stateless day roll: one day in 10 (`FOGGY_DAY_RARITY = 10`), only where rainfall ≥ 150 mm (peaking at 300), strongest from night to early morning. Wind is a day-seeded random angle and intensity plus terms for altitude and current rain. — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Four seasons of early/mid/late months; annual rainfall 0–500 mm, varying through the year to a peak of up to twice the local average, with the peak season shown to the player. *(search summary of the field guide, page not opened)* — [TFC Field Guide: Calendar and Climate](https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/the_world/climate.html)

### Inferences
- The pattern that fits this project is Vintage Story's split: **precipitation and temperature as pure functions of (position, date, seed)**; everything stateful kept small, coarse and derived (their 8-corner hourly snow deltas). Their stateful part (per-region pattern RNG with saved state, interpolated between regions) is exactly what this project should not copy: it needs neighbour state to agree at a seam.
- Dwarf Fortress shows how little is needed for weather to matter in a fortress game: a calendar, a climate class per site, rain/snow, and a hard freeze/thaw switch on surface water. Its cost is that nothing is spatially coherent and freeze/thaw is instantaneous (and lethal) — a thickness model avoids both.
- DF's "plants and caravans follow the calendar season, weather follows the biome" is a useful separation: calendar-driven content (trade, festivals, migrants) stays globally predictable while local climate decides what the season feels like.
- TFC's day-seeded daily offset is the simplest stateless anomaly but is the same everywhere in the world on a given day — fine for one base, wrong for a world where regions hundreds of kilometres apart should differ. It needs a spatial coordinate in the hash (see section 2).
- Hysteresis (TFC: snow freezes at −2, melts at +2) is what stops flicker at the threshold; with a pure function the equivalent is to threshold a smoothed (multi-day mean) temperature rather than the instantaneous one.

## 2. Generating plausible day-to-day weather from climate normals (WGEN and descendants), spatially coherent and stateless

### Takeaway
The standard recipe is Richardson's: a two-state first-order Markov chain for wet/dry days, a gamma (or exponential) distribution for wet-day amounts, and temperature as a seasonal cycle plus autocorrelated residuals with a different mean on wet days. A Markov chain is stateful and has no spatial coherence; the stateless, coherent equivalent is to threshold one smooth space–time noise field against each cell's monthly wet-day probability — Wilks's multisite trick (independent per-site models driven by spatially correlated random numbers) taken to the continuum, and what Vintage Story ships.

### Cited Findings
- WGEN structure (Richardson 1981; Richardson & Wright 1984): precipitation status from a first-order two-state Markov chain with separate transition probabilities per month; wet-day amount from a two-parameter gamma distribution with monthly parameters; minimum temperature, maximum temperature and radiation as normal variables whose mean and standard deviation vary daily along fitted cosine curves, with maximum temperature and radiation conditioned on wet/dry status (separate curves), constant lag-1 autocorrelation and constant cross-correlation between the three. — [Semenov et al. 1998, Climate Research 10:95–107, Table 1](https://www.int-res.com/articles/cr/10/c010p095.pdf)
- LARS-WG replaces the Markov chain with alternating wet and dry series whose lengths are drawn from semi-empirical (10-bin histogram) distributions for the month in which the series starts, and fits Fourier series (three harmonics for precipitation parameters in the comparison) to the means and standard deviations of temperature, separately for wet and dry days. The comparison found the Markov chain's geometric spell lengths cannot reproduce observed wet/dry series shapes well, and that both generators understate inter-annual variability of monthly means. — [Semenov et al. 1998](https://www.int-res.com/articles/cr/10/c010p095.pdf)
- A worked Richardson-type generator with fitted values for Manhattan, Kansas: P(wet | dry) = 0.13–0.29 by month, P(wet | wet) = 0.32–0.50, gamma shape 0.59–0.75 (always below 1, i.e. many small falls and a long tail), scale 4.2–22.3 mm; over the whole record P(W|D) = 0.206 and P(W|W) = 0.417. — [Patrignani, PyNotes in Agriscience: Rainfall generator](https://soilwater.github.io/pynotes-agriscience/exercises/rainfall_generator.html)
- Parameters from monthly data alone: Geng, Penning de Vries & Supit (1986) found the transition probabilities are approximately linear in the monthly fraction of wet days and the gamma scale is tied to mean rain per wet day, so both can be estimated from monthly totals and wet-day counts. *(search summary; the paper's coefficients were not seen)* — [Geng et al. 1986, Agric. For. Meteorol. 36:363–376](https://typeset.io/papers/a-simple-method-for-generating-daily-rainfall-data-2ylg892yvg)
- The gamma shape can be set from the mean and standard deviation of wet-day rainfall (shape = mean² / SD²). *(search summary)* — [precipr WGEN_ppt.R](https://rdrr.io/github/MartinHoldrege/precipr/src/R/WGEN_ppt.R)
- Spatial coherence in the literature: Wilks (1998) extends the chain-dependent model to many sites by driving each site's own model with "serially independent but spatially correlated random numbers" (correlated Gaussians mapped to uniforms by the quantile transform); it reproduces inter-station correlations of daily and monthly values. Known weaknesses: too little variance at monthly and annual scales, long dry spells and multi-day extremes. *(search summary)* — [Wilks 1998, J. Hydrology 210:178–191](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/60137895), [Keller et al. 2015, HESS 19:2163](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/19/2163/2015/)
- Daily generators commonly show "overdispersion": their seasonal totals vary less from year to year than observed. *(search summary)* — [Wilks & Wilby 1999, Prog. Phys. Geogr. 23:329–357](https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/valerie.monbet/doc/Wilks&Wilby.pdf)
- A shipped stateless coherent field: Vintage Story's `getPrecipNoise` (formula in section 1) — 3D simplex noise over (x + drift·t, z, t), minus a second noise that punches dry gaps, plus an offset from the worldgen rainfall value, thresholded at 0.5. — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSystemBase.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSystemBase.cs)

### Inferences
- **Markov-chain algebra worth having** (standard results, derived, not quoted from a page read): the stationary wet-day probability is π = p01 / (1 + p01 − p11); the lag-1 autocorrelation of occurrence is r = p11 − p01; wet spells are geometric with mean 1 / (1 − p11), dry spells with mean 1 / p01. With the Kansas values, π ≈ 0.26, r ≈ 0.21, mean wet spell ≈ 1.7 days, mean dry spell ≈ 4.9 days. These are the targets a noise-based generator must match: a wet fraction of π and a persistence of a couple of days.
- **What the forge must store per climate cell per month** for a generator to run: mean temperature, diurnal range (or continentality), precipitation total, and wet-day fraction π. If the forge produces only totals, π can be derived as P / (days · mean wet-day amount), with the mean wet-day amount taken from a rule by climate type (low in drizzly maritime climates, about 4–8 mm; high in convective and monsoon climates, 10–20 mm). My recollection of Geng's relations, unverified, is p01 ≈ 0.75 π, p11 ≈ 0.25 + p01, gamma scale β ≈ −2.16 + 1.83 · (mean mm per wet day), shape = mean / β.
- **Stateless occurrence**: let `N(x, y, t)` be a smooth space–time noise mapped to a uniform value `u ∈ [0, 1)` (apply the noise's cumulative distribution as a lookup table so `u` is genuinely uniform). The cell is wet when `u < π(cell, month)`. This reproduces the wet fraction exactly by construction; persistence comes from the noise's time scale (choose the time lattice so the autocorrelation at one day is ≈ 0.2–0.4, i.e. features lasting 2–4 days); spatial coherence comes from the space scale (synoptic systems are 500–2,000 km on Earth; scale to the world's size so a storm covers several regions).
- **Stateless amount**: reuse the same `u`. On a wet cell `v = 1 − u/π` is uniform in (0, 1] and is 0 at the edge of the rain area and near 1 at its core; amount = Q(v), the quantile function of the wet-day distribution scaled so its mean matches the monthly normal. For an exponential distribution Q(v) = −mean · ln(1 − v) — a 256-entry integer table. This makes rain heaviest in the middle of a system and tapering to drizzle at its edge, for free, and keeps the monthly total right. A gamma with shape < 1 is the same idea with a different table per shape class.
- **Stateless temperature anomaly**: a second, slower field `A(x, y, t)` (time scale 5–10 days, amplitude = stored monthly SD, larger in winter and in continental interiors) added to the seasonal curve; plus WGEN's wet-day conditioning as a deterministic term: daytime maximum lowered and diurnal range shrunk in proportion to cloud cover, which is itself a function of `u` (cloudy when `u < π + margin`).
- **Fronts and moving systems without state**: sample the noise at an advected position, `N(x − U·t, y − V·t, t)`, where (U, V) is the stored prevailing wind for the season. A velocity that varies in space shears the field without bound as `t` grows (the gradient picks up a term proportional to t · ∂U/∂y), so advect with a velocity that is constant per large band and use the flow-map trick from water shaders: two samples whose advection clocks restart every period P, half a period out of phase, cross-faded with triangular weights. Both are pure functions of `t`.
- **Named storms as stateless objects**: divide time into epochs (say 4 days) and space into coarse cells (say 500 km). For each (epoch, coarse cell), `roll(epoch, cellId, SALT_STORM)` decides whether a storm is born there, its birth offset in space and time, lifetime, radius and intensity; its track is a closed-form curve (birth point plus the integral of the seasonal steering wind, parabolic recurvature for tropical cyclones). To evaluate at (x, y, t), enumerate the 3×3 coarse cells × the 2–3 epochs that could still be alive — a bounded, constant amount of work, the same from any region. Gating the birth roll by the stored climate (sea-surface warmth and season for cyclones, convective season for thunderstorm clusters, cold and wind for blizzards, dryness and wind for sandstorms) gives storm seasons. This is the worley/"feature point" idiom applied to space–time.
- **Hash noise for deterministic fixed-point maths**: value noise on an integer lattice — hash each lattice corner with the existing splitmix-style `roll(tLattice, cornerId, salt)`, interpolate trilinearly with an integer smoothstep (`3s² − 2s³` in Q16). Only integer multiplies and shifts; identical on every platform including wasm32. Two or three octaves are enough. Gradient/simplex noise looks better but brings nothing here: the field is sampled once per climate cell, not per pixel, and value noise is easier to map to an exact uniform distribution.
- **Cellular-automaton weather is the wrong tool here**: it needs neighbour state every step, so a region would need its neighbours' weather cells through the halo, parked regions would need replay, and rollback would have to snapshot it. Nothing it produces (moving bands, growth and decay) is out of reach of advected noise plus storm objects.

### Gaps
- Richardson (1981), Richardson & Wright (1984, ARS-8), Geng et al. (1986) and Wilks & Wilby (1999) were not readable in full (paywall, connection refused, HTTP 403/429). The Geng coefficients above are recollection and must be checked before use; the structural description of WGEN is verified through Semenov et al. (1998).
- No write-up was found of a shipped game that states "our weather is a pure function so multiplayer clients need no weather sync"; Vintage Story's source is the closest primary evidence and it still syncs its cloud-pattern state. A flight-sim forum thread asserts that the same seed, time and place regenerate the same clouds ([AVSIM](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/567271-will-real-world-weather-deliver-the-same-exact-clouds-to-all/page/2/)) — anecdotal.
- Matching a target lag-1 autocorrelation and spell-length distribution with thresholded noise needs an offline calibration run (measure wet-spell histograms from the noise at several π); no source gives the mapping in closed form.

## 3. Calendar design: year length, seasons, hemispheres, day length, moon and tides

### Takeaway
Games compress the year to between about 1 and 17 hours of play: long enough that a season demands preparation, short enough that a session sees the weather turn. Hemispheres are handled by a half-year phase shift on latitude sign, the tropics by replacing summer/winter with wet/dry. Day length follows from one closed-form formula in latitude and solar declination.

### Cited Findings
- Year lengths in real time: Dwarf Fortress 336 days = 1 h 07 min at 100 FPS (403,200 ticks); RimWorld 60 days = about 16 h 40 min at normal speed; Serene Seasons 96 Minecraft days by default; Don't Starve (Reign of Giants) 70 days, each season configurable from 5 to 50 days. — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time), [RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time), [Serene Seasons wiki](https://github.com/Glitchfiend/SereneSeasons/wiki/Seasons), [Don't Starve wiki](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Seasons)
- RimWorld deliberately traded days for day length: it doubled the day and halved days per year while keeping the year's real-time length. — [RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time)
- Two time scales on one calendar: Dwarf Fortress runs the same 336-day year at 72 game-seconds per tick in fortress mode and 0.5 in adventure mode. — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- Hemispheres: RimWorld maps each quadrum to opposite seasons north and south and makes the equatorial belt seasonless; Vintage Story reverses the coldest/hottest month south of the equator and scales the seasonal swing from ±0 °C at the equator to ±32.5 °C at the poles; the DF wiki's month table lists Granite as March (north) or September (south). — [RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time), [Vintage Story wiki: Temperature](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Temperature), [DF wiki: Calendar](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Calendar)
- Tropics: Serene Seasons gives tropical biomes wet and dry seasons instead of four; Dwarf Fortress announces "wet" and "dry" seasons in some biomes; Don't Starve: Shipwrecked uses mild / hurricane / monsoon / dry. — [Serene Seasons wiki](https://github.com/Glitchfiend/SereneSeasons/wiki/Seasons), [DF wiki: Calendar](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Calendar), [Don't Starve wiki](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Seasons)
- Solar declination by day of year: δ = −23.44° · cos(360°/365 · (N + 10)), N = 0 on 1 January; error up to about 1.5° near the September equinox. A more accurate variant with orbital eccentricity stays within ±0.2°. — [Wikipedia: Position of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_of_the_Sun)
- Sunrise equation: cos H₀ = −tan φ · tan δ (φ latitude, H₀ hour angle at sunrise/sunset); with refraction and the Sun's disc, cos H₀ = (sin(−0.833°) − sin φ sin δ) / (cos φ cos δ). No solution when |tan φ · tan δ| > 1: polar day or polar night. Daylight at φ and −φ on the same day sums to 24 hours. — [Wikipedia: Sunrise equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise_equation)
- Moon: Dwarf Fortress fixes full moons to calendar dates that step back about two days each month, and uses them as a gameplay trigger (werebeasts). — [DF wiki: Calendar](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Calendar)

### Inferences
- Day length in hours = 24 · H₀ / 180° = (24/π) · acos(clamp(−tan φ · tan δ, −1, 1)); the clamp yields 24 h (polar day) and 0 h (polar night) directly. Sun elevation at local hour angle h: sin(elevation) = sin φ sin δ + cos φ cos δ cos h (standard spherical astronomy; not on the page read).
- For a 336-day year the declination becomes δ = −tilt · cos(2π · (d + offset) / 336); with the year starting at the spring equinox (1st of Granite = first day of spring) the simplest form is δ = tilt · sin(2π · d / 336) for the north. Axial tilt is a world parameter (Earth 23.44°); raising it widens the polar-day belt and strengthens seasons.
- No trigonometry is needed at run time: day length depends only on (latitude row, day of year). A table of `rows × 336` u16 "daylight ticks", built once at world load with `libm`, is a few hundred kilobytes for thousands of rows — or 64 latitude bands interpolated.
- The temperature seasons lag the sun: Vintage Story puts the extremes in mid-January and mid-July, about a month after the solstices. With stored monthly normals the lag is in the data; with a harmonic, add a phase lag of about 1/12 year on land and up to 1/6 year at maritime cells.
- **The existing calendar is very fast**: `DAY_MS = 10_000` gives a 10-second day and a 56-minute year — the DF fortress pace (1 h 07 min per year), ten times faster than Don't Starve and seventeen times faster than RimWorld. At that pace a visible day/night cycle is a strobe (DF has none in fortress mode), a "storm lasting three days" is 30 seconds, and diurnal temperature is noise to the player. Options: (a) keep the pace and drop diurnal effects in fortress view — weather events measured in days, seasons the unit that matters, as in DF; (b) lengthen the day (RimWorld's move) so the year is several hours; (c) keep one world tick but decouple "weather days" from calendar days is NOT advisable — two clocks is the kind of thing that breaks seams. With one world tick shared by fortress and adventure players, DF's two-speed trick is not available, so this is a design decision to settle before tuning any weather time scale.
- A year of 12 × 28 days divides evenly by 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 12 — convenient for monthly normals (12 values, no fractional month), 4 seasons, 8 sub-seasons, and a 28-day lunar month (one full moon per month on a fixed date: zero-cost moon phase = day of month).
- Wet/dry seasons need no special case if the forge stores monthly precipitation: the tropics simply have flat temperature and strongly peaked rain. The season *name* shown to the player can be classified from the normals (if the annual temperature range is below about 5 °C and the wettest month has more than about 3× the driest month's rain, announce wet/dry; if neither varies, announce nothing — DF's three cases).
- Tides, if wanted, are a closed-form function: height = A_cell · cos(2π · t / T_lunar_half_day + phase_cell) · (1 + 0.4 · cos(2π · dayOfMonth / 14)) for the spring–neap cycle, with amplitude per coastal cell from the forge (shelf width, funnel-shaped bays). Since water is a per-column surface height, a tidal offset on sea columns is cheap; whether a 10-second day makes tides legible is the same pacing question as above.

### Gaps
- No developer statement was found on *why* a particular year length was chosen, other than the DF wiki's remark on fortress versus adventure pace and RimWorld's changelog. The trade-off paragraph above is inference from the numbers.
- Minecraft's 20-minute day (hence Serene Seasons' 32-hour default year) and Don't Starve's 8-minute day are from memory, not from a page read.

## 4. Temperature at run time

### Takeaway
Every game that computes temperature uses the same additive stack: stored annual or monthly mean, a seasonal term whose amplitude grows with latitude and continentality, a diurnal term whose amplitude shrinks with humidity, a small noise term, and an altitude lapse. Underground is a constant near the annual mean.

### Cited Findings
- Vintage Story's numbers for each term: seasonal ±0 / ±16.25 / ±32.5 °C (equator / temperate / pole); diurnal ±9 °C when dry to ±2.5 °C when wet, peak at 16:00, trough at 04:00; lapse about 1.5 °C per 10 blocks; noise up to 3 °C yearly and 1 °C daily. — [Vintage Story wiki: Temperature](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Temperature)
- TerraFirmaCraft: lapse 0.16225 °C per block capped at 17.8 °C; monthly modifier interpolated linearly between months; daily term within ±3.9 °C; underground the seasonal and daily terms fade with depth toward a fixed 15 °C. — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Dwarf Fortress: underground is a constant 10015 °U (8.3 °C) regardless of the surface; temperatures are integers and conduction per tick is the difference divided by specific heat. — [DF wiki: Temperature](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Temperature)
- RimWorld: the outdoors of a map has one temperature; rooms equalise toward it every 120 ticks; temperatures are whole degrees Celsius internally. — [RimWorld wiki: Temperature](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Temperature)
- Vintage Story treats exposure, not air temperature, as the gameplay variable: wetness subtracts up to 13.5 °C from felt temperature, strong wind outside a room cools the body, an enclosed room counts as warmer. — [Vintage Story wiki: Temperature](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Temperature), [Vintage Story wiki: Weather](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Weather)
- WGEN conditions maximum temperature on wet/dry status (wet days have a lower maximum) and gives the daily values a constant lag-1 autocorrelation. — [Semenov et al. 1998](https://www.int-res.com/articles/cr/10/c010p095.pdf)

### Inferences
- Proposed function, all terms pure in (cell, tick, seed):
  `T = seasonal(cell, dayOfYear) + diurnal(cell, tickOfDay, cloud) + anomaly(x, y, t) − lapse · (height − cellMeanHeight)`.
  - `seasonal`: linear or Catmull-Rom interpolation between the 12 stored monthly normals, each anchored at mid-month. With 28-day months this is integer arithmetic and needs no sine at all. If the forge stores harmonics instead (mean, amplitude and phase of the annual and semi-annual waves — 5 numbers instead of 12), evaluation needs a sine: a 1,024-entry quarter-wave table in Q15, or `libm::sinf` (allowed by the project rules and deterministic). Monthly normals are simpler and capture monsoon-shaped curves that two harmonics cannot.
  - `diurnal`: amplitude = stored monthly diurnal range × (1 − 0.7 · cloudFraction), shaped by a 24-entry table peaking mid-afternoon; cloudFraction comes from the precipitation field `u` (section 2). Scale amplitude by daylight fraction so polar night has no diurnal cycle.
  - `anomaly`: slow value noise (5–10 day features), amplitude = stored monthly SD (roughly 2 °C tropics and oceans, 4–6 °C mid-latitude continental winter — a forge output or a rule from continentality).
  - `lapse`: the forge's own `lapse_c_per_km` so runtime and worldgen agree; evaluate the climate terms per climate cell and apply the lapse per column only where a consumer needs column precision (snow line on a mountainside).
- Units: store and compute temperature as `i16` centi-degrees Celsius (range ±327 °C) or DF-style offset integers; never as a float in hashed state. All thresholds (freeze, crop limits) are then exact integer compares.
- Underground: T(depth) = annual mean + (surface seasonal anomaly) · e^(−depth/d) with the phase lag ignored; in practice a 3-step table is enough — full seasonal signal at the surface, half at 1–2 m, none below about 5–10 m — which is TFC's fade and DF's constant. A geothermal gradient (+25 °C/km) only matters for very deep fortresses and magma.
- Indoor temperature is a separate, stateful system (rooms, heat sources) of the RimWorld kind; the weather system only supplies the boundary value. Wind chill and wetness belong in the creature's felt-temperature rule, not in the air temperature: felt = T − k₁ · wind · (exposed) − k₂ · wetness.
- Rollback safety: none of the above is state. A consumer that reads T at tick t during a re-simulated tick gets the same answer by construction; nothing needs an `#[undo]` wrapper and nothing enters the state hash.

### Gaps
- Typical real-world values for the day-to-day standard deviation of temperature by climate type, and for how diurnal range depends on cloud cover, were not sourced; the figures above are working assumptions to be replaced by forge outputs or checked against a climatology reference.

## 5. Seasonal world state: what is a pure function and what needs accumulation

### Takeaway
Snow depth, ice thickness, soil moisture and river level are integrals of weather with a floor at zero, so they are not strictly closed-form — but each has a cheap exact answer: either a normal curve precomputed by the forge and modulated by noise, or a bounded replay of daily steps from a known-zero date, cached as derived data. Vintage Story proves the coarse version works in a shipped game: hourly deltas at 8 points per 512-block region, summed on chunk load.

### Cited Findings
- Degree-day (temperature-index) melt: melt = factor × positive degree-days. Reported snow melt factors span 0.5–20 mm °C⁻¹ day⁻¹ across sites; typical values are 2–6, converging on 3–5 for deep snowpacks; the threshold is usually 0 °C (calibrated between −1 and +1 °C). *(search summary)* — [HESS 30:2613 (2026)](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/2613/2026/)
- Stefan's law: ice thickness is proportional to the square root of accumulated freezing degree-days. — [Wikipedia: Stefan's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan%27s_equation)
- Coefficients: empirical 0.014–0.017 m per (°C·day)^½ for small water bodies (1.4–1.7 cm per √(°C·day)). US Army CRREL form `thickness[in] = C · √(AFDD[°F·days])` with C about 0.8 for a windy lake without snow, 0.5–0.7 for an average lake with snow, 0.12–0.15 for an average river with snow; snow cover insulates and lowers the coefficient; the formula applies only during growth, not after the seasonal peak of freezing degree-days. *(search summary)* — [Water 14(23):3886 (2022)](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/14/23/3886/xml), [CRREL TN 04-3](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090109023326/http://www.crrel.usace.army.mil/techpub/CRREL_Reports/reports/TN04-3.pdf)
- Vintage Story's shipped snow rule and catch-up (per hour: melt `T/15` when warm or dry, accumulate `rainfall/3` otherwise; 8 corner samples per region; one-year ring buffer; per-chunk last-update hour; reset if older than a year). — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSimulationRegion.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationRegion.cs), [WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs)
- TerraFirmaCraft uses thresholds with hysteresis plus patch noise instead of accumulation (snow −2/+2 °C, ice −4/+2 °C; ice also depends on water depth 0–5 blocks; salt water makes sea ice). — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Dwarf Fortress models freezing as an instantaneous state switch by depth (≥ 4/7 becomes a wall), with lethal and structure-destroying side effects on freeze and thaw; pools dry in hot dry seasons and refill by rain; plant growths are scheduled by tick ranges within the year. — [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather), [DF wiki: Ice](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Ice), [DF wiki: Climate](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Climate), [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- Crop rules in shipped games are threshold tables against temperature: RimWorld stops growth at 0 °C and 58 °C, slows it below 6 °C and above 42 °C, kills at −10 °C; Vintage Story stops all growth at 0 °C and halves the harvest on cold or heat damage; Farthest Frontier gives each crop frost/heat/drought loss percentages. — [RimWorld wiki: Temperature](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Temperature), [Vintage Story wiki: Temperature](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Temperature), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/farthest-frontier-crop-rotation-guide)

### Inferences

Classification (all inference):

| Phenomenon | Pure function of (stored climate, tick, seed)? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Sun position, day length, moon phase, tide | Yes | Closed form / table (section 3) |
| Air temperature, cloud, precipitation rate and type, wind, fog | Yes | Sections 2 and 4 |
| Storms (cyclones, blizzards, thunderstorm clusters, sandstorms) | Yes | Epoch-hashed storm objects with closed-form tracks (section 2) |
| Monsoon onset, wet/dry season | Yes | In the monthly normals; onset date jittered by a yearly roll |
| Vegetation phenology (leaf-out, leaf-fall, grass browning) | Yes, as climatology | Dates precomputed by the forge from the normals (growing degree-days above 5 °C reaching a species threshold; photoperiod below a threshold for leaf-fall; dry-month count for browning), shifted by a per-year anomaly roll |
| Animal migration / hibernation windows | Yes | Calendar windows per climate cell derived from the normals |
| Wildfire *season* (danger index) | Yes | Function of recent stateless rainfall and temperature (a 14–30 day sum, cached) |
| Snow depth | No (floored integral) | Bounded daily replay from a snow-free date, or forge normal curve × anomaly |
| Lake / river / sea ice thickness | No (floored integral, growth ∝ √FDD) | Same; Stefan on the running freezing-degree-day sum |
| River discharge and level | Mostly yes | Forge stores a 12-month discharge normal per river reach (routing the monthly rain and snowmelt offline); runtime multiplies by a basin-scale anomaly that is a lagged sum of the stateless rainfall over the upstream basin's climate cells |
| Floodplain inundation, lake level | Derived | Level = function of discharge (stage curve from the forge); water is a per-column height so the surface simply rises |
| Drought | Derived | Standardised 3–6 month rainfall deficit computed from the stateless field, cached per month |
| Soil moisture, crop growth, individual fires, player-altered snow (shovelled, trampled) | No — true state | Region-owned sim state, stepped daily, rollback via the normal undo machinery |

- **Snow, exactly and statelessly, by bounded replay.** Define daily `snowfall(cell, d)` (precipitation on days with T below about +1 °C) and `melt(cell, d) = DDF · max(0, T − 0)` with DDF about 3–5 mm/°C/day, both pure. Snow water equivalent obeys `S(d) = max(0, S(d−1) + snowfall − melt)`. For any cell that is snow-free at some point in the year there is a date d₀ (late summer) with S = 0 regardless of history, so S(d) is determined by at most 336 daily steps from d₀ — a pure function of (cell, d) that can be memoised per (cell, day) in a cache outside the hashed state. For permanent-snow cells (glaciers, ice caps), clamp at a cap and call it permanent. Cost: one step per cell per game day when evaluated incrementally; 336 steps per cell on a cold start.
- **Cheaper alternative**: the forge runs the same recurrence on the monthly normals and stores a 12-value "normal snow depth" curve per cell; runtime depth = normal(d) × (1 + k · winterAnomaly(cell, year)), with winterAnomaly from a per-year low-frequency roll. No replay, but individual snowstorms do not leave visible steps in the depth. A hybrid works: normal curve as the base plus the last 7–14 days of stateless snowfall minus melt as the fresh layer.
- **Ice** follows the same pattern: freezing-degree-day sum since the autumn zero-crossing (a floored running sum → bounded replay), thickness = α · √FDD with α about 1.5–2.5 cm per √(°C·day) for lakes, lower for rivers (current) and under snow; break-up when the thawing-degree-day sum since the spring zero-crossing exceeds a threshold proportional to peak thickness. Sea ice: lower freezing point (about −1.8 °C) and slower. Because thickness is continuous, gameplay gets gradations DF lacks: thin ice that breaks under a loaded wagon, a river that is crossable for six weeks, a harbour closed until break-up — and no instant killing freeze.
- **Where the result lives**: snow depth and ice thickness should be *recipe data on the column*, like `ColumnGround::water_top` — a derived overlay computed from (climate cell value, column height, slope/shade modifier, local edits), never voxels written into the chunk. That keeps them out of the terrain store, the save cut and the undo log. Only player edits (a cleared path, a hole cut in the ice, a snow fort) are state: store them as sparse per-column deltas with an expiry (cleared snow refills as new snowfall accumulates past the edit's timestamp).
- **Hysteresis without state**: threshold a trailing 3–5 day mean (sum of the pure daily function over a fixed window) instead of the instantaneous value; the freeze/thaw line then moves smoothly and deterministically, with no flicker and nothing to store.
- **Rollback**: a pure function evaluated at tick t is the same before and after an undo. The only requirement is that caches are keyed by their inputs (cell, day) and never mutated in place by sim code paths that are hashed; a cache miss recomputes. True state (soil moisture, crops) steps on a day boundary inside the region's normal tick and uses the existing `#[rollback]` wrappers; keep it per farm plot or per climate cell, not per column.
- **Vegetation**: leaf colour and presence as a function of (species phenology dates for the cell, day, per-tree hash jitter of a few days) is a render-side decision the client can make alone — the sim needs only "is this tree in leaf" as a pure predicate, for gameplay such as fruit or foliage.

### Gaps
- Degree-day factors and Stefan coefficients come from search summaries of the cited papers, not from the papers' full text; Hock (2003), the usual reference for degree-day modelling, was not opened.
- No source was consulted for phenology thresholds (growing degree-days for leaf-out, photoperiod for leaf-fall), river routing lags, wildfire danger indices or cyclone climatology; the corresponding rows above are design proposals without numbers behind them.
- Whether a 12-month discharge normal per river reach is affordable in the forge's stored data was not checked against the forge code.

## 6. Region-seam consistency and catch-up of parked regions

### Takeaway
If weather is a pure function of world-global coordinates, the world tick and the world seed, two regions on different hosts agree at their shared edge with no message at all, and a parked region needs no catch-up for weather itself. What remains is derived accumulation (bounded replay or forge normals) and true local state (daily closed-form or coarse stepping on load).

### Cited Findings
- Vintage Story gets seam consistency for precipitation for free because the function takes absolute block coordinates and total days; its *stateful* pattern simulation needs a different mechanism — each 512-block region owns a state and every position blends the 4 nearest regions, and the state must be saved and sent to clients. — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSystemBase.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSystemBase.cs), [WeatherSimulationRegion.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationRegion.cs)
- Vintage Story's catch-up for unloaded chunks is a replay of stored coarse deltas, capped (480 hours per tick) and bounded (one year of history; beyond that the old accumulation is discarded). — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs)
- A forum report describes the visible effect: snow that "should have fallen" appears when an area loads, without a gradual build-up. *(search summary)* — [Vintage Story forum](https://www.vintagestory.at/forums/topic/5224-does-anyone-know-what-is-up-with-the-snowfall-in-my-world/)

### Inferences
- **Rules that make the seam invisible**:
  1. Every weather input is addressed in world-global units: the climate-cell lattice is global (cell id = global cell x, y), noise lattice corners are hashed by global lattice coordinates, time is the world tick. Nothing may be region-local — not the lattice origin, not a per-region seed, not a "region's current weather" variable.
  2. Interpolation across a region edge reads climate cells that belong to the neighbour. The forged world is installed on every host, so those are local reads of immutable data — no halo traffic, no `WorldView` dependency.
  3. Evaluation is quantised in time identically everywhere: e.g. weather fields are evaluated at "weather steps" of N ticks (every 10 ticks = 1/20 of the current game day) derived from the world tick by integer division, and interpolated between steps only on the client for rendering.
  4. No floating-point dependence on evaluation order: integer/fixed-point throughout, or `libm` with a fixed expression order.
  5. Storm objects are enumerated from global coarse cells; a storm straddling four regions is the same object in each because each derives it from the same (epoch, coarse cell) roll.
- **Consequence for the multi-host design**: weather adds no `RegionInput`/`Transfer`, no row to any table (so nothing two hosts could both write), nothing to a cut, and nothing to the state hash. The client can compute it too, from the same function, so weather is never streamed — only the forged climate (already needed) and the tick.
- **Parked regions**: on load at tick t the region evaluates weather at t; snow/ice come from the bounded replay or the forge normals; true state uses per-system catch-up, in order of preference: (a) closed form (crop maturity = planted day + growth days adjusted by the summed growing-degree function over the interval — a sum of a pure function, computable in one pass); (b) coarse replay at daily steps over the absence, capped at one year, after which the result is replaced by the steady-state value (fields gone to weed, soil moisture at its seasonal normal) — Vintage Story's "more than a year → ignore old accumulation" rule; (c) nothing at all for state that should freeze while parked.
- Catch-up must itself be deterministic and independent of *when* the region is loaded relative to other regions: compute it as a function of (saved state at tick s, tick t), never of wall-clock or load order. Run it before the region's first tick so the first hashed state already includes it.
- **Player-visible seam for accumulated state**: if region A has been running and region B was parked, snow in both comes from the same pure function, so depths match at the edge. Only true state (a trampled path) can differ, and that is local by nature.
- **Things that break the property** and must be refused in review: weather that depends on entities (a wizard's storm) must be modelled as an explicit world-level event recorded by the coordinator with a start tick, centre and radius in global coordinates — a parameter of the pure function, delivered to all regions before its start tick, never as region-local state.

### Gaps
- No published description was found of a multi-server game that keeps weather consistent across server boundaries by pure functions; the argument above is from first principles plus Vintage Story's single-server code.

## 7. Long-term climate variability

### Takeaway
Daily generators are known to under-produce year-to-year variability, so wet and dry years must be added deliberately as low-frequency terms. With stateless rolls this is cheap: a per-year, per-large-area anomaly and a multi-year oscillation, both functions of the year number.

### Cited Findings
- Daily stochastic generators understate inter-annual variability ("overdispersion" of observed seasonal totals relative to generated ones); the WGEN/LARS-WG comparison found failures mainly in the inter-annual variability of monthly means. — [Semenov et al. 1998](https://www.int-res.com/articles/cr/10/c010p095.pdf); *(search summary)* [Wilks & Wilby 1999](https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/valerie.monbet/doc/Wilks&Wilby.pdf)
- Project Zomboid varies season severity by year: one year a long harsh winter, another a short mild one. *(search summary)* — [PZ wiki: Fog](https://pzwiki.net/wiki/Fog)
- RimWorld layers rare events on the base climate: cold snaps, heat waves and volcanic winters shift outdoor temperature for a period. — [RimWorld wiki: Temperature](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Temperature)
- Timberborn makes the hazardous season the core loop and escalates it: droughts lengthen each cycle, with a forecast shortly before onset. — [Timberborn wiki: Weather](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Weather)

### Inferences
- **Yearly anomaly**: `yearWet(area, year)` and `yearWarm(area, year)` from value noise over (coarse x, coarse y, year) — spatial scale of a subcontinent, so a drought covers many regions and neighbouring fortresses share it. Apply as a multiplier on π and on mean wet-day amount (±20–30 %) and an offset on temperature (±1 °C). Interpolate across the year boundary (noise is continuous in the year coordinate) so nothing jumps on New Year's Day.
- **Multi-year oscillation**: one or two world-wide indices as smooth noise of time with a 3–7 year scale (an El Niño analogue), each with a stored spatial response pattern from the forge (which coasts get wet, which get dry). Because the index is a function of the tick, sages or almanacs in the game can forecast it honestly.
- **Rare extremes with return periods**: per (year, coarse area) roll against 1/N for an N-year event (a 20-year flood, a 50-year drought, a year without a summer); the event then modifies the functions for that year. Return periods are exact by construction, and history can record them ("the great frost of 312") by evaluating the same rolls for past years — the weather history of the world before the players arrived is free.
- **Volcanic winters tied to history**: eruptions are history events (the forge already stores volcanoes); each contributes a cooling term decaying over 2–3 years, summed over recorded eruptions. These are parameters delivered by the coordinator's history, not rolls — the same mechanism as the "explicit world-level event" in section 6.
- For gameplay, a Timberborn-style forecast (the anomaly for the coming season shown as "the elders expect a dry summer") turns variability from a random punishment into a planning problem.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source was read for realistic magnitudes of inter-annual variability (coefficient of variation of annual rainfall by climate type) or for El Niño periodicity; the ranges above are working values.

## 8. Rendering and UX hooks

### Takeaway
The sim-side function should expose a small, fixed weather sample per climate cell — the client interpolates and embellishes; nothing visual needs to be simulated.

### Cited Findings
- Dwarf Fortress's adventure mode shows three cloud layers (cumulus, cirrus, stratus) with named combinations and three fog levels; fortress mode shows none of it. — [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather)
- Vintage Story drives visuals from pattern data interpolated between regions (cloud types, fog density, mist), switches rain to snow below 4 °C, and adds hail and thunder as temperature- and rainfall-gated events; desert and snow biomes get sandstorms and snowstorms in heavy wind. — [Vintage Story wiki: Weather](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Weather)
- TerraFirmaCraft computes fog from a day roll, rainfall and time of day (night to early morning), and wind from a day-seeded direction plus altitude and rain terms. — [TFC: OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)
- Timberborn shows a forecast of the coming hazard; Vintage Story has a `/whenwillitstopraining` command — possible only because its rain is a function of future time. — [Timberborn wiki: Weather](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Weather), [Vintage Story wiki: Weather](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Weather)

### Inferences
- Suggested `WeatherSample` (per climate cell, per weather step; all integers): temperature (centi-°C), cloud fraction (0–255), precipitation rate (mm/day or 0–255), precipitation type (none / rain / sleet / snow / hail — from temperature bands: snow below about 0–1 °C, sleet 0–2 °C, rain above; hail only from a storm object in warm conditions), wind vector (prevailing seasonal wind from the forge + a noise perturbation + the storm object's circulation), fog flag (high humidity, low wind, night/morning, near water), storm id if inside a storm object, snow depth and ice thickness (derived, section 5), daylight state (sun elevation).
- The client renders from the same function it shares with the server (the function lives in `game`, engine-agnostic), evaluated at the camera's climate cells and bilinearly blended, with its own purely visual embellishment (cloud shapes, rain particles, lightning timing from a client-side hash) that never feeds back into the sim. Lightning *strikes that start fires* are sim events and must come from `roll(tick, column, SALT_LIGHTNING)` inside storm cells.
- A forecast UI is a query of the same function at t + Δ. Honest forecasts are therefore perfect; to make forecasting a skill, degrade them deliberately (add hash noise that grows with lead time, shrinks with the forecaster's skill or instruments).
- Sun position for the sky and shadows comes from the day-length/declination table (section 3); the fortress view may want a fixed pleasant light with only a tint, given the 10-second day.

### Gaps
- Nothing was sourced on how these games render clouds or sky; out of scope for the sim design.

## 9. Performance and level of detail

### Takeaway
Evaluate weather per climate cell and per weather step, never per column or per tick; consumers read a small cached grid. Vintage Story's own code comment gives the reason and the scale: 8 samples per 512-block region, hourly.

### Cited Findings
- Vintage Story samples its snow model at 8 corner points per 512×512-block region once per game hour and interpolates, explicitly to avoid iterating the region's blocks; chunk-level snow updates run on a separate scanner thread and hand "place snow" tasks back to the main thread. — [vsessentialsmod: WeatherSimulationRegion.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationRegion.cs), [WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs)
- Vintage Story's pattern simulation is one state per 512-block region, blended from the 4 nearest. — [Vintage Story wiki: Weather](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Weather)
- Dwarf Fortress advances seasons and weather every 10 ticks, not every tick, and treats per-tile temperature as its slow path; wind and weather are uniform over an embark. RimWorld treats a whole map's outdoors as one temperature. — [DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time), [DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather), [RimWorld wiki: Temperature](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Temperature)

### Inferences
- Cost model per region per weather step: `cells × (2–3 noise fields × 2–3 octaves × 8 corner hashes + table lookups)` ≈ 100–200 integer operations and 50–70 hashes per cell. With a region of 256 m and climate cells of 64–256 m that is 1–16 cells per region; at one weather step per 10 ticks the cost is nanoseconds per tick — negligible beside physics. Even a whole-world overview map (say 1,000 × 1,000 cells) is about 10⁸ operations, a fraction of a second, done on demand by a UI job, not by the sim.
- Corner hashes are shared between adjacent cells and between consecutive steps (the time lattice changes far more slowly than the weather step), so a per-region cache of lattice-corner values, refreshed when the time lattice index advances, removes most of the hashing.
- Level of detail by consumer: (1) climate cell (hundreds of metres to kilometres) for weather fields; (2) column, only for the altitude lapse and slope/shade modifiers on snow and for precipitation type near the snow line; (3) voxel, never. Far LOD rings can show snow cover from the climate-cell value directly (snow line as a function of height within the cell), so distant mountains whiten without loading anything.
- Bounded replay for snow/ice costs 336 daily steps per cell on a cold start (once, on region load) and 1 step per cell per game day after; memoise per (cell, day).
- Storm objects: 9 coarse cells × 2–3 epochs × a handful of candidates = tens of distance tests per evaluation, cacheable per region per epoch (the list of storms that can touch this region during this epoch).
- Keep all caches outside hashed state and rebuildable; the state hash must not depend on whether a cache is warm.

### Gaps
- No measurements exist yet; the operation counts are estimates. The forge's planned climate-cell size for the seasonal overhaul (which sets the cell count per region) was not checked.
