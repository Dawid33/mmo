# Water features and coastal landforms for an offline Earth-like world generator

Reading guide. "Cited Findings" hold only what a fetched source said (the source is linked). "Inferences"
hold my own design proposals and background geomorphology knowledge that I did NOT verify against a source
in this session — treat those as unverified unless stated otherwise. Sources I tried and could not read are
listed under "Gaps" (notably: Azgaar's source code, the Dwarf Fortress wiki, Martin O'Leary's notes, the
Coastal Wiki classification page, the DeltaRCM runtime figures).

The generator this is written for: offline, CPU, minutes; raster grids (fine cells 16–32 m, coarser levels
above); output = heightfield + per-column water-surface height; it already has Priority-Flood, depression-graph
flow routing, rainfall-weighted discharge, stream-power incision, spill-level lakes, river/lake objects,
floodplains, salinity and soil-wetness fields.

## 1. Rivers: network, classification, channel form, special river features

### Takeaway
Keep the existing flow-routing network (it is already the right base); the hydrology-first papers contribute
the *classification and geometry layer* on top: Strahler order + slope → a river type per reach, discharge →
width/depth by power laws, and a vector post-pass (Paris et al. 2023) for meanders, cutoffs/oxbows and
avulsions. Braided/meandering/straight is decided by a slope–discharge threshold (Leopold & Wolman 1957).

### Cited Findings
- Génevaux et al. 2013 (ACM TOG 32(4), art. 143, DOI 10.1145/2461912.2461996) builds the river network FIRST and the terrain second: candidate nodes start at river mouths on the coast contour and the graph grows inland — [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01339224), [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Génevaux node selection: find the lowest candidate elevation z, keep candidates within [z, z+ζ], expand the one with the highest priority (= Horton–Strahler number), ties broken by lowest elevation. ζ≈0 gives many ramifications; larger ζ favours the main rivers — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Génevaux expansion grammar: continuation (probability Pc), symmetric Horton–Strahler junction (Ps: order n → two of n−1), asymmetric junction (Pa: n and m<n); Pc+Pa+Ps=1, user-set — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Génevaux flow: watersheds are unions of Voronoi cells of the river nodes; mean flow from an empirical power law (after Dunne & Leopold 1978) φ = 0.42·A^0.69 (φ in m³/s; the paper text prints A in m², which looks like a units slip — check before using) — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Génevaux river typing: each node gets a Rosgen (1994) class from river slope and proximity to the coast; nodes near the coast (geodesic distance threshold) are labelled braided (Rosgen D/DA); river mouths with flow above a fixed value are marked as deltas; edge type comes from its two nodes, junction type from a look-up table; the paper speaks of nine river categories, each with its own trajectory/cross-section primitive — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Génevaux timing: graph 0.1–5.3 s, watersheds/flows 0.1–1.1 s, construction tree 1.8–4.9 s, 1024² mesh evaluation ≈1 s (C++, 2013 hardware) — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Génevaux stated limitations: only terrains "once subject to hydraulic erosion"; "the river network can subdivide only upstream; thus our algorithm cannot represent deltas or oxbow lakes"; greedy construction can give unnatural crests — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Peytavie et al. 2019, "Procedural Riverscapes" (Computer Graphics Forum 38(7), 35–46, DOI 10.1111/cgf.13814; Peytavie, Dupont, Guérin, Cortial, Benes, Gain, Galin): starts from a bare-earth terrain, derives river paths, carves riverbeds by combining compactly supported elevation modifiers along the course, and represents the water surface as a time-varying function (blend-flow tree). It is a carving + water-surface/animation method, not a network generator — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf13814), [authors' PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Peytavie19CGF.pdf)
- Paris, Guérin, Collon, Galin 2023, "Authoring and Simulating Meandering Rivers" (ACM TOG 42(6), DOI 10.1145/3618350): input is a terrain plus a low-resolution river network as a directed graph; channels are vector paths evolved by a physically based, curvature-driven migration equation with control terms (topography, user constraints); reproduces downstream bend migration, cutoffs that form oxbow lakes, and avulsions; validated on sinuosity and wavelength; "runs at interactive rates"; code is public — [project page](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/paris2023_Meanders.html), [code](https://github.com/aparis69/Meandering-rivers), [HAL preprint](https://hal.science/hal-04227965)
- Hydraulic geometry (Leopold & Maddock 1953, USGS Prof. Paper 252): w = aQ^b, d = cQ^f, v = kQ^m; average downstream exponents b = 0.5, f = 0.4, m = 0.1 (reported via search summary of these sources, not read in full) — [USGS PP 252](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp252), [HESS 2014 review](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/18/4883/2014/hess-18-4883-2014.pdf)
- Braided vs single-thread threshold (Leopold & Wolman 1957, USGS PP 282-B): a critical slope that falls with discharge, quoted in a later review as Sc = 0.06·Q^−0.44 with Q in ft³/s; "at a given discharge, meanders occur at smaller values of slope than do braids" — [USGS PP 282-B](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp282B), [review preprint (esurf-2016-47)](https://esurf.copernicus.org/preprints/esurf-2016-47/esurf-2016-47.pdf)
- Braiding controls beyond slope: critical slope falls as discharge rises and rises with grain size; erodible banks → wide shallow braided channels, cohesive banks → narrow deep meandering ones; more suspended load relative to bedload shifts braided → meandering; anastomosing rivers have multiple SEMI-PERMANENT channels separated by floodplain (not temporary bars) — [Wikipedia: Braided river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braided_river)
- Red Blob Games mapgen4: rainfall is computed everywhere and only the larger rivers are drawn; rivers are binary trees on a dual mesh; Strahler and Shreve numbers are the stated ingredients (Shreve tracks flow) — [rivers page](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/rivers/), [Elevation from Rivers](https://www.redblobgames.com/x/1724-elevation-from-rivers), [mapgen4 river representation](https://simblob.blogspot.com/2018/10/mapgen4-river-representation.html)
- Waterfalls at hanging valleys: tributary glacial valleys sit above the main valley and "waterfalls often form where they meet" (Muldalsfossen drops several hundred metres into Tafjorden) — [Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)

### Inferences
(Own conversions and design proposals; background knowledge where marked "bg" is unverified here.)
- Unit conversion of the Leopold–Wolman line to SI: 0.06·(35.31·Q)^−0.44 ≈ 0.0125·Q^−0.44 with Q in m³/s (my arithmetic). This matches the commonly quoted "S ≈ 0.012–0.013·Q^−0.44" (bg). Q is bankfull discharge, not the mean.
- Proposed per-reach classifier on the existing river objects (reach = segment between confluences, 0.5–2 km long):
  1. bankfull Q from the mean discharge (bg: bankfull ≈ a 1.5–2-year flood; a constant multiplier of mean flow per climate is a cheap stand-in);
  2. valley slope S from the filled DEM over the reach, valley-floor width W_v from the floodplain mask;
  3. straight/confined if W_v < ~2–3 channel widths (gorge, canyon — no room to migrate);
  4. braided if S > 0.0125·Q^−0.44 AND high bedload supply (glacier-fed, arid mountain front, steep source area);
  5. anastomosing if very low slope + cohesive banks + rising base level (inland deltas, wet lowlands);
  6. otherwise meandering; run a Paris-style vector migration pass only on meandering reaches.
- Width/depth: w = a·Q^0.5, d = c·Q^0.4 (cited exponents); coefficients a, c are tunables (bg: a ≈ 3–5 for Q in m³/s gives realistic widths; braided reaches get a larger width multiplier and smaller depth). Because the output is a per-column water surface, a river of width < 1 cell must stay a vector object with a width attribute (drawn/meshed later), exactly as mapgen4 keeps rivers on mesh edges.
- Oxbow lakes: emitted by the meander pass at each neck cutoff — store as a small closed lake polygon at floodplain elevation minus channel depth; they need cells ≤ channel width (a 100 m river → representable at 16–32 m cells; small rivers' oxbows are sub-cell and should be dropped or stored as point features).
- Waterfalls/rapids detection on the existing data: walk each river long-profile and flag knickpoints where drop over a short window exceeds a threshold (e.g. ≥5 m over ≤2 cells = waterfall; slope > 2–4% over ≥100 m = rapids/cataract). Sources of knickpoints the generator can create on purpose: (a) a hard-rock band crossing a river in the stream-power pass (erodibility K contrast — the incision already produces a step if K varies by lithology), (b) hanging valleys after a glacial pass (cited above), (c) a fault scarp or plateau edge (escarpment falls), (d) sea-cliff river mouths. Store: position, height, river id, discharge class → name ("X Falls").
- Gorges/canyons: reaches where incision depth below the surrounding 1–2 km mean surface exceeds a threshold and valley width/depth ratio is small; arises where a river with high discharge crosses uplifting/high ground (antecedent or superimposed rivers) or arid plateau (little hillslope retreat). Detection: relief within k cells of the channel vs valley width.
- Alluvial fans: where a channel leaves a confined mountain valley onto a plain with an abrupt slope drop (slope ratio upstream/downstream > ~3 and loss of confinement). Generate as a cone of deposition (radius ∝ (drainage area)^~0.5 (bg), surface slope 1–5°), with distributary channels; in arid climates coalescing fans form bajadas, in wet climates megafans (bg). A fan entering standing water is a fan delta (cited under deltas).
- Seasonal/ephemeral rivers (wadis, arroyos): classify each reach by flow regime using monthly climate: perennial if the driest-month discharge > 0 after losses, intermittent if some months dry, ephemeral if flow only after storms (arid, annual P ≪ PET). Add a transmission-loss term along arid reaches (discharge decreases downstream) so rivers can END in the desert (cited: none — bg). Store a `regime` enum and min/max monthly discharge per reach; the water-surface height then needs a seasonal variant (or a dry bed with a seasonal flag).
- Endorheic rivers: the depression graph already knows basins with no outlet to the sea; with water-balance lake levels (section 2) these end in salt lakes or playas instead of spilling.
- Karst/underground rivers: in carbonate lithology with enough rainfall, let a stream sink (ponor) where it enters the limestone and re-emerge at a spring (resurgence) at the lithology boundary or valley floor downstream; route discharge through a straight subterranean link in the flow graph. Surface signature: blind valleys, dry valleys, dolines, poljes (bg). The flow-routing graph just needs an "underground edge" type.
- River freezing: a per-reach ice-season length from monthly temperature (months with mean T < 0 °C, lagged for large rivers) (bg). Rivers flowing poleward (Ob, Lena, Mackenzie) flood on break-up because the upstream thaws first (bg) — a flavour attribute, not geometry.
- Rosgen in practice: the full Rosgen scheme (A, B, C, D, DA, E, F, G + Aa+) keys on entrenchment ratio, width/depth ratio, sinuosity, slope and bed material (bg). For a generator, the reduced set used by Génevaux (slope + coast proximity → trajectory/cross-section template) is enough; map: steep headwater (A: >4% step-pool/cascade), moderate (B: 2–4% riffle-dominated), low-gradient meandering with floodplain (C/E: <2%), braided (D), anastomosing (DA), entrenched gullies/gorges (F/G). The slope breakpoints are standard Rosgen values quoted from memory (bg) — verify against Rosgen 1994 before hard-coding.

## 2. Lakes by origin, and lake level from a water balance

### Takeaway
Lake ORIGIN is a property of what made the basin (tectonic, glacial, volcanic, fluvial, karst, coastal,
permafrost), so each origin should be produced by the terrain pass that creates the basin and tagged then;
lake LEVEL should come from a water balance — a closed lake's area grows until evaporation from its surface
equals inflow — and only lakes whose balance reaches the spill point become open, fresh, overflowing lakes.

### Cited Findings
- Closed lakes (no surface or subsurface outlet) "lose water only through evaporation" — [Britannica: hydrologic balance of lakes](https://www.britannica.com/science/lake/The-hydrologic-balance-of-the-lakes)
- Langbein (1961), as cited by Huybers, Rupper & Roe: closed-basin lakes fluctuate more than open lakes because variations in inflow "can only be compensated by a change in the lake's surface area"; small imbalances drive large area changes — [Huybers, Rupper & Roe 2015](https://earthweb.ess.washington.edu/roe/GerardWeb/Publications_files/HuybersRupperRoe_Lakes_ClimDyn15.pdf)
- A steady-state regional model exists that predicts the lake-to-basin area ratio from an aridity index (PET/P), with a Budyko-type relation for basin runoff (Bracht-Flyr, Istanbulluoglu & Fritz 2013) — [UNL digital commons](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/geosciencefacpub/377)
- Kettle lakes: buried blocks of glacier ice melt and leave depressions in outwash plains/till; most kettles < 2 km across and < 10 m deep (some lakes to 61 m); kettle lakes are stream-fed, kettle ponds are precipitation/groundwater-fed and may dry in summer; acidic ones become kettle bogs; "kame and kettle topography"; Prairie Pothole Region — [Wikipedia: Kettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_(landform))
- Fjord lakes: freshwater lakes in glacially carved valleys "often behind thresholds, moraines, or deltas"; Hornindalsvatnet is at least 500 m deep; glacial valleys are overdeepened and end at a threshold — [Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)
- Thermokarst (thaw) lakes: usually shallow freshwater lakes in depressions left by thawing ice-rich permafrost (excess ground ice > 30% by volume), in flat Arctic/subarctic lowlands (Alaska coastal plain, Banks/Victoria Islands, Yukon, northern Siberia); some are "oriented lakes" elongated in one direction (elliptical, D-shaped…), possibly wind-related; a deep enough lake has a talik beneath; collapsed pingos leave depressions — [Wikipedia: Thermokarst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermokarst)
- Oxbow lakes arise from meander cutoffs (Paris et al. model) — [Paris 2023 project page](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/paris2023_Meanders.html)
- Azgaar's generator, per its wiki mirror: "lakes form in depressions", with a step to "define lake groups" (the group rules themselves were not retrievable) — [wiki mirror: Generation Process](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/src/commit/05c53d276aaa1153f8d61c6ab515f79fccd6744f/wiki/Generation-Process.md)
- Undiscovered Worlds acknowledges that "lakes occasionally go haywire" in its generator (spill-level lake filling is fragile in practice) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/fegennari/Undiscovered_Worlds)

### Inferences
- Water-balance lake level (standard steady state; equation is my formulation, consistent with the cited Langbein/Britannica logic but not quoted from a source). For each depression in the existing depression graph, with hypsometry A(h) (lake area at level h, from the DEM — a sorted histogram of cell heights in the basin):
  - Q_in = inflow from the catchment (already have: rainfall-weighted discharge, incl. overflow of upstream lakes), E = open-water evaporation rate, P = precipitation on the lake, G = net groundwater loss (optional).
  - Equilibrium area A* = Q_in / (E − P) when E > P. Find h* with A(h*) = A*.
  - If E ≤ P, or h* ≥ spill level → OPEN lake: level = spill level, outflow = Q_in − (E − P)·A(spill); fresh.
  - If h* < spill level → CLOSED (endorheic) lake at h*: no outflow, salinity accumulates → salt lake; if A* is tiny or the mean depth at h* is under ~0.5–1 m → playa / salt pan (seasonally flooded; store as dry bed with evaporite surface + seasonal-water flag).
  - Process basins in topological order of the depression graph (upstream first), because a closed upstream lake removes its inflow from everything downstream. One pass suffices if closed lakes pass nothing on; iterate when a lake switches open/closed.
  - Seasonal amplitude: run the balance with wet-season and dry-season Q_in and E to get a level range (shoreline band = mudflat/salt crust).
  - Cost: O(cells log cells) for the hypsometry sorts, done per basin — negligible next to erosion.
- This also fixes "too many lakes": with the balance, most arid-zone depressions become dry basins or playas instead of full lakes, and humid-zone ones stay full (Earth pattern: lake density peaks in formerly glaciated humid shields — bg).
- Catalogue of lake origins → generator hook → detection/tagging (formation notes are background knowledge unless cited above):

- Classifier order for an already generated lake (when origin was not recorded at creation): coastal (touches the sea-level band, behind a barrier) → volcanic (inside a volcano footprint) → karst (carbonate, no outlet) → glacial family (inside ice extent; split by size/elongation/altitude) → fluvial (on a floodplain) → tectonic (on a rift, deep, elongated) → endorheic salt lake/playa (closed by balance) → generic.

## 3. River mouths: deltas, estuaries, rias, fjords, tidal flats

### Takeaway
A river mouth is a delta when the river's sediment wins against waves and tides, and an estuary/ria/fjord
when the sea has drowned the valley faster than sediment refills it. Decide per mouth from four numbers the
generator can estimate — sediment flux, wave energy, tidal range, receiving-basin slope/depth — and then
build the delta with a cheap rule-based distributary model (Undiscovered Worlds' backward-traced branches,
or a small DeltaRCM-style parcel walk on a local window).

### Cited Findings
- Deltas form where a sediment-laden river enters standing/slow water; they are classified by the dominant control — river, wave or tide (Galloway 1975): river-dominated where tidal range and wave energy are low (buoyancy-dominated outflow like the Mississippi gives long distributaries with few bifurcations, "bird's-foot"); wave-dominated deltas are smoothed, sediment deflected alongshore (high nearshore wave energy, steep offshore slope); tide-dominated ones are dendritic with many sandbars and ridges — [Wikipedia: River delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)
- "Rivers on coasts with significant tidal range generally do not build a delta" — they enter the sea as an estuary (examples given: Gulf of Saint Lawrence, Tagus) — [Wikipedia: River delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)
- Avulsion: a river breaches its levees (often in flood) and takes a shorter, steeper route; repeated switching builds the distributary network; rarely avulsing rivers make bird's-foot deltas, frequently avulsing (fan) deltas approximate an ideal fan. The Mississippi had seven distinct channels/lobes in ~5,000 years — [Wikipedia: River delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)
- Gilbert deltas: coarse sediment into (usually) a freshwater lake; topset / steep foreset / bottomset beds — [Wikipedia: River delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)
- DeltaRCM (Liang, Voller & Paola 2015, Earth Surf. Dynam. 3, 67–86): a rule-based cellular morphodynamic model — stochastic parcel routing of water and sediment plus phenomenological deposition/erosion rules; resolves channel "elongation, bifurcation, avulsion and migration", produces fan deltas and deltas with multiple orders of bifurcation, and records simple stratigraphy (coarse/fine, deposit age) — [ESurf abstract](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/3/67/2015/)
- DeltaRCM mechanics (read from the paper PDF): each time step routes n_w = 2000 water parcels then n_s = 2000 sediment parcels (sand and mud fractions) by weighted random walk over the 8 neighbours; water routing weight w_i = h_i^θ · max(0, F·d_i) / Δ_i with θ = 1 (h = neighbour water depth, F = local routing direction combining inertia and water-surface gradient, d_i = unit direction, Δ_i = 1 or √2); four phases per step: water routing → water-surface update (backwater) → sediment routing with deposition/erosion → routing-direction update; cell size 50 m for a field-scale delta; grid on the order of a hundred cells per side — [ESurf paper PDF](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/3/67/2015/esurf-3-67-2015.pdf)
- Undiscovered Worlds, "Delta blues": standard flow models cannot split flow, so deltas are a separate layer. Delta likelihood per river mouth depends on the mouth's tidal range, river size and terrain steepness. Branch mouths are placed in the sea along a line roughly parallel to the coast; an "origin point" a few cells upstream is chosen; each branch is traced FROM its mouth TOWARD the origin as if it were a river source (paths may deviate from strict downhill); branches that meet merge; each branch carries river flow / number of branches; land around is flattened to just above sea level; stored in a separate delta river array with directions reversed, planned at global scale and refined on regional maps — [Undiscovered Worlds, June 2019](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2019/06)
- Undiscovered Worlds tidal inlets and flats: inlets are cut at river mouths depending on river size and tidal range; a temporary "silt" field is seeded on coastal cells in inlets (level from river size and tidal strength), diffused inland and seaward, and cells above a threshold become mud flats (set to 1 m above sea level; saltwater wetlands nearby). The same method gives sand/shingle beaches with tidal range weighted over river size (mud near big rivers, sand/shingle near small ones). Mud flats can spawn barrier islands on their seaward edge (Wadden Sea analogue); in the tropics mud flats and salt wetlands get mangroves. Tide strength is a planetary "lunar gravity" variable — [Undiscovered Worlds, Feb 2023](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2023/02)
- Génevaux et al. mark river mouths with flow above a fixed value as deltas, and note that large river mouths and deltas are frequently found in concave parts of the coast — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Estuaries/rias: most present-day estuaries sit in fluvial valleys incised during the last glacial and flooded by post-glacial sea-level rise; rias are drowned river valleys, fjords are drowned valleys carved by land ice — [Hijma thesis record](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/37147), [Bosboom & Stive, Coastal Dynamics 2.5.2](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Oceanography/Coastal_Dynamics_(Bosboom_and_Stive)/02%3A_Large-scale_geographical_variation_of_coasts/2.05%3A_(Holocene)_transgression_versus_regression/2.5.2%3A_Role_of_sea-level_rise_in_Holocene_coastal_evolution)
- Fjords: glacier-carved U-shaped valleys, overdeepened, ending at a rock sill/threshold (reduced erosion + terminal moraine) at the mouth, then flooded by the sea; mainly high and upper-middle latitudes up to ~80°N on mountainous coasts facing moist westerlies (W Norway, W North America, SW New Zealand, S Chile, Greenland, Antarctica); most are deeper than the adjacent sea (Sognefjord ≈ 1,300 m deep, 226 km long; longest 420 km); skerry belts (thousands of rocky islets) at the outer coast; underwater hanging valleys (Fjærlandsfjord ~400 m vs 1,200 m main fjord). Rias lack the sill and overdeepened basin — [Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)
- Norway's coast is ~29,000 km with fjords but ~2,500 km without — a measure of how much a fjord pass changes coastline length — [Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)
- Mogensen & Isenbecker 2025, "Generation of Zoomable Maps with Rivers and Fjords" (arXiv:2504.08383): recursive triangle subdivision giving unlimited zoom without a full high-resolution map; the abstract does not explain the fjord rule — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08383)

### Inferences
- Per-mouth decision (proposal). Compute at each river mouth: Qs = sediment flux (the stream-power pass already knows eroded volume per catchment; else Qs ∝ Q·relief), W = wave energy at the mouth (fetch-based, section 5), T = tidal range (section 5), and the receiving-basin depth/slope within a few km.
  - Delta if Qs is large relative to (W + T) and the offshore is shallow (wide shelf, lake, or enclosed sea). Type on the Galloway triangle by the largest of normalised (Qs, W, T): river-dominated → elongate bird's-foot, few long distributaries, natural levees, interdistributary bays; wave-dominated → arcuate/cuspate, smooth shoreline, beach ridges, one or two mouths (Nile, São Francisco — bg); tide-dominated → funnel-shaped distributaries widening seaward, elongate tidal bars, mangrove/mudflat fringe (Ganges–Brahmaputra, Fly — bg).
  - Estuary if T is meso/macro-tidal or the valley is drowned (section 5) and Qs is small: funnel-shaped mouth, width decaying roughly exponentially inland (bg), tidal flats and salt marsh on the margins.
  - Ria if the coast is unglaciated, hilly, and sea level has risen: branching drowned valley, no sill, depth increasing seaward.
  - Fjord if the valley was glaciated to below sea level: U-profile, overdeepened basin, sill at mouth.
  - Steep collision coasts with a deep nearshore and narrow shelf: no delta (sediment goes down submarine canyons — bg); small fan deltas only.
  - Lake deltas (Gilbert type) at every sizeable river entering a lake: cheap and very visible in a fortress-scale game.
- Delta construction, two tiers:
  1. Cheap (Undiscovered Worlds style, verified above): choose apex a few km upstream where valley confinement ends; choose N mouths (N grows with Q and falls with wave energy) along the shore; trace branches from mouths back to the apex with a noisy least-cost path; split discharge; flatten the delta plain to 0–2 m above sea level; prograde the shoreline seaward by a lobe polygon (area ∝ Qs × time / basin depth). The flow graph needs "distributary" edges (a DAG rather than a tree) — the existing "rivers as objects" store must allow a node with two downstream edges.
  2. Simulated (DeltaRCM style): run the parcel random walk on a local window (e.g. 256² cells at 32–50 m) per major delta for a few hundred to a few thousand steps; avulsion, bifurcation, levees and abandoned lobes emerge. Only worth it for the handful of largest rivers; add a wave-smoothing step (diffuse shoreline sediment alongshore) to get wave-dominated shapes, since base DeltaRCM is river-dominated (bg).
- Fjord generation without a full ice-flow model: (a) take the pre-glacial fluvial valley network in the zone that was glaciated and mountainous near the coast; (b) widen each trunk valley to a parabolic/U cross-section with width ∝ (ice drainage area)^~0.4 and deepen ∝ ice flux, allowing the bed to go below sea level (overdeepening), ending with a sill at the former coast/ice-shelf grounding position; (c) tributaries get less deepening → hanging valleys (waterfalls); (d) flood by sea level. Add skerries: low-amplitude knobby relief in a strip at the outer coast (strandflat — bg).
- Detection on an existing land/sea mask + bathymetry: for each river mouth, walk seaward along the thalweg of the drowned valley; classify the inlet by (length/width ratio, max depth, presence of a shallower sill near the mouth, glaciated flag, branching): fjord = long (L/W > ~5), deep, sill present, glaciated; ria = branching (dendritic like the river network), depth increases seaward, unglaciated; estuary = funnel plan shape with tidal influence, shallow; delta = shoreline protrudes seaward of the regional trend at the mouth (convex bulge) with ≥2 distributaries. Fjard (bg): drowned low-relief glaciated rock coast — shallow, irregular, many islets.
- Tidal flats: cells between low-water and high-water elevation (sea level ± T/2) on sheltered, low-slope coasts; since water is a per-column surface height, tidal flats are best stored as a material/biome band with a "tidal" flag and the tidal range per coast segment, rather than animated water.

## 4. Coasts: classification, named features and how to detect them, constructional and erosional landforms, reefs

### Takeaway
First classify each ~10–100 km coast segment by tectonic setting, sea-level history, wave energy, tidal range
and sediment supply; that class then selects which landforms to stamp (barriers/lagoons, cliffs, reefs,
mangroves, deltas). Named features (bay, gulf, cape, peninsula, strait, isthmus, archipelago) are not
generated — they are DETECTED on the land/sea mask by geometric tests, of which the UNCLOS semicircle rule
is the one with a crisp definition.

### Cited Findings
- Tectonic classification (Inman & Nordstrom 1971): first-order division at ~1000 km alongshore scale into collision, trailing-edge and marginal-sea coasts; trailing-edge coasts (e.g. US East Coast) have flat topography, lagoons and wide continental shelves; marginal-sea coasts have the greatest diversity of form — [Bosboom & Stive, Coastal Dynamics 2.3.2](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Oceanography/Coastal_Dynamics_(Bosboom_and_Stive)/02%3A_Large-scale_geographical_variation_of_coasts/2.03%3A_Tectonic_control_of_coasts/2.3.2%3A_Tectonic_plate_setting_of_coasts), [summary 2.3.4](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Oceanography/Coastal_Dynamics_(Bosboom_and_Stive)/02%3A_Large-scale_geographical_variation_of_coasts/2.03%3A_Tectonic_control_of_coasts/2.3.4%3A_Summary_of_the_tectonic_classification)
- Shepard's genetic classification: primary coasts shaped mainly by non-marine processes (drowned river valleys, rocky and deltaic coasts), secondary coasts shaped mainly by marine processes or organisms (barrier coasts, coral reefs, mangroves). Sources disagree slightly on wording/attribution of the scheme — [UNCW coastal classification notes](https://people.uncw.edu/hosier/cmgmt/coastal_classification.htm), [Perlego summary](https://perlego.com/index/geography/classification-of-coasts)
- Bay — legal/geometric definition (UNCLOS Art. 10): a "well-marked indentation" enclosing landlocked waters, "more than a mere curvature of the coast"; it counts as a bay only if its water area is at least that of a semicircle whose diameter is the line across its mouth; area is measured between the low-water mark and the line joining the natural entrance points; with several mouths (islands in the entrance) the diameter is the SUM of the mouth lines; islands inside count as water area; closing line up to 24 nautical miles — [UNCLOS Part II](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm)
- Dragons Abound does not create bays; it identifies them by searching the coastline for bay shapes. For each detected bay it knows start/end points, the coast between them, centre, sinuosity and area. Naming: compass position by map thirds; "Great/Little" by area; size-class synonyms with real-world frequency weights (small: Cove, Inlet, Hole…; medium: Bay, Anchorage, Sound…; large: Gulf, Basin, Reach…); climate adjectives; a city on the bay ("X Harbor"); or the largest river flowing in — [Dragons Abound: Bay Watch](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/07/the-naming-of-places-part-6-bay-watch.html)
- Dragons Abound barrier islands: placed only on FLAT stretches of coast (low sinuosity — high-sinuosity spots are bays and are avoided), not on short coastlines (small islands), not overlapping other barriers or any coast; outline is offset from a SMOOTHED copy of the coast, clipped for self-intersection, capped with arcs; seaward side smooth, landward side noisy; inlets (breaks) not yet implemented; an offset island that overlaps the coast is in reality a spit — [Dragons Abound: Barrier Islands Revisited](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/12/barrier-islands-revisited.html?m=0)
- Barrier islands — conditions: wave-dominated coasts with small to moderate tidal range: microtidal (0–2 m) → long, nearly continuous islands; mesotidal (2–4 m) → shorter islands with frequent tidal inlets, "drumstick" shapes in mixed-energy settings (large tidal prism, ebb shoals); macrotidal (> 4 m) → very rare. Need a low-gradient shelf, ample sediment supply (fluvial or glacial), relatively stable sea level; favour tectonically stable trailing-edge coasts and small marine basins; absent on the US Pacific coast (rocky, narrow shelf). ~1,500 barrier islands line roughly 13–15% of the world's coasts; longest is Padre Island, 182 km. Formation theories: offshore bar emergence, spit breaching, submergence of coastal ridges — no single one explains all — [Wikipedia: Barrier island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island)
- Lagoons behind barriers connect to the ocean through inlets between barrier islands (e.g. the Outer Banks "sounds") — [Sumner, Barrier Islands and Lagoons (LibreTexts)](https://geo.libretexts.org/Courses/University_of_California_Davis/GEL_109%3A_Sediments_and_Strata_(Sumner)/Textbook_Construction/Barrier_Islands_and_Lagoons)
- Coral reefs — conditions: optimum 26–27 °C, few reefs below 18 °C; roughly 30°N–30°S; photic zone, mostly < 50 m depth (reef builders above ~70 m); clear water — corals weaken where loose sediment accumulates; absent near large river outflows (Amazon, Ganges); rare on the west coasts of the Americas and Africa because of upwelling and cold currents (Humboldt, Benguela, Canary) — [Wikipedia: Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef)
- Reef types and sizes: fringing (attached or behind a narrow shallow lagoon; usually < 100 m wide), barrier (lagoon several km wide, 30–70 m deep), platform (few hundred m to many km across), patch (tens of metres inside lagoons), atoll (ring reef around a lagoon, no central island). Zones: fore reef, reef crest, back reef/lagoon, reef flat. Vertical growth 1–25 cm/yr; reefs cannot grow above sea level — [Wikipedia: Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef)
- Darwin's subsidence sequence: extinct volcanic island with fringing reef → island subsides, reef grows up → barrier reef with wider, deeper lagoon → island disappears → atoll; fringing reefs ~10,000 years, atolls up to 30 million years — [Wikipedia: Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef)
- Mangroves: tropical and subtropical coasts mainly between 30°N and 30°S (most area within 5° of the equator); upper half of the intertidal zone; saline/brackish shores, estuaries, tidal rivers; fine organic-rich sediment in sites protected from high wave energy; scrubby at the poleward limit — [Wikipedia: Mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove)
- Azgaar's generator groups water and land into typed features (per its wiki mirror: lakes in depressions, lake groups) — [wiki mirror](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/src/commit/05c53d276aaa1153f8d61c6ab515f79fccd6744f/wiki/Generation-Process.md)
- Undiscovered Worlds: beaches (sand/shingle) and mud flats from a diffused coastal sediment field; barrier islands on the seaward edge of mud flats; crinkly small islands kept in arctic and rough areas (fjord-like coasts) and reduced elsewhere — [Undiscovered Worlds, Feb 2023](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2023/02)

### Inferences
- Coast-segment classifier (proposal; combines the cited schemes with background knowledge). Walk the coastline at the coarse grid, cut into segments of ~20–50 km, and compute per segment:
  - tectonic class from the plate model: collision (convergent margin: narrow shelf, steep relief, cliffs, raised terraces, few barriers, small deltas), trailing-edge (passive margin: wide shelf, coastal plain, barriers/lagoons, big deltas), marginal sea (behind island arcs: sheltered, low wave energy, big deltas);
  - relative sea-level trend: submergent (drowned valleys → rias, fjords, estuaries, drowned shelf islands) vs emergent (raised beaches, marine terraces, straight coast; post-glacial rebound areas, uplifting collision coasts);
  - wave exposure (fetch + wind, section 5), tidal range class (micro/meso/macro), sediment supply (river Qs within the littoral cell + glacial deposits + soft cliffs), nearshore slope, winter SST, lithology hardness, glaciated flag.
  - Then: hard rock + high wave energy + steep → cliffed coast (sea cliffs, wave-cut platform, stacks, arches, sea caves, pocket beaches in coves); soft rock + waves → retreating low cliffs with wide beaches; low gradient + sediment + wave-dominated + micro/meso-tidal → barrier islands, lagoons, spits, dunes; low gradient + macro-tidal or sheltered + mud → tidal flats with salt marsh (temperate) or mangrove (tropical, cited limits); warm clear shallow water away from river mouths → reefs; glaciated mountains → fjords/skerries; glaciated lowland → fjards, drumlin islands, till cliffs; arid low coast → sabkha (bg).
- Feature detection on the land/sea mask (bays etc.). Suggested pipeline, multi-scale because the same test at different scales yields cove / bay / gulf:
  1. Flood-fill connected components of water and land (ocean vs inland sea vs lake; continent vs island). Area thresholds give continent / island / islet and ocean / sea. An "archipelago" = a cluster of islands by single-linkage with gap < k × typical island size (DBSCAN on island centroids or on a dilated mask).
  2. Morphological CLOSING of land with a disk of radius r (or equivalently: closed(land) − land) yields the water that sits in concavities narrower than 2r: candidate bays, inlets, fjords, straits. Do it for r in a geometric ladder (e.g. 0.5, 2, 8, 32, 128 km) → cove, bay, gulf hierarchy; nested results form a tree (a cove inside a bay inside a gulf).
  3. For each candidate component, its mouth = boundary shared with the remaining open sea. Apply the semicircle rule (cited): keep it if water area ≥ π·(mouth length)²/8; if two or more mouths → it is a STRAIT/sound/channel (connects two water bodies), not a bay. Shape descriptors: elongation (length/mouth width) → inlet/firth/fjord/ria when > ~3; near-circular with a narrow mouth (mouth ≪ √area) → harbour/lagoon-like bay; huge with a narrow mouth → gulf or (if nearly enclosed) inland sea.
  4. Morphological OPENING of land (land − opened(land)) yields land narrower than 2r: peninsulas, capes, spits, isthmuses. A component attached to the main land along ONE neck = peninsula (large) / cape, headland, point (small; pick the most seaward-convex vertex as the cape point); attached along TWO necks, i.e. it connects two larger land masses = isthmus. The dual of the strait.
  5. Alternative/complement: convex-hull deficiency of each landmass (hull − land = bays; depth of each deficiency component gives the size) and coastline curvature at several smoothing scales (strong convex extrema = capes, concave = bay heads). Medial axis / skeleton of the sea near land gives strait centre-lines and their minimum width (the narrowest point names the strait); the skeleton of land gives peninsula axes for label placement.
  6. Sounds: water between a mainland and an offshore island/barrier chain, open at both ends. Lagoons: sea-level water with a mouth much narrower than the body and a barrier of sand material. Fjord/ria/estuary: per section 3.
  - All of these are O(N) or O(N log N) raster operations (distance transforms give the disk closing/opening exactly: closing by r = threshold of the distance-to-land transform followed by a distance transform back). On a planetary coarse grid (km cells) this is seconds.
- Barrier island + lagoon generation (proposal consistent with the cited conditions and with Dragons Abound's geometry): on eligible segments (wave-dominated, tidal range < 4 m, nearshore slope below a threshold ~1:1000 (bg), sediment available, not inside a narrow bay) (a) smooth the shoreline at a ~5–10 km scale, (b) offset seaward by a lagoon width (0.5–10 km, wider on flatter coasts), (c) lay a sand ridge 200–2000 m wide and 2–8 m high with dunes on top (bg dimensions), (d) cut inlets with spacing inversely related to tidal range (micro-tidal: tens of km apart; meso-tidal: every 5–15 km with drumstick-shaped islands — the tidal-range relation is cited, the spacings are bg), with an ebb-tidal shoal seaward and flood delta landward, (e) set the lagoon to sea level with 1–3 m depth, marsh or mangrove on its landward edge, (f) salinity of the lagoon from river inflow vs inlet exchange (brackish → hypersaline in arid climates). Where the offset ridge touches a headland instead of floating free → spit (attached at one end, recurved tip, grows down-drift) or baymouth bar (closes a bay → coastal lake/liman); a ridge linking an island to the mainland → tombolo (island within a few island-widths of the shore, wave shadow). These need 16–32 m cells: assign to the fine grid, with only an eligibility flag on the coarse grid.
- Beaches and dunes: beach where sediment exists and slope is low (width 20–200 m), grain size by energy (sand on exposed, shingle on high-energy sediment-starved, mud on sheltered — matching the cited Undiscovered Worlds rule). Coastal dunes behind beaches where onshore wind + sand supply + sparse vegetation (arid or temperate windy coasts): foredune ridge 5–30 m, parabolic/transverse dunes inland (bg).
- Cliffs, platforms, stacks, arches: on hard-rock exposed segments, cut the land at the shoreline to a cliff of height = the land height at a retreat distance d (d ∝ wave energy × erodibility × time), leaving a wave-cut platform at −0…−10 m sloping ~1° seaward (bg); with rock-hardness noise the retreat is uneven → headlands (hard) and coves (soft); isolated remnants seaward of headlands = stacks (single cells/columns at 16 m resolution — representable as 1–3 cell pillars), arches need voxel-level authored prefabs since a heightfield cannot overhang. Emergent coasts get a staircase of old platforms (marine terraces).
- Reef generation (from the cited conditions): eligibility = winter SST ≥ 18 °C, depth < ~50 m, not within the turbid plume of a large river (distance scaled by Qs), not on upwelling west coasts (cold-current flag from the climate model). Fringing reef: a 50–500 m wide reef flat at ~−1 m hugging the shore; barrier reef: a ridge at ~−1 m, 1–20 km offshore at the shelf break or along a drowned former shoreline, lagoon 10–70 m deep behind, passes opposite river mouths (corals avoid fresh/turbid water — bg); atoll: on volcanic islands in the eligible zone, use island AGE from the hotspot track: young = high island + fringing reef, middle = lowered island + barrier reef + lagoon, old = no island, ring reef 2–50 km across with low sand islets (motu) on the windward rim and a 20–60 m deep lagoon (dimensions bg); beyond the reef-growth limit ("Darwin point", bg) the atoll drowns into a guyot. Since reefs cannot grow above sea level (cited), reef cells sit at 0 to −2 m and islets are storm-piled sand/rubble 1–4 m high.
- Kelp coasts (bg, unverified): cold, nutrient-rich water (roughly SST < 20 °C), rocky substrate, depth 2–30 m, exposed — the cool-water counterpart to reefs on upwelling west coasts; a biome tag on rocky subtidal cells.
- Continental margin profile (bg, unverified numbers): shelf from 0 to ~−130 m (shelf break; consistent with LGM lowstand), width ~10 km (collision) to 100s km (trailing-edge), mean slope ~0.1°; continental slope ~3–4° down to ~−3000 m; rise; abyssal plain ~−4000 to −5000 m; trenches at subduction zones. Submarine canyons incise the shelf edge, often continuing large rivers (the river valley cut at lowstand) — generate by continuing the flow-routing of big rivers across the shelf at the lowstand sea level, then incising on the slope.

## 5. Sea level, tides, waves

### Takeaway
Yes — generating fluvial/glacial terrain against a LOWER sea level (≈ −120 m) and then raising the sea to 0
is the single cheapest trick for realistic coasts: it yields rias, estuaries, fjords, drowned shelves, shelf
islands and submarine canyons for free. Tides and waves need only scalar fields per coast segment: tidal
range from basin size/funnel geometry, wave energy from fetch × wind.

### Cited Findings
- At the Last Glacial Maximum (~26–20 ka; USGS figure ~21 ka) sea level was about 125 m lower than today (other passages: "more than 110 m"); shelves were exposed and landmasses joined: Sundaland (Borneo, Bali joined to Asia), Sahul (Australia + New Guinea + Tasmania), New Zealand's main islands joined, Doggerland; ice covered ~25% of land area (Laurentide to the Missouri/Ohio rivers and Manhattan, ~3.2 km thick; northern Europe to Germany/Poland; Patagonia) — [Wikipedia: Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum)
- The Persian Gulf floor was largely dry with a river through the Strait of Hormuz, flooded ~8,000 years ago — [Wikipedia: Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum)
- Most present estuaries occupy valleys incised during the glacial lowstand and flooded by post-glacial rise; this transition is typical of wide, low-gradient shelves — [Hijma thesis record](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/37147)
- Drowning can be fast: the Paleo-Ems valley on the North Sea shelf submerged within ~200 years because the river could not adjust its gradient to the rising sea (Hepp et al. 2019) — [J. Quaternary Science record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jqs.3147)
- Tidal classes by mean range: micro-tidal < 2 m, meso-tidal 2–4 m, macro-tidal > 4 m (hypertidal > 6 m); typical open-ocean range ≈ 1 m; range depends on the adjacent water volume and basin geography, which "can act as a funnel"; largest: Bay of Fundy 11.7 m mean (17 m extreme), Ungava Bay 9.75 m, Bristol Channel 9.6 m (15 m max); Mediterranean, Baltic and Caribbean have some of the smallest ranges; amphidromic points have almost zero range — [Wikipedia: Tidal range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_range)
- Fjord sills create strong tidal currents (Saltstraumen) — [Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)
- Wave size is limited by wind speed, duration, fetch ("uninterrupted distance of open water over which the wind blows") and water depth. Fully developed sea examples: 19 km/h wind over 19 km fetch → 0.27 m average height; 37 km/h over 139 km → 1.5 m; 56 km/h over 518 km → 4.1 m; 74 km/h over 1,313 km → 8.5 m; 92 km/h over 2,627 km → 14.8 m. Shallow-water waves break at height ≈ 0.8 × depth — [Wikipedia: Wind wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_wave)
- Undiscovered Worlds treats tide strength as a single planetary parameter ("lunar gravity") that scales mud flats and beaches, and uses per-mouth tidal range in delta and inlet decisions — [Undiscovered Worlds, Feb 2023](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2023/02), [June 2019](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2019/06)

### Inferences
- Lowstand-then-flood recipe (proposal): (1) run flow routing + stream-power incision with base level at −120 m (rivers extend across the exposed shelf and cut valleys graded to the lowstand shore; continue the largest into submarine canyons at the shelf break); (2) run the glacial pass where ice reached the coast (overdeepening allowed below even the lowstand level → fjords with sills); (3) raise sea level to 0: every valley mouth below 0 becomes a ria/estuary/fjord, shelf hills become islands and island chains, land bridges become straits; (4) partially REFILL drowned valleys with sediment where Qs is high (deltas and infilled estuaries — on Earth many rias on sediment-rich coasts are already filled; on sediment-poor hard-rock coasts they stay open — bg); (5) apply the coastal passes (section 4) at the new shoreline; (6) optionally apply glacio-isostatic rebound in formerly ice-loaded regions (emergent coasts with raised beaches: land rises → the sea retreats; Hudson Bay/Baltic pattern — bg), which also explains why not all glaciated coasts are drowned.
  - With the existing Priority-Flood/flow tools this costs one extra erosion configuration, no new algorithm. Lakes on the exposed shelf and in overdeepened basins simply merge with the sea when flooded. The fine grid inherits the drowned valleys from the coarse grid.
  - Estuary infill time vs sea-level stability also determines delta presence: Earth's modern deltas all post-date the slowdown of sea-level rise ~7–8 ka (bg). A generator knob "time since stillstand × Qs" decides how far each drowned valley is refilled.
- Cheap tidal range per coast cell (proposal; physical reasoning is bg): start from a planetary base range (≈1 m open ocean, cited; scaled by a moon parameter as in Undiscovered Worlds), then multiply by
  - an enclosure damping factor: basins connected to the ocean only through narrow straits (compute: ratio of strait cross-section to basin area via the water-component graph from section 4) → range × 0.1–0.3 (Mediterranean, Baltic, cited as smallest);
  - a shelf amplification factor growing with shelf width (wide shallow shelves amplify — bg);
  - a funnel factor for each bay from section 4's detection: convergence = mouth width / head width, and length relative to the quarter-wavelength resonance length L = T·√(g·h)/4 (T = 12.42 h; for h = 50 m, L ≈ 250 km; my arithmetic from the shallow-water wave speed — bays near this length resonate, as Fundy does — bg). Clamp to ≤ ~12 m mean (cited max).
  This is a per-water-component and per-bay scalar, O(coast length). A real co-tidal solution (shallow-water equations on the coarse grid) is feasible offline but not needed for landform choices, which only need the micro/meso/macro class.
- Wave exposure per coast cell: for 16–32 compass directions cast a ray over water up to a cap (e.g. 1,500–2,500 km, where the cited table shows seas approaching full development for strong winds) → fetch_d; exposure = Σ_d fetch_d^~0.5 × windSpeed_d² × frequency_d using the climate model's prevailing winds (the functional form is a design choice loosely following fetch-limited growth laws — bg). Ray casting at coarse resolution (km cells) costs N_coast × directions × ray length — seconds to a minute; or use a distance-to-land sweep per direction (linear time). Outputs: exposed vs sheltered flag, dominant wave direction, and the alongshore component → longshore drift direction.
- Longshore drift (proposal): transport ∝ wave energy × sin(2α), α = angle between dominant wave crests and the shoreline (CERC-type relation — bg). Along each littoral cell (bounded by headlands/deep canyons), integrate sediment sources (rivers, cliffs) down-drift; deposition where transport capacity falls (coast turns away, bay entrance, wave shadow of an island) → spits growing down-drift, baymouth bars, tombolos, cuspate forelands; erosion where capacity rises → cliffs. This is a 1-D calculation along the coastline polyline and cheap. A fuller model is the Coastline Evolution Model of Ashton & Murray (high-angle wave instability producing capes and spits) — known to me from background, not verified in this session.

### Gaps

- Sea-level curve details (rates, meltwater pulses, the ~7 ka stillstand) were not sourced beyond the LGM lowstand figure and the 8.2 ka jump mentioned in the Hijma record.
- Fetch-limited wave growth formulas (SMB/JONSWAP) were not fetched; only the fully-developed-sea table.

## 6. Groundwater: water table, springs, aquifers, wetlands, geothermal

### Takeaway
A full groundwater simulation is unnecessary: a topographic wetness index (already close to the existing
"drainage" field) plus lithology permeability and climate recharge gives a water-table depth field good
enough to place springs, aquifers, oases and every wetland type; the wetland TYPE then follows from water
source (rain vs groundwater vs river vs tide), climate and salinity.

### Cited Findings
- Topographic wetness index (compound topographic index), from Beven & Kirkby's TOPMODEL (1979): TWI = ln(a / tan b), a = upslope contributing area per unit contour width, b = local slope; a steady-state index; cells with similar TWI saturate under similar conditions; methods differ mainly in how upslope area is computed; in flat areas accumulation becomes very large and "TWI will not be a relevant variable" — [Wikipedia: Topographic wetness index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_wetness_index)
- Dwarf Fortress aquifers: found in soil layers and some porous rock layers, often spanning several layers / more than one z-level; light vs heavy aquifers differ in how fast they produce water (heavy ones fill almost immediately and effectively halt digging at that level); aquifers cannot be drained (limitless); the embark screen reports whether a site has a light or heavy aquifer — [DF wiki: Aquifer](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Aquifer), [40d: Aquifer](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Aquifer)
- Kettle ponds fed only by precipitation/groundwater dry out in summer if not connected to groundwater; acidic kettles become bogs, lime-buffered ones peatlands (fens) — [Wikipedia: Kettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_(landform))
- Undiscovered Worlds places saltwater wetlands next to tidal mud flats and mangroves on them in the tropics — [Undiscovered Worlds, Feb 2023](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2023/02)

### Inferences
- Water-table depth field (proposal): d_wt = clamp( D0 · aridity · permeabilityFactor · exp(−k·(TWI − TWI_mean)) ), anchored to 0 at rivers, lakes and the sea. Equivalent cheap alternative: "height above nearest drainage" (HAND — bg): for each cell, elevation minus elevation of the stream cell it drains to (one pass down the existing flow graph); water table ≈ a damped copy of the terrain: z_wt = z_stream + f·HAND with f from recharge/permeability (humid, low permeability → f near 1, water table follows the surface; arid or highly permeable → f near 0, deep water table). HAND behaves better than TWI in flat areas (the cited TWI weakness) and reuses the flow routing.
  - Better still for the offline budget: one steady-state solve of ∇·(T ∇h) = −R with h fixed at surface water and capped at ground level (cells where h reaches the surface become seepage/springs/wetland). A multigrid or a few hundred Jacobi sweeps at the coarse grid is seconds. Published global models (Fan, Li & Miguez-Macho 2013 water-table depth) do essentially this — from background, not verified here.
- Springs: where the water table intersects the surface — emit a spring at (a) the up-valley head of each perennial stream (channel initiation point), (b) the contact where a permeable layer overlies an impermeable one on a hillside (contact springs → spring line), (c) karst resurgences (big, at the base of limestone massifs), (d) fault lines, (e) artesian springs where a confined aquifer's recharge area is higher than the outcrop. Since the game stores geology layers (DF-style), (b) and (e) come from the layer stack: aquifer = permeable layer (sand, gravel, sandstone, limestone, fractured basalt) below the water table; confined if capped by clay/shale. Per column store `water_table_height` and per layer an `aquifer` class (none/light/heavy ← transmissivity × recharge), mirroring DF's light/heavy split (cited).
- Oases: arid cells where d_wt < ~2–5 m — at depressions reaching the water table, at springs along faults/escarpment feet, or along the buried course of an ephemeral river (wadi underflow). Artesian wells: cells over a confined aquifer whose pressure head (recharge-area height minus losses) exceeds ground level.
- Wetland classifier (types and controls are background knowledge):

| Type | Water source | Conditions | Detection from fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marsh (herbaceous, mineral soil) | Surface water, river/lake margins | Shallow standing water most of the year, any climate | d_wt ≈ 0, adjacent to lake/river, low slope |
| Swamp (forested) | River flooding / high water table | Warm-temperate to tropical, or boreal conifer swamps | d_wt ≈ 0 + forest climate; floodplain backswamps |
| Floodplain wetland / várzea | Seasonal river overflow | Large rivers with seasonal discharge | inside floodplain mask, below flood stage |
| Fen (peat, groundwater-fed, neutral/alkaline) | Groundwater seepage | Cool, humid; mineral-rich water | d_wt ≈ 0 at spring/seepage zones, cool climate, base-rich lithology |
| Bog (peat, rain-fed, acidic) | Precipitation only | Cool, P > PET all year; flat or blanket bogs on wet uplands | P − PET > 0 every month, mean T low, low slope (raised) or any slope in hyper-oceanic climates (blanket) |
| Peatland / muskeg / palsa mire | Rain + impeded drainage | Boreal/subarctic, permafrost or flat shield | cold + wet + flat |
| Salt marsh | Tides | Temperate/high-latitude sheltered intertidal mud | upper intertidal band, low wave exposure, outside mangrove zone |
| Mangrove | Tides | Tropical/subtropical (30°N–30°S, cited), sheltered muddy intertidal | upper intertidal band + warm |
| Inland delta / endorheic marsh | River ending in a flat basin | Okavango/Sudd type: river enters a flat with high evaporation | river spreads on slope < threshold in semi-arid zone; water balance closes by evaporation |
| Playa / salt flat / sabkha | Ephemeral inflow, evaporation | Arid endorheic basins; arid low coasts | closed basin with A* small (section 2) |
| Prairie potholes / vernal pools | Snowmelt/rain in small closed depressions | Hummocky glacial terrain, semi-arid to sub-humid | small closed depressions, seasonal balance |

- Geothermal springs / geysers / fumaroles / mud pots: place where a heat-flow field (from the tectonic/volcanic stage: active volcanic arcs, hotspots, rifts, young calderas) is high AND groundwater is available (d_wt shallow, recharge present). Geysers need the rarer combination of high heat, abundant water and a sealed fracture plumbing (silica-rich rhyolitic rocks — bg) → restrict to calderas/rhyolite; hot springs are common along faults even outside volcanic zones (deep circulation — bg). Features: point objects with temperature, discharge and mineral deposits (sinter/travertine terraces as small stamped terracettes).

### Gaps
- The DF wiki rate-limited direct fetches (HTTP 429); aquifer facts come from search-result summaries of wiki pages across versions (40d, v50) and Steam posts — treat specifics as approximate. How DF world generation places rivers, brooks and lakes was not retrieved at all.
- TOPMODEL's water-table equations (exponential transmissivity profile, saturation deficit) and HAND are not sourced here.
- No sources fetched for wetland classification thresholds (e.g. Ramsar/Cowardin), geothermal placement, or oasis hydrology.

## 7. Ice-related water

### Takeaway
All ice-related water features reduce to climate-field thresholds plus a few stamps: sea ice from monthly sea
temperature (seasonal min/max masks), glacier-fed regime as a discharge seasonality flag, and thermokarst
lakes/pingos as scattered small features in flat ice-rich permafrost.

### Cited Findings
- Sea ice: fast ice is attached to shore/seabed/grounded bergs; drift (pack) ice moves with wind and currents; first-year ice is typically 0.3–2 m thick, multiyear ice 2–4 m (far more common in the Arctic); Arctic extent minimum in September and maximum in March, Antarctic minimum in February and maximum in September/October; polynyas are persistent open-water areas (sensible-heat type from upwelling warm water; latent-heat type from persistent offshore winds); icebergs are calved from glaciers/ice shelves (land ice), unlike sea ice (frozen seawater) — [Wikipedia: Sea ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice)
- Thermokarst lakes: shallow lakes from thaw of ice-rich permafrost (excess ice > 30% by volume) in flat Arctic/subarctic lowlands; oriented lakes; taliks under deeper lakes; Teshekpuk Lake is the largest — [Wikipedia: Thermokarst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermokarst)
- Pingos are large ice-cored mounds grown from ice lenses; when they thaw and collapse they leave depressions — [Wikipedia: Thermokarst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermokarst)
- Jökulhlaups (sudden drainage of ice-dammed lakes) bury ice blocks and create rimmed kettles — [Wikipedia: Kettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_(landform))
- Glaciers reaching the sea carve fjords up to ~80°N; ice-sheet extent at the LGM (25% of land) defines where glacial lake types can occur — [Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord), [Wikipedia: Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum)

### Inferences
- Sea ice masks: seawater freezes near −1.8 °C at normal salinity (bg — the Sea ice article gave no number). Per ocean cell compute winter-minimum and summer-maximum SST from the climate stage → three classes: perennial pack (summer SST below freezing), seasonal ice (only winter below), ice-free. Store per cell `ice_months` (0–12); fast ice = seasonal/perennial ice cells within a few km of shore or in sheltered bays/shallow water (< ~20–25 m — bg); polynyas at coasts with persistent offshore wind (cited mechanism). Low-salinity enclosed seas (Baltic-like, from the salinity field) freeze more easily — raise the freezing threshold with lower salinity.
- Lake and river ice: `ice_months` from air temperature with a lag proportional to lake depth (deep lakes freeze late or never — bg).
- Icebergs: spawn zones = tidewater glacier fronts and ice-shelf edges; drift lanes follow the cold currents from the climate model; a density field ("iceberg alley") rather than objects in worldgen.
- Ice shelves: where an ice sheet reaches the sea in an embayment with mean annual temperature well below freezing (bg: roughly < −5 to −9 °C) the ice floats instead of calving at the coast: store as a floating ice slab over the water column (water-surface height + ice thickness), cliff edge 20–50 m above water (bg).
- Glacier-fed rivers: flag reaches whose catchment glacier fraction exceeds a few percent: discharge peaks in mid/late summer (melt) instead of with rainfall; strong diurnal cycle; high sediment load → braided gravel beds (sandur) and milky "glacial flour" water colour, turquoise proglacial lakes (bg). Feeds the braided classifier in section 1 and the regime enum.
- Snowmelt-fed (nival) rivers: spring peak; rain-fed (pluvial): follow the wet season. A 12-value monthly discharge vector per reach (from monthly precipitation, snow storage/melt by temperature, glacier melt) covers all regimes including ephemeral rivers, and costs 12 accumulation passes over the existing flow graph.
- Thermokarst generation: in cells with permafrost (mean annual T < ~−2 °C for continuous-ish permafrost — bg), slope < ~1°, fine-grained ice-rich sediment (lowland, not bedrock): Poisson-scatter elliptical lakes covering 10–40% of the area (bg figure), depth 1–3 m (deeper → talik flag), long axes aligned (perpendicular to the prevailing summer wind is the usual interpretation — bg; the cited article says the wind role is unproven). Drained lake basins (alases) as shallow grassy depressions. Pingos: rare cone stamps 3–70 m high, up to several hundred metres across, in drained lake basins (bg). Needs ≤ 32 m cells for pingos; lakes work at 32–100 m.
- Patterned ground / ice-wedge polygons are 10–30 m features (bg) → texture/biome detail, not heightfield features at 16–32 m.

### Gaps
- No km² figures for seasonal sea-ice extent were in the fetched text; freezing temperature, fast-ice depth limits, ice-shelf temperature limit and all thermokarst/pingo dimensions are unverified background numbers.
- Glacier-fed discharge regimes and permafrost temperature thresholds were not sourced in this session.
- No source found for how any existing game generator models sea ice (Undiscovered Worlds page fetched did not mention it).

## 8. What existing generators and the graphics literature actually do (source survey)

### Takeaway
Hobby generators mostly DETECT and NAME water features on what flow routing gives them and add a few stamped
extras (deltas, barrier islands, mud flats); the academic graphics literature covers rivers thoroughly
(network, carving, meandering) but — as far as these searches found — not coastal landforms, so the coastal
catalogue has to come from geomorphology rather than from an off-the-shelf graphics paper.

### Cited Findings
- Génevaux et al. 2013 — hydrology-first terrain: river graph from mouths with Horton–Strahler grammar, Rosgen-typed reaches, Voronoi watersheds, terrain from a construction tree of primitives; cannot do deltas or oxbows — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Peytavie et al. 2019 — riverbed carving with compact elevation modifiers and procedural water-surface/flow on an existing terrain; interactive editing of river paths and primitives — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf13814)
- Paris et al. 2023 — vector meander migration with cutoffs (oxbows) and avulsions on a terrain with a coarse network; public code — [project page](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/paris2023_Meanders.html), [code](https://github.com/aparis69/Meandering-rivers)
- Liang et al. 2015 — DeltaRCM reduced-complexity delta model (parcels, 50 m cells, ~100-cell grids) — [ESurf](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/3/67/2015/)
- Mogensen & Isenbecker 2025 — zoomable maps with rivers and fjords by recursive triangle subdivision — [arXiv:2504.08383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08383)
- Red Blob Games — mapgen4: rainfall-driven rivers on a dual mesh as binary trees; river-first experiments ("procedural river drainage basins", "elevation from rivers") using Strahler/Shreve numbers — [rivers page](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/rivers/), [drainage basins](https://redblobgames.com/x/1723-procedural-river-growing), [elevation from rivers](https://www.redblobgames.com/x/1724-elevation-from-rivers)
- Dragons Abound (Scott Turner) — detects bays on the coastline and names them from size, position, climate, cities and rivers with frequency-weighted generic terms; generates barrier islands as offset polygons on low-sinuosity coast — [Bay Watch](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/07/the-naming-of-places-part-6-bay-watch.html), [Barrier Islands Revisited](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/12/barrier-islands-revisited.html?m=0)
- Undiscovered Worlds (Jonathan Hill; GPL C++ source) — global + regional maps; rivers and lakes from flow; deltas as a separate reversed-direction river layer chosen by tidal range, river size and steepness; tidal inlets, mud flats, sand/shingle beaches via a diffused silt field; barrier islands off mud flats; mangroves; planetary tide strength; January/July river flows (seasonality) — [GitHub](https://github.com/fegennari/Undiscovered_Worlds), [June 2019](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2019/06), [Feb 2023](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2023/02)
- Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator — rivers follow elevation, lakes form in depressions and are assigned to lake groups; a 2019 hands-on review found lakes looked randomly placed and rivers had sharp right-angle turns — [wiki mirror](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/src/commit/05c53d276aaa1153f8d61c6ab515f79fccd6744f/wiki/Generation-Process.md), [Dragons Abound, Aug 2019](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2019/08)
- Dwarf Fortress — aquifers as a per-layer property with light/heavy variants shown at embark — [DF wiki: Aquifer](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Aquifer)

### Inferences
- Division of labour suggested by the survey: flow routing (already present) → Paris-style vector pass for meanders/oxbows on low-slope reaches → Undiscovered-Worlds-style distributary layer for deltas (or DeltaRCM windows for the biggest) → geomorphology-driven coast classifier and stamps → Dragons-Abound-style detection and naming for bays/capes/straits. Nothing in the surveyed work requires a GPU or more than seconds-to-minutes per stage at the stated grid sizes.
- Naming: Dragons Abound's approach (generic term chosen by size class with real-world frequency weights + a specific from position/climate/nearby settlement/river) transfers directly to lakes, straits, capes, falls; in a DF-style game the specific can come from the history/civilisation layer.
- The Rosgen-typed "river primitive" idea (Génevaux) and the "compact elevation modifier" idea (Peytavie) both suit the fine 16–32 m grid: store rivers as vector objects with type/width/depth and rasterise the channel cross-section into the heightfield + water-surface height at region generation time. That keeps `generate_region` a pure function of coordinates plus the stored objects.

### Gaps
- Martin O'Leary's terrain notes (mewo2.com/notes/terrain) returned 404 at the URL tried; from memory it uses Planchon–Darboux filling, flux-based erosion and coast clean-up and produces only rivers and a coastline — unverified here.
- Azgaar's source (lakes, features, rivers modules) could not be read — three URL guesses returned 404; its concrete rules (lake open/closed by evaporation vs flux, ocean/sea/gulf by size, river width formula) remain unverified.
- Dwarf Fortress worldgen for rivers/brooks/lakes was not retrieved (wiki rate limit).
- mapgen4's exact river-width function was not confirmed (search could not see the source).
- I did not find graphics papers on procedural coasts/barrier islands/reefs/tides. Possibly relevant but unverified from memory: Galin et al. 2019 "A Review of Digital Terrain Modeling" (CGF STAR), Cordonnier et al. glacial erosion "Forming Terrains by Glacial Erosion" (SIGGRAPH 2023), Paris et al. "Desertscape Simulation" (2019), Schott et al. "Large-scale terrain authoring through interactive erosion simulation" (2023) — worth a targeted follow-up, particularly the glacial erosion paper for fjords/glacial lakes.

## 9. Resolution: which grid each feature belongs to

### Takeaway
Assign each feature to the coarsest grid on which its defining dimension spans at least ~3–5 cells; the
coarse planetary grid decides WHERE and WHAT TYPE (eligibility, class, parameters), the fine 16–32 m grid
builds the SHAPE. Features narrower than a fine cell stay vector/point objects.

### Cited Findings
- Dimension anchors from sources: kettles mostly < 2 km across, < 10 m deep — [Kettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_(landform)); fringing reefs usually < 100 m wide, barrier-reef lagoons several km wide and 30–70 m deep, patch reefs tens of metres, platform reefs hundreds of metres to many km — [Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef); barrier islands up to 182 km long, "long, low and narrow" — [Barrier island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island); fjords up to 420 km long and >1 km deep — [Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord); bay closing lines up to 24 nautical miles (~44 km) in law — [UNCLOS](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm); DeltaRCM field-scale deltas use 50 m cells — [DeltaRCM PDF](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/3/67/2015/esurf-3-67-2015.pdf); Génevaux evaluates final terrain at 1024² samples over tens of km — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)
- Undiscovered Worlds splits exactly this way: deltas are "planned at the global level" and drawn in detail on regional maps — [June 2019](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2019/06)

### Inferences
(Proposed assignment; typical sizes not cited above are background estimates.)

|---|---|---|---|---|
