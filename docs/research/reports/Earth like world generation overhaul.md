# Rebuilding the forge into a pocket Earth

An Earth-like default world is buildable as an offline pipeline that keeps the forge's erosion core and wraps five new stages around it: a time-stepped plate simulation on a coarse east–west cylinder, a twelve-month climate model, a hydrology stage with a moving sea level and water-balance lakes, a layered classifier for the 72 biomes, and a runtime weather function that is pure in (world tick, position, stored climate). The binding constraint is scale, not algorithms: with both poles on a 100 km map each degree of latitude is 0.56 km wide, so the owner must first choose between a compressed "pocket planet" and a much larger or differently shaped world. Goal 1 (hot equator, glacial top and bottom) is already a knob value in the forge today (`pole = 3`); goals 2 to 5 each need a structural change, and the main blockers are a land/sea mask fixed before erosion, a climate stored as two annual rasters, single-thread river trees, fixed-size raster arrays in the artifact, and a runtime that has no season, weather or changing water level. The evidence is uneven and this report keeps it that way: the plate rules, Köppen criteria, plant limits, glacial erosion laws and game weather systems were read from papers or source code, but the recommended plate design (Cortial-style rules on a flat raster), the monthly climate model, the ocean-current rules, most coastal landform recipes and nearly every numeric biome threshold are the researchers' own untested designs or recalled values, and every run-time figure for the new stages is an estimate. Nobody found a published system that does the whole chain, so the plan below is a sequence of prototypes with validation gates, not an assembly of known parts. Every stored-format change forces a re-forge of existing worlds, which the project already accepts for dev worlds.

Throughout, claims carry the researchers' own labels: **verified** (read in the paper, documentation or source code), **search summary** (seen only as an abstract or search snippet), **recalled** (from memory, not re-checked), and **inferred** (the researcher's derivation, arithmetic or design proposal). Where this report combines notes into something no single note says, it is marked **synthesis**. Facts about the current code come from a read-only audit of `develop` on 2026-11-14 in which nothing was built or run, so every number quoted from the code is a quotation, not a measurement.

## A 100 km map gives each degree of latitude 0.56 km

### What the forge is today, measured against the five goals

The forge (`crates/worldgen/src/forge/`) is a nine-stage pipeline on a square `n × n` grid of `f64` values: an initial world (height, ocean mask, uplift rate, outlets, edges), geology, climate, an erosion loop, shaping, hydrology, derived fields, a spawn point, and biomes with regions. It runs coarse to fine: the menu default of **100,352 m at 16 m cells is 6,272² = 39.3 million cells**, eroded at 64 m, 32 m and 16 m with 200, 40 and 80 iterations of 2,500 years each, about 0.8 million years of modelled erosion in all. Its erosion is the published state of the art for this class of generator: Priority-Flood routing, the linear-time depression graph of Cordonnier, Bovy and Braun, and implicit stream-power incision after Braun and Willett, bit-identical for any thread count. That core is the part to keep.

| Goal | Today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Equator in the middle, poles top and bottom | A knob value: `pole = 3` ("Both") in `climate.rs`, covered by a test; the default is one pole | Small in the forge; the real problem is 180° of latitude over 16–100 km |
| 2. Simulated plates, aged ranges | Static Voronoi plates with one random drift direction each; land and sea come from a separate layout formula; no time, no ages, no crust type | Large: a new stage, consumed in one place (`World::new_continental`) |
| 3. 72 biomes | 20 biomes from annual mean temperature and annual precipitation | Medium to large: classifier, stored fields, about ten consumers with fixed-size tables |
| 4. All water features | River polylines, flat spill-level lakes (nine carved bowls per world), a salt rule, static ice; sea at most 90 m deep on a fixed mask | Large: several features are impossible with a fixed ocean mask and tree-shaped rivers |
| 5. Yearly climate cycle | None, and a stated non-goal of the current design; a 336-day calendar exists but no simulation reads it | Large, mostly in `game` and `client`, under the determinism rules |

Three properties of the current design shape everything that follows. First, the initial world must be recomputable at any cell size from seed and knobs alone, because every refinement level calls `World::new` again; plates and belts are therefore analytic functions of position, and **a plate simulation that produces a raster breaks this assumption unless its output is computed once and resampled per level**, as the volcano reference grid already is. Second, the forge is deliberately not bit-reproducible across machines; its output is a stored artifact, and only what the runtime derives from the artifact must be exact. That frees the new offline stages to use ordinary floating point, and puts the whole determinism burden on the runtime sampler and the weather function (synthesis from the audit). Third, land never becomes sea and sea never becomes land: the ocean mask is fixed by stage 1 and land is clamped to 0.05 m, which is the single largest blocker for the water goal.

### The forge is a 1:1 landscape wearing a planet-scale climate

Erosion, rivers, floodplains, lakes, volcano cones and the climate's relaxation lengths are in physical metres and do not scale with the world (9 km inland moisture relaxation, 8 km volcano spacing, 140–420 m lake bowls). Coast noise and plate geometry do scale. So at 100 km with both poles on the map, a climate zone is 5–15 km wide, a player walks from tundra to desert in about 25 km, and the default 5° latitude meander is 2.8 km. The audit puts the choice plainly: either a real planet abstracted at coarse cells, in which case 16 m cells and a 39-million-cell budget are the wrong tool for the global stage, or a roughly 100 km pocket planet with compressed latitude, in which case plate tectonics is a stylised generator and not a physical one.

This report recommends the pocket planet with a two-grid design (synthesis; the plate, climate and water notes each assume it independently). A coarse **planet grid** of a few hundred cells per side carries everything that is planetary: plates, crust ages, pressure, winds, currents, monthly climate, coast classes, tides. Its horizontal lengths are expressed in degrees of latitude or map fractions, never metres; the climate researcher asks for exactly this (inferred), and it happens that a 10–20° rain-out length at 0.56 km per degree is 5.6–11 km, the same range as today's 9 km constant (arithmetic in this report). The existing fine grid stays a 1:1 physical landscape for erosion, rivers and landforms. Vertical scale is uncompressed on both. The honest consequence is that numbers taken from Earth-scale sources, such as a subduction uplift reach of 1,800 km, become shape parameters to be re-tuned as map fractions.

The alternative is a far larger map. The numbers argue against it at 16 m cells: the forge's own model (`9.0 s × Mcells^1.05`, `100 MB + 180 B × cells`, fitted to measurements at 1 and 4 million cells on 20 cores) gives about 7 minutes and 7.2 GB for 100 km at 16 m, and that top size has never been timed with the current code. A 2:1 map of 200 km × 100 km at 16 m would be 78.7 million cells and about 14 GB by the same model, over the 12 GiB cap the menu enforces for in-client forging; at 32 m cells it would be about 3.6 GB (arithmetic in this report from the audit's model).

### Shape, wrap and polar edges are format decisions, so they come first

The world is square everywhere: one `size_m`, rasters `n × n`, and no width/height pair in the forge, the artifact, the tiles or the menu. A 2:1 equirectangular-style map needs a second dimension threaded through all of them. The map does not wrap; `EdgeKind` has only `Sea` and `Land`, and which edges are sea is picked by the seed. A complete plan for an east–west cylinder exists and is unimplemented (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-05-east-west-world-wrap.md`); its decision D1 is "Cylinder, x only. Latitude drives climate, so z never wraps."

Three research strands converge on that cylinder. The plate researcher recommends it over a torus (PlaTec's wrap in both axes sends crust leaving the north edge back in at the south, which contradicts poles) and over a sphere projected flat (an equirectangular unwrap stretches east–west by 1/cos(latitude), doubling at 60°), noting that the game map is flat by design, so a sphere "buys realism the player can never see" (inferred). The climate researcher calls the wrap "strongly recommended" for winds and currents, because otherwise the upwind edge needs an invented open-ocean inflow of moisture and temperature (inferred). And the audit observes that an equator-centred world with real east and west edges has continents cut by the map border. For the top and bottom, the plate note proposes treating the edge rows as the far side of an ocean: crust that leaves is destroyed, gaps are filled with new oceanic crust, uplift fades in an absorbing margin, and mostly oceanic plates start there; the climate note adds that the pole rows can simply be ice. No source discusses polar-edge handling for a non-wrapping axis; these rules are a design proposal and need a prototype.

For the Earth-like default this means: an explicit setting that north and south are sea-or-ice edges, east and west a new `Wrap` variant, `pole = 3` with a 90° span, and a new "Earth-like" preset so that existing presets keep their exact-match names. One presentation detail follows from the flat rectangle: meridians do not converge, so polar land is over-represented compared with a sphere, and every area statistic used for validation must be declared as either cosine-weighted ("Earth-like as a planet") or unweighted ("Earth-like share of the playable map").

## Moving plates must hand erosion an uplift map and an age map

### Only three families simulate plates over time, and one records what is needed

The plate researcher read the primary material for three families that move plates through time; everything else in the hobby space draws plates once and classifies boundaries once, which is what the forge does now and why it produces one belt of one age per boundary.

Cortial, Peytavie, Galin and Guérin's "Procedural Tectonic Planets" was read in full. It moves rigid plates on a sphere in 2-million-year steps for about 250 million years, with phenomenological rules its authors describe as chosen "for their performance and plausibility" and "without guaranteed physical accuracy". Each sample carries crust type, thickness, elevation, oceanic age and ridge direction, and for continental crust the three things this overhaul needs: **orogeny age, orogeny type (Andean from subduction, Himalayan from collision) and fold direction**. Subduction uplift on the overriding plate is a rate, `u0 · f(distance to front) · g(relative speed) · h(relief of the subducting plate)` with `u0` = 0.6 mm/yr; continental collision is a discrete event in which a terrane that interpenetrates by more than 300 km is sutured to the other plate with an elevation surge scaled by its area; plates rift at a Poisson rate that grows with area and continental fraction, which the paper says "prevents the formation of unnatural super-continents"; new ocean crust forms at ridges at −1 km and sinks toward a −6 km abyssal plain, with trenches at −10 km. Published cost on a 2015 quad-core is 0.19 to 1.90 seconds per step from 60,000 to 500,000 samples, plus 0.58 to 13.1 seconds per global resample ([Cortial et al. 2019](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eric.galin/Articles/2019-planets.pdf)). Its stated omissions matter here: no continental shelves or passive margins, no hotspots, and too many "forced subduction" events. The paper also gives no values for the rift rate, the slab-pull constant, the fold-update constant or the control distance of the uplift profile; those must be tuned.

PlaTec and its fork `plate-tectonics` were read from the C++ source, not from Viitanen's thesis, which could not be fetched. Each plate is its own raster sliding over a world grid that wraps in both axes; height doubles as crust mass; overlapping oceanic crust subducts and queues sediment for the overriding plate; overlapping continents fold and, past an overlap threshold, the colliding segment is moved to the other plate. There is no rifting: when plates stall the simulation discards them and re-cuts the heightmap into fresh random plates for another cycle ([lithosphere.cpp](https://github.com/Mindwerks/plate-tectonics/blob/master/src/lithosphere.cpp)). It records only a crust timestamp, not orogeny age or type. Its strength is the data structure: per-plate rasters on a wrapping grid are simple, fast and lossless under translation. Its run times are secondhand.

Tectonics.js was read from its author's posts. Its second version inverts the causality: crust age sets density, dense old ocean crust decides where subduction starts, and plates are an output of the velocity field. The author's own account of the first version (random plates, random velocities) is that it left ocean crust a billion years old, and of early erosion that mountains "eventually occup[ied] every available cell of continental crust" and then stood about 15 km high ([Tectonics.js, version 2.0](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/_posts/2017-9-16-version2.md); [erosion post](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/_posts/2014-1-29-reddit-erosion-photosynthesis.md)). Both are failure modes to test for.

The academic line since 2019 did not extend plate motion; it moved to uplift-driven erosion, where a painted uplift map feeds stream power ([Cordonnier et al. 2016](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf12820)), uplift becomes the authoring domain ([Schott et al. 2023](https://h-schott.github.io/publications/uplift/publi_uplift.html)), and time becomes an explicit parameter of an analytical solution ([Tzathas et al. 2024](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf15033)). The last two were seen at abstract level only. No source gives a tested recipe for coupling a moving-plate simulation to Braun–Willett erosion; that coupling is the project's own work.

### The recommended design: Cortial's rules on a PlaTec-style raster, on a cylinder

Everything in this subsection is the plate researcher's synthesis and is labelled by them as untested: no published implementation of a Cortial-style model on a flat raster was found, and its run-time estimate is not measured.

The tectonic grid is 512 × 256 to 1,024 × 512 cells wrapping in x. World arrays hold a plate id and a per-cell record that is a superset of Cortial's: crust kind, crust age (which doubles as a craton flag when very old), crust thickness, orogeny age, orogeny kind (none, volcanic arc, Andean cordillera, Ural-style collision, Himalayan-style collision plateau, accreted terrane, rift shoulder, hotspot), fold direction and peak uplift. Each plate keeps a pristine source raster and an affine transform (offset plus a small rotation about its centroid); each step updates the transforms, inverse-maps plates onto the world grid so rotation leaves no holes, resolves overlaps and gaps, applies the rules, and every few steps bakes the world arrays back into fresh plate rasters so that sutured and rifted crust changes owner. The rules are Cortial's with raster distance fields in place of mesh queries: the older oceanic cell is consumed on overlap and a distance-to-front field drives the uplift formula; continental overlap beyond a threshold floods the terrane, sutures it and applies the surge, converted to an uplift pulse spread over 10–20 million years so the erosion stage sees a rate and not a step; gaps become new ocean crust with depth from age; a small pull toward subducting edges stands in for slab pull; rift lines are noise-warped Voronoi cuts that avoid high-age craton cells and record rift shoulders and one failed arm; 10–20 fixed mantle points stamp age-progressive hotspot chains, which Cortial's paper suggests in one sentence.

The run starts from one supercontinent covering 25–30% of the map plus a few microcontinents and simulates the breakup for 150–300 steps of 2–5 million years. The reasoning for the supercontinent start is that continents then fit like a jigsaw, with passive margins on the rifted sides and active margins outside, and that "several continents" becomes a rifting-versus-suturing balance governed by one rate. Several ranges of different sizes per continent follow structurally: every suture, accreted arc and abandoned subduction margin leaves a belt whose age keeps counting, and Cortial's collision footprint scales with the collider (`r = rc·sqrt(v/v0 · A/A0)`), so an island arc leaves a small low belt and a continent a wide high one. Sea level is solved afterwards as the level that gives the target land fraction. Because the outcome cannot be guaranteed, the researcher proposes scoring finished worlds (continents above a size, belts per continent by age class) and re-running failed seeds; the forge already has a seed-search score for this. The cost estimate is tens of milliseconds per step at 1,024 × 512 multi-threaded, so seconds to tens of seconds per run, from operation counts only.

Shelves, back-arc basins, hotspots and large igneous provinces are modelled by none of the simulators found. The shelf proposal (mark continental crust stretched at a rift and place it just below sea level, width by margin age) is the researcher's own. Real-world rules of thumb used for tuning (rifts start inside continents and avoid cratons, supercontinent cycles of 400–750 million years, plate speeds from under 1 to 20 cm/yr) come from Worldbuilding Pasta, a hobbyist synthesis of the geology literature and not a primary source ([Worldbuilding Pasta, part Va](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html)).

### Old mountains will come out flat unless age is modelled as more than "uplift off"

The geology supports the young-sharp, old-rounded contrast the owner wants, with a warning. The standard detachment-limited stream-power model, which is the law the forge uses, predicts post-orogenic decay times of only 1–10 million years; isostatic rebound of the crustal root lengthens that by at most about six times, a shift to transport-limited conditions by two to three times, and a critical shear stress for erosion by about twenty times in the authors' example, which together explain residual topography lasting over 100 million years ([Baldwin, Whipple & Tucker 2003, abstract](https://umimpact.umt.edu/en/publications/implications-of-the-shear-stress-river-incision-model-for-the-tim/)). Both researchers who cite this took the multipliers from abstracts, not from the paper's text. Measured erosion rates span the same contrast: 25–30 m per million years in the Great Smoky Mountains against 2.7 mm/yr in the High Himalaya ([USGS record of Matmon et al.](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70024865); [Vance et al. 2003](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003E%26PSL.206..273V/abstract); both search summaries). The Appalachians are eroded remnants of a collision about 300 million years ago whose present relief owes much to later re-uplift ([US National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/geology/plate-tectonics-collisional-mountain-ranges.htm)).

For the forge this has a specific meaning, on which the audit and both terrain researchers agree. The erosion run models 0.8 million years, far too short for a range to age on its own, and if uplift is simply switched off the range is gone within a few simulated million years: "old, low, rounded" would come out as "flat". Age therefore has to enter as inputs. The carriers already exist: `erosion.rs` reads uplift and erodibility per cell, and exhumed basement rock appears where `uplifted` is large. The recommended first coupling (the plate note's option A, inferred) is a single erosion run at the end fed by the present uplift rate, an initial height from isostasy on crust thickness, and per-cell erodibility and hillslope diffusivity driven by orogeny age: young belts get high uplift and are stopped far from steady state (sharp, high, immature drainage); old belts get inherited height, a low residual uplift equal to the rebound, lower erodibility and a higher diffusion-to-incision ratio (low, smooth, dendritic). The mountains researcher adds the cheap physics that makes this hold up, all as their own recipes: a rebound term that returns about 0.8 of each eroded metre through a low-pass filter (their arithmetic from densities of 2.7 and 3.3 gives 82%, matching Baldwin's factor of six), an incision threshold, a sediment-deposition term, and a late broad rejuvenation pulse of a few hundred metres to cut gorges into old plateaus. The richer option, interleaving erosion with plate steps so old belts carry old drainage, needs the eroded surface advected with the plates and should wait.

Two existing mechanisms need adjusting. `fit_height_band` scales all land by one factor when the highest peak exceeds 2,800 m, so absolute heights per range are not controllable; and the height-versus-age rule of thumb the notes offer (mean height starting near 2,500 m and dropping 5 m per million years) is explicitly the blogger's own approximation, with the researcher's exponential alternative labelled a guess. The numeric relief targets by age class in the mountains note (peak heights, modal slopes, local relief) are recalled, not sourced; the proposed validation set (erosion-rate map, slope histogram per belt, hypsometric curve, local relief in a 5 km window) is the sounder guide.

### Mountain types are uplift shapes plus rock columns; glaciers are a routing problem

No paper was found that gives a recipe per mountain type. The mountains researcher's proposals, all inferred with numbers from memory, express each type as a different uplift history and rock column: fold belts as an asymmetric uplift profile over folded strata so hard beds make strike ridges; volcanic arcs as constructed cones of weak rock with hard lava lobes, set parallel to the trench; hotspot chains as wide basalt shields with age-progressive erosion and subsidence, ending in atolls; fault-block ranges and rifts as sawtooth tilted-block uplift with sediment-filled basins; plateaus as broad uniform uplift under a hard cap rock so escarpments, mesas and stepped canyons appear on their own; domes as a Gaussian bump that exposes concentric hogbacks. Two of these collide with the audit: the forge has **one global rock column for the whole world** and six fixed rocks with no link between plates and rock type, so folded belts, cap-rock plateaus, volcanic provinces and karst all need geology to vary in space, which is new work; and the notes' call for a rock-dependent threshold-slope pass (cliffs over scree, the signature of young ranges) is a new erosion term. The published parameter set for that family of terms is Cordonnier and colleagues' 2023 table: a debris-flow law with a 37° critical slope, fluvial and hillslope constants, read from the PDF with a caveat that its text extraction was garbled in places ([Cordonnier et al. 2023](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonnier23ToG.pdf)).

For ice, the verified findings set the method. Mass balance can be as simple as `M = min(p_max, β·(surface − ELA))`, or a monthly degree-day balance as OGGM uses ([OGGM docs](https://docs.oggm.org/en/stable/mass-balance-monthly.html)). The costly part is flow: explicit shallow-ice steps scale with the square of cell size, and Cordonnier's 2023 glacial-erosion method, the best in graphics, needs a GPU and a neural emulator, about 2,000 steps, and 9.8 minutes at 1,024² and 37.5 minutes at 2,048²; the same paper shows that shallow-ice flow coupled to erosion carves wrong, V-shaped valleys. The CPU-friendly alternative is Hergarten's glacial stream-power law: route ice down the same drainage tree as water, with an effective erodibility blending glacial and fluvial terms by the ice share of the flux and slope taken on the ice surface. It is fully implicit, linear in grid size, costs **1.7 to 2.7 times a fluvial step**, has been run on 5,000 × 5,000 grids, and reproduces wide flat-floored valleys, overdeepenings and hanging valleys at least qualitatively; it is limited to valley glaciers and a referee questioned its physical basis ([Hergarten 2021](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/9/937/2021/)). The researcher's recommended split (inferred): ice sheets on the planet grid by shallow-ice relaxation or a plastic profile (the profile formula is recalled); valley glaciers by Hergarten routing on a level two to four times coarser than final, which their unmeasured budget sketch puts at tens of seconds to a few minutes; and the visible present-day ice body as the routed thickness placed on the final bedrock.

A past glaciation is what makes mid-latitude land read as formerly glaciated. Cordonnier's own scenario lowers the equilibrium line by 1,800 m over 100,000 years and then warms quickly, and finds a second glaciation adds little; real ice-age lowering was about 900 m ([Quaternary Research, search summary](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/quaternary-research/article/effect-of-sealevel-lowering-on-ela-depression-during-the-lgm/9E0BFA4886EE7BE4D4A617AEDD04D480)). The researcher's schedule (inferred, including the simplification that one boosted cycle stands in for many) is one cold stage with the sea 120 m lower, glacial erosion inside the ice mask, a stepped retreat that stamps a moraine at each still-stand, and a short finishing pass too brief to erase the lakes. Depositional forms are produced by none of the models read; moraines, drumlins, eskers, kettles and outwash must be stamped from the simulated ice extent and flow direction, with dimensions that are recalled.

### How this maps onto the forge

`World { h, ocean, uplift, outlet, edges }` is already the contract between "tectonics" and "erosion", and `World::new_continental` is its single consumer; the new stage replaces `ContinentalMass`, `ContinentalShape`, `plates::plate_field` and `interior_ranges`, and feeds `volcano_sites` and `fields::volcanism`, which also call the old plate field. The stage runs once on its fixed grid before the level loop and is resampled per level with noise-warped upsampling so coasts keep fine detail. The contract widens by initial height, crust and orogeny records, and per-cell erosion modifiers. The sea floor's −90 m clamp and the 16-bit height raster (a 4,096 m span at 6.25 cm steps, with blocks scanned from −1,024 to 4,096) cannot hold Cortial-scale bathymetry; the realistic choice is to keep kilometre-deep ocean as data on the planet grid and clamp the playable sea floor, which makes abyssal plain and trench labels rather than diggable depth (synthesis; an owner decision below). Tests hard-coded to the current scheme will be replaced, not repaired: `belts_follow_plate_boundaries`, the plate-field determinism and continuity tests, and `plates_agree_across_resolutions`. The island layouts are pinned bit for bit (`island_layout_unchanged`) and the `forge_quality` gate measures the one-island path, so the audit's advice is to freeze the island layouts as a legacy path and replace only the continental one.

## Twelve monthly normals replace two annual rasters

### What exists elsewhere, and where it fails

The climate researcher read source code for the generators that matter. Only three hobby or game sources produce seasonal fields at all: Undiscovered Worlds (January and July, about 14,500 lines of climate C++), Frozen Fractal's "Around the World" (January and July from latitude curves fitted to reanalysis), and the ExoPlaSim workflow, which is a real general circulation model. Azgaar, WorldEngine, Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld and Tectonics.js are annual. No computer-graphics paper that generates a planetary monthly climate was found.

The two seasonal generators are instructive mostly through their repairs and failures. Undiscovered Worlds marches rain along one wind map and then needs explicit correction passes that shift rain to winter to "encourage" Mediterranean climates, boost equatorial rain for rainforest, and cut winter rain near ±12° for savanna ([globalclimate.cpp](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds/blob/main/globalclimate.cpp)). Frozen Fractal validated on real Earth topography and got the coasts roughly right but lost Asia's continental climates entirely to cold desert ([Frozen Fractal, part 9](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/29/around-the-world-9-climates/)). The researcher reads these (inferred) as evidence that a pure wind-march does not produce Earth's seasonal regimes without a subsidence term tied to the seasonally shifting subtropical high, and that continentality built from two blended latitude curves, with no land moisture recycling and no summer continental low, fails in exactly the place a model should be tested.

A real climate model is out of budget. ExoPlaSim at its coarsest (32 × 64 cells) takes 30–60 seconds per model year on HPC nodes and about 3.75 to 6 minutes per year on consumer machines, needs tens of model years to equilibrate, has a slab ocean with no currents (so north-west Europe comes out too cold), under-produces the East Asian monsoon, and needs a sphere ([Paradise et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07685); [Worldbuilding Pasta ExoPlaSim tutorial](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/11/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vi.html)).

### The recommended model, stage by stage

The researcher's recommendation is a twelve-month diagnostic model on the planet grid, estimated at 1,500–3,000 lines of Rust and tens of seconds of run time. It is a synthesis of hand rules and partial implementations, "not something I found implemented in one place", and its cost is a back-of-envelope figure.

**Insolation** is closed form and verified: daily mean top-of-atmosphere flux from latitude and solar declination, with polar day and night falling out of the sunrise hour angle ([Wikipedia: Insolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolation)). A table of twelve months by latitude rows costs nothing, and the same hour angle gives day length for later use.

**Temperature** comes from a two-dimensional seasonal energy-balance model: per cell, heat capacity times the temperature change equals absorbed sunlight, minus linear outgoing radiation `A + B·T`, plus diffusion, plus advection by the prevailing wind. The one-dimensional version is a standard teaching model whose defaults were read from the climlab documentation (A = 210 W/m², B = 2 W/m²/°C, D = 0.555, ice albedo 0.62 below −10 °C), and whose seasonal notebook finds a 10 m mixed layer matches the observed zonal cycle ([climlab API](https://climlab.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/climlab.model.ebm.html)). Giving land a shallow effective depth and ocean a deep one produces seasonal amplitude, lag and continentality from one equation; the researcher's own slab derivation reproduces the observed pattern of about a month of lag on land and two to three months over ocean ([Wikipedia: Seasonal lag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_lag)) with roughly 1–2 m for land and 40–70 m for ocean. The wind advection term is what makes west coasts maritime and east coasts continental. Snow and ice albedo is applied as a diagnosed ramp iterated to convergence, and the outgoing-radiation constant is solved so the global mean hits a requested 15 °C, which guards against a map with much polar land tipping into a snowball. The two-dimensional land–sea version has a classic published form that the researcher cites from memory only. The fine grid then applies the lapse rate per cell from the fine heightmap, as the forge does now at 6.5 °C/km.

**Pressure and wind** are diagnosed per month: a zonal pressure profile measured from a seasonal thermal equator (the latitude of maximum smoothed sea-level temperature along each column, clamped), with subtropical highs near 25° in the winter hemisphere and 35° in the summer one; a thermal anomaly that puts highs over cold winter continents and lows over hot summer ones; and wind as the pressure gradient rotated by a latitude-dependent angle that relaxes to straight down-gradient flow at the equator. The band positions and seasonal shifts are Worldbuilding Pasta's hand rules ([part VIb](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/05/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-vib.html)); the pressure-to-wind step follows Tectonics.js, whose formulas were not read, only their call sites. Monsoons are meant to emerge where the summer low draws onshore flow. On a compressed flat map the Coriolis effect is a rule, not a physical quantity. This replaces the forge's strictly zonal row winds with no seasonal shift.

**Ocean currents** are simulated by no generator the researcher read. The proposal is to mechanise Pasta's gyre rules: classify each ocean cell by latitude band and by its position in the basin, apply a sea-surface temperature anomaly (warm on the western side of basins in the subtropics, flipped poleward of about 45°), add an upwelling index where wind runs along a coast, and let the wind carry the result onshore. Magnitudes of up to +15 °C and −10 °C on high-latitude coasts are Pasta's air-temperature rules of thumb. A steady wind-driven stream-function solve is offered as a more physical alternative from textbook memory. All of this is unverified design, and it is the part of the climate most likely to need iteration, because it is what produces mild west coasts and fog deserts.

**Precipitation** is a steady-state moisture advection per month: evaporation over sea rising with temperature, transport by semi-Lagrangian backward steps, and rain-out with four terms, namely convergence near the thermal equator, suppression under the subtropical high, a frontal boost in the westerlies (stronger in winter), and orographic uplift. Frozen Fractal's version of the advection converged in 25–50 steps and about a second for a whole globe ([part 7](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2023/12/15/around-the-world-7-temperature-and-rain/)). For the orographic term the verified option is Smith and Barstad's linear model, a Fourier-space transfer function whose form and example parameters were read from the authors' extended abstract ([Smith & Barstad](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/62756.pdf)); the full paper was paywalled. It assumes real metres and a uniform wind per window, so on a compressed planet the researcher prefers the simpler upslope term, moisture times the wind component up the slope, evaluated on the fine heightmap. That is close to what the forge's `orographic` sweep does along rows today, generalised from row direction to a wind vector (synthesis).

**Derived fields** are one pass over twelve months: snow by a degree-day balance iterated to a periodic state, with permanent snow where it never returns to zero; sea-ice months from monthly sea temperature; growing degree days; potential evapotranspiration by Thornthwaite, which needs only monthly temperature and day length ([Wikipedia: Potential evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_evapotranspiration)); an aridity index; a monthly soil-water bucket whose overflow is runoff. The Thornthwaite formula is verified; the snow, melt, bucket and freezing-point constants are tagged unverified background by the researcher.

### Storage: keep the monthly fields off the 16 m grid

The audit quantifies the naive option. Twelve monthly temperature and twelve monthly precipitation rasters at `u16` add 48 bytes per cell to today's 17: about **+1.9 GB raw at 39 million cells**, and decoded store tiles grow from about 1.4 MB to about 4.5 MB, so the 256 MiB tile cache holds about 57 tiles of 625. The compressed size of a current 100 km world is not recorded anywhere the auditor found, so the true growth on disk is unknown.

The cheaper encodings are well supported. A single annual sinusoid is published as adequate for "the vast majority" of monthly temperature and precipitation climates ([Berghuijs & Woods 2016](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-simple-framework-to-quantitatively-describe-monthly-precipitati-3/)), with expected failure for two-peak tropical rainfall (an abstract seen through a search summary). The researcher's own sensitivity argument, which no study was found to quantify, is that one harmonic suffices for temperature but precipitation needs at least two, and that January-plus-July storage is the reason both seasonal hobby generators need special monsoon handling. Their conclusion is that twelve values quantised to a byte each, 24 bytes per cell, "remove all doubt", and that the choice is about storage, not accuracy.

The recommendation here combines that with the audit's observation that climate varies over kilometres and the 16 m grid is needed only for the lapse-rate term: store twelve monthly normals of sea-level-reduced temperature and of precipitation on a coarse climate grid, re-apply the lapse from the fine height at read time, and keep the existing fine annual precipitation raster as the spatial modulator for rain shadows. At 256 m climate cells a 100 km world has 392² cells, and 24 bytes each is under 4 MB (arithmetic in this report). Three format consequences follow. The artifact's raster lists are fixed arrays of five and five, mirrored in `QRasterId::ALL`, `WorldGlobals::q_scale` and the tile struct, so a second, coarser raster family is a format change, with bumps to `ARTIFACT_VERSION`, `TILED_FORMAT_VERSION` and, if the map shows it, `WORLD_MAP_VERSION` and the `TileSummary` wire type. The runtime currently assumes the default lapse because the world file does not store the knobs it was forged with; the knobs, pole choice and latitude must move into the artifact. And runtime sampling must keep to the exact-arithmetic rule of the sampling path (only add, subtract, multiply, divide, floor and square root), which integer monthly tables satisfy.

### Validation is the one part with hard numbers

The quickest objective check is Köppen class shares on land. Peel, Finlayson and McMahon's criteria were read from the paper and are exact (for example, arid B where annual precipitation is under ten times a threshold of twice the mean annual temperature plus 0, 14 or 28 by rain season; B tested before A, C and D), and so are Earth's shares: **arid 30.2%, cold 24.6%, tropical 19.0%, temperate 13.4%, polar 12.8%**, with hot desert the largest single type at 14.2% ([Peel et al. 2007](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/11/1633/2007/hess-11-1633-2007.pdf)). Beck's newer 1 km maps could not be opened. The proposed suite (inferred) is an Earth regression that feeds real topography through the model and compares against a reference Köppen raster, named-regime checks (Mediterranean on west coasts at 30–40°, cool fog deserts at 15–30°, oceanic climate at 45–60°, continental interiors, monsoon Asia), and three synthetic worlds: all ocean, one rectangular pole-to-pole continent, and a single north–south mountain wall whose rain shadow must flip sides between the trade-wind and westerly belts. The agreement targets in that suite (70% by area, ±5 points on shares) are the researcher's suggestions. On generated worlds shares legitimately vary with land distribution, so the gate should be ranges, not Earth's exact figures. The existing forge climate tests pin today's model (lapse exactly 6.5 °C/km, rain-shadow direction per band, the annual latitude table) and will be rewritten with it.

## Most water features appear once the sea is allowed to move

### The blockers are specific

The audit lists which mechanism is missing for each feature. Fjords, rias and estuaries are blocked because the ocean mask is fixed before erosion, land is clamped above zero and sea level never changes. Deltas and braided rivers are blocked because routing is single-receiver D8 and a reach is a tree edge, so "no distributaries, so no deltas or braided channels are representable", and because sediment is deposited only inside depressions. Lagoons, barrier islands and atolls have no coastal process at all. Glacial lakes, cirques and U-valleys have no ice in the forge. Lakes exist essentially because nine bowls are carved onto valley floors, whatever the world's size, since "a fluvial steady state has no closed depressions of its own". Seasonal rivers have no seasonality to draw on, and the runtime writes each column's `water_top` once at genesis. Deep ocean, shelves, trenches and ridges are behind the −90 m clamp. Springs, aquifers and wetland open water have no groundwater.

### Erode against a lower sea, then flood it

The water researcher's central recommendation (inferred, but resting on verified facts) is to run fluvial and glacial erosion against a sea about 120 m lower and then raise the sea to zero. At the Last Glacial Maximum sea level was about 125 m lower and shelves were dry land ([Wikipedia: Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum)); most present estuaries sit in valleys cut during that lowstand and flooded since ([Hijma thesis record](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/37147)); rias are drowned river valleys and fjords drowned glacial troughs with an overdeepened basin behind a sill ([Wikipedia: Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord)). In forge terms: route and incise with base level at −120 m so rivers cross the exposed shelf; run the glacial pass where ice reached the coast and allow overdeepening below sea level; raise the sea, so every valley mouth below zero becomes a ria, estuary or fjord, shelf hills become islands and land bridges become straits; then partly refill drowned valleys where sediment supply is high. The researcher's claim that this costs "one extra erosion configuration, no new algorithm" holds for the routing but understates the forge change: the ocean mask must become a result (`h` below sea level after erosion) and the land clamp must go, which touches the invariants several tests assert.

### Lakes by water balance, rivers by regime, deltas as a second layer

Lake level should come from a water balance. A closed lake loses water only by evaporation ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/lake/The-hydrologic-balance-of-the-lakes)), so its area grows until evaporation from the surface equals inflow: equilibrium area is inflow divided by evaporation minus precipitation, found on the basin's hypsometry, and a lake is open and fresh only if that level reaches the spill point; otherwise it is a salt lake, or a playa when the equilibrium area is tiny. The equation is the researcher's formulation of the standard argument and they flag that no fetched source states it in that form. It replaces both the nine carved bowls and today's proxy rule for salt lakes, and it fixes lake counts by climate: arid depressions become dry basins, humid ones stay full. Lake origin should be tagged by whatever made the basin (rift, glacial trough, cirque, kettle, moraine dam, crater, caldera, oxbow, karst, lagoon, thermokarst), which means each terrain pass tags as it creates; the origin catalogue is background knowledge apart from a few cited dimensions, such as kettles being mostly under 2 km across and 10 m deep ([Wikipedia: Kettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_(landform))).

Rivers keep the existing network and gain a classification layer. Width and depth follow hydraulic-geometry power laws with exponents 0.5 and 0.4 (Leopold and Maddock, via search summaries of [USGS PP 252](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp252)); the forge's width rule is already a square root of discharge, but its "discharge" is a rain-weighted area, not a volume flow, so a conversion is needed. Braided versus single-thread is a slope threshold that falls with discharge (Leopold and Wolman, quoted through a later review; the conversion to SI units is the researcher's). Meanders, cutoffs and oxbow lakes come from a vector migration pass over low-slope reaches after Paris and colleagues, whose code is public but whose paper was not read ([project page](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/paris2023_Meanders.html)). A twelve-value monthly discharge per reach, from twelve accumulation passes over the existing flow graph with snow storage and melt, gives every regime at once: perennial, seasonal, ephemeral wadi, snowmelt-fed and glacier-fed, plus rivers that end in the desert once a transmission-loss term is added. Waterfalls and rapids are detected as knickpoints on the long profile and already arise where erodibility changes across a river. The Rosgen slope classes in the notes are from memory.

Deltas need two things the forge lacks: sediment reaching the coast, and a river object that can split. For the first, the deposition form of the stream-power law has a linear-time, implicit, unconditionally stable solver ([Yuan et al. 2019, abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019JGRF..124.1346Y)) with the same structure as the existing one; the mountains researcher ranks it the most valuable single addition because it also builds alluvial fans and basin fill and slows the decay of old ranges. For the second, the verified cheap method is Undiscovered Worlds': deltas are a separate layer, with branch mouths placed along the shore and each branch traced backwards from its mouth to an apex upstream, flow divided among branches and the plain flattened just above sea level ([Undiscovered Worlds, June 2019](https://undiscoveredworlds.blogspot.com/2019/06)). The simulated option is DeltaRCM, a parcel random walk at 50 m cells on grids of about a hundred cells, whose mechanics were read from the paper but whose run time was not extracted ([Liang et al. 2015](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/3/67/2015/)). Whether a mouth becomes a delta, estuary, ria or fjord is decided from sediment flux, wave energy, tidal range and offshore depth; the classification by dominant control is qualitative in the source ([Wikipedia: River delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)) and the decision rule is the researcher's proposal. In the artifact, `RiverReach` polylines must become a directed graph in which a node can have two downstream edges.

### Coasts are classified and stamped; named features are detected

The researcher found no graphics paper on procedural coastal landforms and says so with the caveat that absence in their searches is not proof. The catalogue therefore comes from geomorphology. Each 20–50 km coast segment is classified by tectonic setting from the plate model (collision coasts are steep with narrow shelves; trailing-edge coasts are flat with wide shelves, lagoons and big deltas, after Inman and Nordstrom, read in a [coastal dynamics text](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Oceanography/Coastal_Dynamics_(Bosboom_and_Stive)/02%3A_Large-scale_geographical_variation_of_coasts/2.03%3A_Tectonic_control_of_coasts/2.3.2%3A_Tectonic_plate_setting_of_coasts)), by drowning or emergence, by wave exposure computed as fetch weighted by the climate model's winds, by tidal class, by sediment supply and by winter sea temperature. The class picks what to build. Barrier islands and lagoons have verified conditions: wave-dominated coasts with tidal range under 4 m, a low-gradient shelf and ample sediment, on about 13–15% of the world's coasts ([Wikipedia: Barrier island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island)), and a verified geometric recipe from Dragons Abound, which offsets a smoothed copy of the coast ([Barrier Islands Revisited](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/12/barrier-islands-revisited.html?m=0)). Coral reefs have verified limits (few below 18 °C, mostly shallower than 50 m, absent near large river outflows and on upwelling west coasts) and Darwin's subsidence sequence from fringing reef to atoll ([Wikipedia: Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef)), which pairs naturally with the hotspot chains' island ages. Tidal range per coast is a scalar built from an open-ocean base of about 1 m damped in enclosed basins and amplified in funnel-shaped bays; the classes are verified ([Wikipedia: Tidal range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_range)), the amplification rule is textbook physics from memory. Dimensions for barriers, lagoons, inlet spacing, cliff retreat and shelf profiles are all unverified background.

Bays, gulfs, capes, peninsulas, straits and isthmuses are not generated; they are detected on the land–sea mask and named. The one crisp definition is legal: a bay's water area must be at least that of a semicircle on its mouth line ([UNCLOS Art. 10](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm)). The proposed pipeline (morphological closing of land at a ladder of radii to find concavities, the semicircle test, opening to find peninsulas and isthmuses) is standard image processing that the researcher says "needs prototyping". The world has no region or feature naming today (the map shows "Forest #17"), so naming is a new subsystem; Dragons Abound's scheme of a size-class term plus a specific from position, climate, a nearby city or a river transfers directly ([Bay Watch](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/07/the-naming-of-places-part-6-bay-watch.html)).

### Groundwater, ice and the resolution rule

A full groundwater simulation is unnecessary. A water-table depth field from height above the nearest drainage, damped by recharge and rock permeability, places springs, oases, aquifers and every wetland type; the topographic wetness index is the verified alternative and is documented as unreliable on flat ground ([Wikipedia: Topographic wetness index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_wetness_index)). The height-above-drainage method and the steady-state solve the researcher prefers are from background. This field replaces the forge's `drainage` value. Aquifers become a per-layer property with light and heavy classes, mirroring Dwarf Fortress. Sea ice, lake ice and river ice are month counts from the monthly climate; thermokarst lakes and pingos are stamps on flat permafrost with unverified dimensions.

Each feature belongs to the coarsest grid on which it spans three to five cells: the planet grid decides where and what type, the fine grid builds the shape, and anything narrower than a fine cell stays a vector or point object realised only when voxels are generated. Because `generate_region` must remain a pure function of coordinates plus the stored world, every coarse decision (eligibility, class, parameters, vector geometry) must be stored in the artifact, and every fine stamp needs a deterministic seed from the feature id and position. Sea stacks, arches, sea caves and cenote overhangs cannot be a heightfield and belong to the prefab layer the town kit already uses.

A per-column water surface covers all of this except four things, and all four are runtime limits: tidal variation, seasonal stage of lakes and rivers, floating ice, and overhangs. The static water layer cannot change levels after genesis; the flow engine is researched and prototyped but not in the engine, and two owner decisions on it are still open in `TODO.md`. Until then seasonal rivers, tides and freeze–thaw can be stored (a range or a twelve-month vector on the object) but not played.

## Seventy-two biomes are four layers, not one raster

### The backbone: Köppen for the label, plant limits for the vegetation

The biome researcher's recommendation is to compute Köppen for every land cell as a cheap, exact climate label and validation target, and not to map it one-to-one to biomes, because Köppen does not separate forest from grassland within a class and Holdridge's scheme ignores seasonality. Vegetation structure comes from a BIOME1-style sieve: each plant type has limits on coldest-month temperature, growing degree days and a moisture index, and the survivors' dominance decides the biome. The verified constants are the LPJ limits, read from the model's supplement: tropical trees need a coldest month of at least 15.5 °C; temperate broadleaved evergreens 3.0 to 18.8 °C with at least 1,200 growing degree days above 5 °C; temperate summergreens −17.7 to 15.5 °C with 1,200; boreal needleleaved evergreens −32.5 to −2 °C with 600; larch down to −46.5 °C with 350 ([LPJmL4 supplement](https://gmd.copernicus.org/preprints/gmd-2017-145/gmd-2017-145-supplement.pdf)). The BIOME1 moisture thresholds and Holdridge's numeric boundaries are recalled and "need checking before they become named constants". Moisture must be an index that uses evapotranspiration, since 500 mm is forest at 2 °C and semi-desert at 28 °C.

Four other anchors are verified. Treeline is a temperature line that needs no elevation rule: forest is impossible where the growing season, counted as days above 0.9 °C, is shorter than 94 days or averages below 6.4 °C ([Paulsen & Körner 2014](https://doc.rero.ch/record/324784?ln=fr), seen as a snippet); applied to lapse-corrected temperature it produces altitudinal belts by itself and retires the forge's rule that anything above 1,200 m is `Mountain` whatever its climate. Dryness classes are the UNEP aridity index, hyper-arid below 0.05, arid to 0.20, semi-arid to 0.50, dry sub-humid to 0.65, together 47.2% of land ([Wikipedia: Aridity index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridity_index)). In the tropics, rainfall of 1,000–2,500 mm with a dry season under seven months is bistable: "only fire differentiates between savanna and forest" ([Staver et al. 2011](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-global-extent-and-determinants-of-savanna-and-forest-as-alter/), abstract), so the generator should roll a persistent, spatially coherent fire state there rather than draw a rainfall line; the probability ramp and smoothing passes are the researcher's own. Mangroves follow winter warmth, classically a 20 °C winter sea temperature with real limits from 12.5 to 22 °C, and die back at freezes of −6.3 to −7.6 °C ([Quisthoudt et al. 2012](https://www.africamuseum.be/publication_docs/Quisthoudt%20et%20al%202012%20Temperature%20variation%20among%20mangrove%20-%20Trees.pdf); [Osland et al.](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/mangrove-expansion-and-contraction-a-poleward-range-limit-climate-extremes-and-land); snippets).

Beyond those anchors the researcher is direct about the footing: every threshold marked as inference "is a starting value to be tuned … none is a literature constant". That covers the leaf-type decision table, the desert surface rules, all wetland hydroperiods, the cloud-forest belt, the marine depth and light rules, and the compact rule table for all 72. The rules for heath and moor, shrubland and woodland-savanna are called the least grounded, because on Earth all three are shaped by land use and fire. The single most relevant reference for per-biome drivers, the IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology, was not opened and should be the first thing checked when the rules become a spec. Comparisons with other generators are thin for the same reason: Minecraft and TerraFirmaCraft were read; Azgaar's table came from a snippet; Dwarf Fortress and WorldEngine are recalled.

### Classification in passes

The proposed algorithm (inferred) runs in passes and keeps the zonal result underneath every override. It first derives the indices; then classifies sea cells by ice, depth, and shallow overlays; hard land states (ice, young lava, lakes and rivers by type); the shore strip (mangrove, salt marsh, mudflat, delta, rocky or sandy shore); zonal vegetation by continuous scores in the order cold limit, dry limit, semi-arid, humid, montane specials; azonal overrides for substrate and water (salt flat, sand, badlands, the wetland ladder from tropical peat swamp to seasonal floodplain, oases); the fire and disturbance state; underground classes per column; and last a land-use overlay from the history simulation. Wetlands are placed by water, not climate, and climate picks the type: peat needs a positive water balance or permanent waterlogging, trees against herbs splits swamp from marsh, and rain against groundwater splits bog from fen near pH 5 to 5.5 ([peatland glossary](https://www.ipcc.ie/a-to-z-peatlands/peatland-glossary/), snippet). Desert surface is decided by geomorphology, and sand must be the minority of desert (the 20–25% share is recalled). Boundaries should come from continuous fitness scores with the top two biomes and a weight stored per cell, and from noise added to the climate inputs, not the output, which the researcher supports with TerraFirmaCraft's centre-of-range density and Minecraft's nearest-interval fallback ([TFC docs](https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Documentation/1.18.x/worldgen/decorators); [Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)).

The reading this report takes from that structure (synthesis) is that the list of 72 is not one enum on one raster. About fifty entries are surface cell biomes, land and marine. Rivers by type, vents, seeps, geothermal pools, springs and oases are objects or point features. Dry caves, underground streams, aquifers and sea caves are per-column underground properties. Cropland, pasture, plantation and settlement are an overlay from history, which the researcher recommends storing over the potential natural biome so that abandoned land reverts by succession. Abyssal plain and trench are depth classes on the planet grid's bathymetry if the playable sea floor stays clamped. The artifact then carries a surface biome, a potential biome with a second choice and weight, and the object and overlay layers, and all 72 still fit a `u8`.

The classifier needs fields the current plan does not list, by the researcher's own count: prevailing wind direction (sand transport, windward slopes, wave fetch); relative humidity or cloud-base height (cloud forest, fog desert); tidal range; lava or surface age; a carbonate, evaporite and porosity distinction in rock type (karst, aquifers); a turbidity proxy from river sediment; and soil pH or base status. A small soil model (depth, texture, fertility, drainage from climate, relief, parent rock and time) resolves several otherwise arbitrary splits and serves farming; its associations are recalled pedology with no source fetched. The forge's six rocks do include limestone, but one global column gives every continent the same sequence, so spatially varying geology is again a prerequisite.

### The consumer bill in the codebase

`Biome`, `classify` and `TileSummary` live in the `game` crate, so biome work touches the wire protocol crate. Discriminants are stored as bytes, "append only, never renumber", so the 20 existing biomes keep their numbers and new ones are appended. `CellClimate`, the classifier's single input, gains the seasonal and hydrological indices. The audit lists the consumers that are exhaustive matches or tables sized by `Biome::ALL.len()`: ground cover per biome in `material.rs`, where a missing block name turns **all** biome covers off (`BiomeBlocks::from_names` is all or nothing) and new covers need manifest entries and art; `treeless`, `forest` and `biome_column` in `lib.rs`; glacier thickness and tundra snow keyed on two specific biomes in `forged.rs`; the tree density table and per-species biome lists for eight species, where a new biome gets no native trees until listed; the seed score's "ten distinct biomes"; map layers and the colour table with its spread test; and history's capacity rule, whose wildcard arm silently gives new harsh biomes full capacity. Regions are typed by `RegionType`, not biome, so 72 biomes can keep 10 to 15 region types without exploding the region count. Only trees exist as vegetation, ground plants are a stated non-goal of the current design, and creatures have no biome link, so a biome is for now a ground cover, a tree mix, a map colour and a history capacity. TerraFirmaCraft's separation, in which the biome label is a summary and each species carries its own climate envelope, is the researcher's recommended direction and matches the niche-based tree placement the forge already has.

## Runtime seasons are a pure function of tick and place

### What shipped games do, and the one design that fits

The runtime researcher read wikis and source for the games that matter. Most colony and survival games use a calendar plus per-biome tables and a small stateful weather machine. Dwarf Fortress has the same 12 × 28-day calendar this project already uses, integer temperatures, rain or snow by temperature, and freezing as an instantaneous and lethal switch ([DF wiki: Weather](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Weather)). Two games compute climate from position and date. TerraFirmaCraft adds a monthly term scaled by a triangle wave of latitude and a day-seeded daily offset, with hysteresis thresholds for snow and ice ([OverworldClimateModel.java](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/1.20.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/util/climate/OverworldClimateModel.java)). Vintage Story's precipitation, read from its published source, is the design this project needs: **a stateless function of position and total days**, three-dimensional noise sliding along x and evolving in time, offset by the world-generation rainfall value and thresholded ([WeatherSystemBase.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSystemBase.cs)). Its cloud and wind patterns are the opposite, a per-region state machine with saved random state, blended from the four nearest regions and synced to clients, and that is exactly the part not to copy, because it needs neighbouring state to agree at a seam.

The project's own rules point the same way. The audit quotes them: rollback must be bit-exact, sim paths use `libm` or integers, randomness is only stateless hash rolls, regions talk only by messages, a seam changes nothing observable, and parked regions do not tick, so "anything seasonal in a region must be recomputable from the world tick when it wakes". Weather that is a pure function of world tick, global position and stored climate satisfies all of it for free. The researcher's conclusion (inferred, from first principles; no published multi-server example was found) is that such weather adds no `RegionInput` or `Transfer`, no table row two hosts could both write, nothing to a save cut and nothing to the state hash, and that the client can compute it from the same function in `game`, so weather is never streamed. One correction to today's plumbing is needed: the calendar lives in the coordinator and reaches hosts as a message that "nothing in the simulation reads"; a sim-visible date must be derived from the world tick, which every region already has, with the existing constants (200 ticks per day, 336 days per year).

### The function

All of the following is the researcher's design. Temperature at a point is the seasonal value interpolated between the twelve stored monthly normals (integer arithmetic with 28-day months, no sine needed), plus a diurnal term from a small table scaled by cloud and day length, plus a slow anomaly field, minus the lapse for the column's height above the climate cell's mean, all in `i16` centi-degrees so thresholds are exact integer compares. Precipitation follows the weather-generator literature turned stateless. Richardson's generator, verified through a comparison paper, uses a two-state Markov chain for wet and dry days, a gamma distribution for amounts and temperature conditioned on wet or dry ([Semenov et al. 1998](https://www.int-res.com/articles/cr/10/c010p095.pdf)); a Markov chain is stateful and has no spatial coherence, so the equivalent is to map one smooth space–time noise field to a uniform value and call a cell wet when it falls under the cell's monthly wet-day probability, then reuse the same value for the amount through a quantile table so rain is heaviest at a system's core. Wet fraction is exact by construction; persistence comes from the noise's time scale and needs an offline calibration run, since no source gives the mapping in closed form. Systems move by sampling the noise at a position advected by the stored seasonal wind, with two phase-shifted samples cross-faded to avoid unbounded shear. Named storms are stateless objects: for each epoch and coarse cell a hash roll decides birth, lifetime, radius and intensity, the track is a closed-form curve along the steering wind, and any region enumerates the same few candidates. The noise is value noise on an integer lattice hashed by global lattice coordinates with the existing splitmix-style `roll`, interpolated with an integer smoothstep, identical on wasm32. Year-to-year variability must be added on purpose, because daily generators are known to understate it ([Semenov et al. 1998](https://www.int-res.com/articles/cr/10/c010p095.pdf)): a per-year anomaly over subcontinent-sized areas, a multi-year oscillation, and rare events with exact return periods, which also gives the world's past weather for free.

The forge must therefore store, per climate cell and month, more than totals: mean temperature, diurnal range, precipitation and wet-day fraction. If only totals exist the wet-day fraction is derived from a rule by climate type. The relations the researcher recalls for this (Geng and colleagues' coefficients) are unverified and "must be checked before use".

### What cannot be a pure function, and where it lives

Snow depth, ice thickness, soil moisture and river level are integrals with a floor at zero. The researcher gives each a cheap exact answer. For snow, any cell that is snow-free at some point in the year has a late-summer date with zero snow regardless of history, so depth on any day is determined by at most 336 daily steps from that date: a pure function that can be memoised in a cache outside hashed state. The cheaper variant has the forge store a twelve-value normal snow curve per cell. Ice follows Stefan's law, thickness proportional to the square root of accumulated freezing degree-days ([Wikipedia: Stefan's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan%27s_equation)), with coefficients seen in search summaries; a continuous thickness gives gameplay Dwarf Fortress lacks, such as thin ice and a river crossable for six weeks, with no instant killing freeze. River level is the forge's monthly discharge normal times a basin anomaly. Vintage Story proves the coarse version ships: hourly snow deltas at eight points per 512-block region, summed when a chunk loads ([WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs](https://github.com/anegostudios/vsessentialsmod/blob/master/Systems/Weather/WeatherSimulationSnowAccum.cs)). Flicker at thresholds is avoided without state by thresholding a trailing multi-day mean.

Where the result lives is the decisive design point, and the audit and the researcher agree on it. Snow, ice, foliage and ground cover are today blocks baked at genesis and saved with the region; seasonal change by mass block edits would cost rollback, save and network, while a derived overlay computed from (date, stored climate) "fits the existing recipe pattern, the first fights it". Snow depth and ice thickness should be recipe data on the column in the manner of `water_top`, never voxels, with only player edits (a cleared path, a hole in the ice) stored as sparse deltas with an expiry. True state, such as soil moisture and crops, is region-owned, stepped daily under the normal rollback machinery, kept per plot or climate cell, and caught up on wake as a function of saved state and the two ticks only. Weather that depends on entities must be a world-level event recorded by the coordinator with global coordinates and a start tick, delivered before it begins. On the client, `SUN_DIRECTION` and the sky are constants today; sun position comes from a day-length table by latitude row and day, built once at world load, and every new per-world resource must be registered for session reset. Cost is estimated, not measured: weather evaluated per climate cell every ten ticks is nanoseconds per tick.

### The 56-minute year is a design problem before it is a tuning problem

One game day is 10 real seconds, a month is 4 minutes 40 seconds and a year is 56 minutes. That is Dwarf Fortress's fortress pace (1 hour 7 minutes per year at its default frame cap) and far faster than RimWorld's roughly 16 hours 40 minutes ([DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time); [RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time)). At that pace a visible day–night cycle is a strobe, a three-day storm lasts 30 seconds, and diurnal temperature is noise to the player. The researcher's options (inferred): keep the pace and drop diurnal effects in fortress view, so seasons are the unit that matters, as in Dwarf Fortress, which shows no clouds or fog in fortress mode; or lengthen the day, as RimWorld did when it doubled day length and halved days per year. Dwarf Fortress's own answer, the same calendar running 144 times slower per tick in adventure mode, is not available here, because fortress and adventure players share one world tick; and running separate "weather days" beside calendar days is called inadvisable because two clocks is the kind of thing that breaks seams. Two smaller consequences belong with this decision. With both poles on the map the southern half's seasons are reversed while the calendar year starts on the first day of spring, so season names must be local or neutral. And with monthly rainfall stored, wet and dry seasons need no special case; only the name shown to the player is classified from the normals.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the problem more than it supplies a blueprint. The owner's five goals read like five features, but they reduce to three structural reversals in the forge: land and sea must become an outcome of crust and sea level instead of an input; climate must be stored as a year instead of a mean; and rivers, lakes and coasts must become typed objects with origins instead of the residue of flow routing. Once those hold, a large share of the feature list stops being separate work. Drowned valleys, fjords, shelf islands, salt lakes, playas, treelines, altitudinal belts, rain shadows, monsoons and wet and dry seasons are consequences, not items, and the runtime inherits its seasons from stored normals without adding a byte to the state hash.

The other shift is in where the risk sits. The physics the forge already has is the well-evidenced part, and the well-evidenced additions (a glacial term in the same solver, deposition, rebound, Köppen as a gate) are incremental. The parts with the least evidence are the ones that most define "Earth-like": plates on a flat cylinder with invented polar edges, ocean currents by rule, and seventy-two sets of thresholds that are starting values. Those are tuning problems that only maps and validation shares can settle, which makes the cheapest early investments the unglamorous ones: the Earth-topography regression, the synthetic test worlds, a continent-and-belt score for rejection sampling, and a measured baseline for the forge at full size. A pocket planet 100 km across will never be Earth to scale, and the plan should say so in its presets; what it can be is a world whose mountains, coasts, climates and seasons are where they are for reasons a player can read off the map.
