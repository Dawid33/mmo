**Goal:** Every forged world's climate comes out of one twelve-month model: temperature whose seasons, lag and continentality follow from heat capacities; pressure belts and winds that migrate with the sun and bend round the continents; rain that follows the wind, the thermal equator, the subtropical highs and the mountains — stored as Phase 1b's monthly normals, with the fine annual rasters derived from them.

**Architecture:** Two stages. (1) A **planetary stage** (`forge/climate/`: `insolation`, `ebm`, `circulation`, `ocean`, `moisture`) on a FIXED 256 × 128 model grid, run ONCE per forge from the world's land mask and four knobs: closed-form insolation → a seasonal energy-balance temperature (land and ocean heat capacity, diffusion, wind advection, ice ramp, outgoing radiation solved for a 15 °C mean) → monthly pressure and wind → ocean-current anomalies by basin side → a steady moisture transport giving the monthly precipitation of FLAT ground. It reads no height. (2) A **fine stage** (`forge/climate/fine.rs`) on the forge grid, re-run as erosion changes the relief: four axis sweeps (wind from W, E, N, S) give each fine cell a windward / lee factor per direction, blended by the model's wind — today's row sweep generalised. The annual fine rasters are DERIVED from the monthly family; the container's format does not change and no version constant is bumped. Köppen, the synthetic worlds and every derived field live forge-side (`forge/koppen.rs`, `forge/climate/derived.rs`, `forge/climate/synthetic.rs`), never in `game` or `forged.rs`.

**Tech Stack:** Rust; crate `worldgen` only (the forge, the `forge_proto` and `world_map` examples). `game` is NOT touched. No new dependencies.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-earth-like-world-overhaul-design.md` (Intent; Goals 1, 5, 6; Non-goals; Approach A; Decisions 4, 6–9, 12; the Phases table; "Open questions": ocean currents). - **Roll-exactness** (the half-width shift: "the climate of a world rolled by half its width equals the rolled climate, exactly"). It holds by construction only if EVERY planetary step obeys five rules; a step that cannot is BLOCKED, not approximated:

  2. any sum or mean over x (a zonal mean, the global mean) goes through `grid::exact_sum` (fixed-point integers): a float sum's rounding depends on where the row starts;
  3. smoothing along x is either a direct window sum in a fixed order RELATIVE to the cell (`grid::window_mean`, the model grid's small radii) or a running sum of FIXED-POINT integers (`fine::blur_exact`, the fine grid's large radii) — never a float running sum started at column 0 (which is what `box_blur_rows` is: the new model does not call it);

## Shared vocabulary (decided here, used by every task)

- **Three grids.** *Fine grid*: the forge grid of a level (`World { nx, nz, dx }`). *Climate grid*: Phase 1b's container grid (`climate_stride`; `cnx × cnz`). *Model grid*: `MODEL_NX = 256`, `MODEL_NZ = 128`, `MODEL_DEG = 180.0 / 128.0 = 1.40625` degrees per cell both ways (a 2 × 2 block of Phase 2's planet cells); the *EBM grid* is `EBM_NX = 128`, `EBM_NZ = 64` (2.8125°, a 2 × 2 block of model cells). All are row-major, x west → east and wrapping, z north → south and not wrapping; cell `(cx, cz)`'s centre is at latitude `90 − (cz + 0.5) · 180 / nz` (north positive) — `latitude_at`'s line.
- **The map is flat.** One degree of longitude is one degree of latitude everywhere (360° across the width, 180° down the height); meridians do not converge. The Laplacian is `∂²/∂λ² + ∂²/∂φ²` in radians; areas are cell counts. The SUN is not flat: insolation and day length are the sphere's, by latitude. Two means exist and are never mixed: the **planet mean** `⟨·⟩` weights each row by `cos φ` (only the 15 °C solve uses it — Ruling 2); every validation SHARE is unweighted (Decision 12).
- **Months.** Phase 1b's: `m` in `0 .. 12`, month `m` is calendar month `m + 1`; the year starts at the northern spring equinox; the northern summer solstice is at 3.0 months; "July" = month 3, "January" = month 9. The orbital angle from the March equinox at model time `t` months is `θ = 2π · t / 12`. **A model month is a twelfth of a YEAR**: precipitation "per month" and every per-year threshold from the literature (Köppen's, Thornthwaite's) apply unscaled. Where a literature formula counts days it uses `EARTH_DAYS_PER_MONTH = 365.25 / 12` (Thornthwaite's `N`, degree days, melt); only the wet-day fraction counts the calendar's 28 game days (`DAYS_PER_MONTH`). The model year lasts `YEAR_S = 365.25 · 86_400` seconds for the heat capacities' lag.
- **Half-years for Köppen.** Peel's AMJJAS is months `0 ..= 5` (equinox to equinox, the northern summer half), ONDJFM is months `6 ..= 11`. A place's "summer" is whichever half has the higher mean temperature (ties: `0 ..= 5`).
- **Degrees to cells.** A radius or length given in degrees on a grid of `cell_deg` per cell is `grid::deg_cells(deg, cell_deg) = max(1, round(deg / cell_deg))` cells (8° on the model grid is 6 cells, 10° is 7, 20° is 14, 4° is 3); a latitude LIMIT ("`|φ| ≤ 35°`") selects the rows whose CENTRE latitude satisfies it. On the fine grid `fine::deg_cells_fine(deg, w) = radius_cells(deg · metres_per_degree(height), dx)` — today's rule (none under one cell).
- **The polar rows.** No latitude is ever clamped: the outermost model rows' centres are at ±89.3°, where `tan φ` is finite and the insolation's `cos h0` is clamped to −1 ..= 1 by its own rule. North and south the grids do not wrap: a missing neighbour is the cell itself (Neumann), a window is clamped, a gradient is one-sided. The container's sampler clamps z to its outermost row, and the annual temperature raster is built by the SAME clamped read of the same twelve planes (`grid::resample` from the climate grid): the two cannot disagree at the polar edge by more than the quantisation steps (Phase 1b's placeholder disagreed by up to 1 °C there).
- **Planes** on the model grid are month-major like the container's: `i = m · MODEL_NX · MODEL_NZ + cz · MODEL_NX + cx`.
- **Units and signs.** Temperature °C at SEA LEVEL (the container's datum); precipitation mm per month; pressure hPa as an ANOMALY from the map mean (positive = high); wind m/s, `east` toward +x, `south` toward +z (Phase 1b's planes: a trade wind in the northern tropics has `east < 0`, `south > 0`). "Wind FROM the west" has `east > 0`. `η` (eta) is the belt coordinate of Ruling 3 (0 at the thermal equator, 1 at the pole of that side). Land fraction `ℓ` in 0 ..= 1 per model cell.
- **`ClimateKey`** — everything the planetary stage reads besides the land: `{ global_mean_temp_c, rainfall, inland_reach_deg, ocean_currents }` (`ClimateKey::DEFAULT = { 15.0, 1.0, 18.0, 1.0 }`).

## Rulings (made while writing this plan; each `what — why — cost if wrong`, with the alternative rejected)

1. **The planetary stage runs on a fixed 256 × 128 model grid (the energy balance on 128 × 64), ONCE per forge, and reads no height; relief acts only through the fine stage and the lapse.** The program says "on the planet grid"; Phase 2 fixed that grid at 512 × 256 and left the choice open ("Phase 3 … may adopt `PLANET_NX × PLANET_NZ`"). Half of it is taken because (i) the container it fills is 1.4°–1.9° per cell on Dev, Tiny and Small anyway (Phase 1b Ruling 1), so a 0.7° model would be averaged away on three of five sizes; (ii) the energy balance is an elliptic problem — with climlab's `D` an explicit step on 0.7° cells is limited to about 9 minutes of model time — and its cost grows with the square of the cell count per axis; (iii) a FIXED grid gives the same planetary climate at every world size and preview cell, as Phase 2's planet does. It runs once because it is a function of the land mask alone, taken from the FINEST level's `World::new` (the final world's mask; each coarser erosion level builds its own mask of the same coast, which the planetary fields are simply resampled onto) and no mask changes during a level (until Phase 4): erosion changes heights, and heights reach the climate only through the fine stage, which IS re-run at every `RAIN_REFRESH_ITERS` as today. **Time stepping:** 48 implicit steps a year, years repeated until the monthly means repeat within 0.02 °C (Ruling 2). **Budget:** energy balance 8,192 cells × 48 steps × 16 red–black sweeps × about 30 years (two passes: the builder check's 1-D sketch took 15–18 years for the first) = 1.9 × 10⁸ point updates; moisture 12 months × 128 iterations × 32,768 cells = 5 × 10⁷ bilinear updates; the rest is under 10⁷. Designed to **1.5 s on the dev box in release, the same at every size**: about +100 % on a Dev / 32 m forge (near 1.4 s after Phase 2), +15 % on Small / 32 m, +4 % on Medium / 32 m, under 1 % on Medium / 16 m; one-entry memo so a preview that changes an erosion knob does not pay it again. **The fine stage's true cost** (estimated, not measured; the builder check's count): per call 3 laps of each row sweep in BOTH directions (today 2 laps of one or two), two column sweeps, and twelve blur passes against six — **4 to 6 × today's `orographic`**; about 7 full-resolution-equivalent calls per forge (the coarse levels' calls are on 1/16 and 1/4 of the cells). If today's call is about 3 s at Large / 16 m (78.7 M cells; an assumption — Task 7 measures it), that is 60–110 s more on Phase 1a's measured 655 s: **+10 to +17 %** at the one size where it matters, a few per cent elsewhere beside the fixed planetary second. Accepted under "measured, not capped"; the cut, if Task 15 measures more than +20 % at Medium / 16 m or Large / 32 m: refresh the FACTORS at every second rain refresh on the finest level (a ruling then). **Transient memory:** the stage works one direction at a time in `f32` — at most 8 planes of 4 B (ground, land share, one factor, two blur scratch planes, the annual accumulator, the integer row buffer is per row) = **32 B per fine cell**, against the roughly 48 B of `f64` planes today's `orographic` holds (ground, rain, land and their blurs): no more than today, 2.5 GB at Large / 16 m. Measured (`climate.planetary_ms`, `climate.refresh_ms`, `climate.final_ms`; max RSS in Task 15), not capped — rejected: (a) the full model at every erosion level (four to seventeen runs: seconds on Dev for a result that cannot differ, since it reads no height); (b) the model on the climate grid (a different grid per size: the cross-size evidence of Phase 1a Task 18 would measure the grid, not the climate); (c) 512 × 256 with a multigrid solver (four times the linear work, a solver that is a task of its own, for detail three sizes cannot store) — cost if wrong: coasts and narrow seas under 1.4° (392 m on Dev … 784 m on Large) are blurred in temperature and flat rain; then `MODEL_NX/NZ` double (constants; the red–black rule still holds) and the budget quadruples. The planetary stage is blind to plateaus (no snow albedo over high ground, no blocking of the flow): Known limits.
2. **Temperature is a one-layer seasonal energy-balance model on a flat metric; the mean it is solved for is the cos-weighted planet mean.** On the EBM grid: `C(x,T) · ∂T/∂t = S(φ,t) · (1 − α(φ,T)) − (A + B·T) + D · ∇²T − K · (u, v) · ∇T`.
   - `S`: daily-mean top-of-atmosphere insolation `S0/π · (h0 · sin φ · sin δ + cos φ · cos δ · sin h0)`, `cos h0 = −tan φ · tan δ` clamped to −1 ..= 1 (polar night and day), `sin δ = sin ε · sin θ`; `SOLAR_CONSTANT_W_M2 = 1361.0`, `OBLIQUITY_DEG = 23.44`, a CIRCULAR orbit (the two hemispheres are mirror images half a year apart: what the all-ocean world checks) — read by the researcher; eccentricity rejected (1.7 % of flux, an asymmetry nobody would see, one more dial).
   - `α = lerp(ALBEDO_A0 + ALBEDO_A2 · P2(sin φ), ALBEDO_ICE, ice(T))`, `ALBEDO_A0 = 0.30`, `ALBEDO_A2 = 0.078`, `ALBEDO_ICE = 0.62` (climlab, second-hand), `ice(T) = 1 − smoothstep(ICE_FULL_C = −10.0, ICE_NONE_C = 0.0, T)` — a RAMP where climlab steps at −10 °C (the note's proposal: a step makes the solve jump); `T` of the previous step.
   - `C = ℓ · C_LAND + (1 − ℓ) · lerp(C_OCEAN, C_SEA_ICE, ice(T))`, `C = 4.2e6 J/m³/K × depth`: `LAND_HEAT_DEPTH_M = 2.0`, `OCEAN_MIXED_LAYER_M = 40.0`, `SEA_ICE_HEAT_DEPTH_M = 6.0`. Sources: the researcher's slab derivation (1–2 m and 40–70 m reproduce the observed one-month and two-to-three-month lags; ExoPlaSim's default is 50 m); the sea-ice value is RECALLED from North, Mengel & Short 1983 (untested here). The ocean depth is the dial for the PHASE of the maritime cycle: the builder check's 1-D sketch put the warmest month at 45° N in month 6 with 50 m (a slab's lag tends to three months, and backward Euler adds an eighth of a month), against the 4-or-5 target — hence 40 m to start, 30–50 m as its range. **Continentality and lag come out of these three numbers**; Phase 1b's amplitude, lag and `CONTINENTALITY_HALF_DEG` are deleted.
   - `OLR_B_W_M2_K = 2.0`; `A` starts at 210 and is SOLVED, one scalar, as the program asks (it also keeps a world with much high-latitude land out of a snowball). **The iteration, step by step** — after each model year: (1) `e = ⟨T̄⟩ − key.global_mean_temp_c`, `⟨T̄⟩` the cos-weighted mean of the year's 48 end-of-step fields through `exact_sum`; (2) `A += EBM_SHIFT_GAIN (1.0) · B · e`; (3) EVERY cell of the state `T −= EBM_SHIFT_GAIN · e`; (4) the year's twelve monthly means, as reported and as compared with the previous year's, are taken AFTER subtracting the same `e` from them. **Why it converges where an update of `A` alone does not:** the substitution `T' = T − e`, `A' = A + B·e` leaves `−(A + B·T)`, the diffusion and the advection unchanged, so the shifted state is an exact trajectory of the equation with the new `A` — except for the two terms that read `T` itself, the ice ramp in `α` and in `C`. Changing `A` alone moves the equilibrium by `−e` but leaves the mixed layer `e` away from it, to be closed at its own pace (`C/B` ≈ 2.7 years at 40 m, longer with the ice feedback): an integral controller on a lagged plant — the builder check's sketch of the first draft's rule (`A` only, gain 0.5) swung 2.7 → 22.7 → 8.4 → 19.5 °C with a 16-year period and was still ±2.5 °C off at year 40. With the shift the mixed layer has no memory of the error; what remains per year is the ice ramp's response to a uniform shift, a contraction while the ice–albedo gain is under 1 (the sketch: 15 years at `D` 0.8, 18 at 1.0). **This rests on a 1-D sketch, not on the model**: Task 3's first step is a convergence probe with a pass criterion and a named fallback. The target is a sea-level mean (Earth's 15 °C is a surface mean, about 1 °C lower: ignored). `Ebm::planet_mean_c` and `climate.planet_mean_c` are the ENERGY BALANCE's mean, before the current anomaly is added (Ruling 4; the anomaly's own mean is reported beside it).
   - `EBM_DIFFUSION_W_M2_K = 0.8` per rad² to start. climlab's 0.555 is a sphere's; the flat Laplacian conserves the UNWEIGHTED mean while the solve targets the cos-weighted one, so `D` and `A` are coupled (more `D` moves heat to low-weight rows and the solve answers). The sketch, all ocean, annual means: `D` 0.555 → equator 34.0 °C, polar row −26.0; 0.8 → 29.7 / −16.2; 1.0 → 27.3 / −10.8. Task 3's two targets overlap only near 0.8 and with no margin: **expect the fallback** — `D(φ) = EBM_DIFFUSION_W_M2_K · (1 + EBM_DIFFUSION_TROPIC_BOOST · cos² φ)` (more mixing in the tropics flattens the equator without warming the pole; one constant, starting 0 = off) — to be switched on in Task 3 or Task 9.
   - Advection: first-order upwind, implicit, `K · |wind| / Δ` with `EBM_ADVECTION_W_M2_K_PER_MS = 0.15` per radian (untested design: at 8 m/s it equals diffusion over about 25°, which makes west coasts maritime and east coasts continental without carrying sea air across a continent).
   - Solve: backward Euler, `EBM_STEPS_PER_MONTH = 4`; each step `EBM_SOR_SWEEPS = 16` red–black sweeps at `EBM_SOR_OMEGA = 1.7`, warm-started from the previous step's field; a sweep updates colour 0 (`(cx + cz) % 2 == 0`) and then colour 1; sources at the step's mid-time (`t = (s + 0.5) / 4` months for step `s` of the year); the wind of step `s` is month `s / 4`'s. Years repeat until `max |T_month(year) − T_month(year − 1)| < EBM_CONVERGED_C = 0.02` AND `|e| < 0.02`, at most `EBM_YEARS_MAX = 40` (the count is reported; hitting the cap is a finding). Monthly output = the mean of the month's four END-of-step fields, resampled to the model grid. `ω` 1.7 is near optimal for land-dominated rows and over-relaxed for ocean ones (both contract about 0.7 a sweep); with the non-symmetric upwind term and `ω > 1` convergence is not guaranteed — Task 4's first evidence run watches the residual, and the named fallback is `ω = 1.0` with 32 sweeps.
   - **What red–black leaves behind (no "exactly" about symmetry).** After 16 sweeps the two colours of one row are different iterates: about `0.7¹⁶ ≈ 3 · 10⁻³` of the step's change remains, a two-cell checkerboard of order 10⁻⁴ … 10⁻² °C on a field that should be zonally uniform; the north–south mirror swaps colours (`63 − 2·cz` is odd), so it is not bit-exact either. Symmetry lines are therefore judged to a tolerance from that stopping rule: `zonal_asym_c ≤ 0.01` (3 · 10⁻³ of at most 3 °C per step), `mirror_gap_c ≤ 0.05`. What IS exact is the roll by an even number of columns (it keeps colours) — the only bit-for-bit claims in this plan are rolls, round trips and integer counts. Pressure and wind do not see the checkerboard (`Ts` is window-smoothed first).
   - **Two passes** (`CLIMATE_PASSES = 2`): pass 0 with no wind → pressure and wind → pass 1 with advection, warm-started from pass 0's state AND its `A` (the solve continues) → the final pressure and wind from pass 1's temperature. No loop to convergence (a fixed count is exact under the roll and bounded in cost).
   - The lapse is not in the model: the container stays at sea level and `lapse_c_per_km` stays a knob with the readers it has. **Diurnal range** (per climate cell and month; untested design — the research consulted no source on it): `ℓ_c · (fine::DTR_LAND_C 8.0 + fine::DTR_DRY_C 8.0 · dry_m) + (1 − ℓ_c) · fine::DTR_SEA_C 3.0` (NEW names in `fine.rs`: the placeholder's `DIURNAL_*` constants keep their values until Task 11 deletes them), `dry_m = clamp(1 − P_m / PET_m, 0, 1)` (PET by Thornthwaite from the sea-level month; `dry_m = 0` when `PET_m = 0`), capped at 25.5. Aridity and land share only: continentality enters through `ℓ_c`, elevation not at all (the family is a sea-level datum).
   — rejected: a two-layer air / surface model (twice the state for an exchange coefficient nobody sourced); the spherical operator in φ with a flat x (zonal diffusion divides by cos² φ at the poles); explicit stepping (55,000 steps a year); ADI (a cyclic tridiagonal solve breaks the roll rule) — cost if wrong: the flat metric gives the polar rows as much weight as the equator, so `D`, its tropical boost and the ice ramp must carry the profile; a profile no setting reaches is recorded with its numbers in Task 9.
3. **Pressure is built per month from a thermal equator and a thermal anomaly; wind is the down-gradient direction turned by a rule; nothing is seeded.** All of it is the researcher's synthesis of Pasta's hand rules (untested design):
   - `Ts` = the month's sea-level temperature smoothed by a direct box of radius `CIRC_SMOOTH_DEG = 8` (x wraps, z clamps).
   - **Thermal equator** `φ_I(x, m)`: per column the warmth-weighted latitude `Σ φ · w / Σ w` over `|φ| ≤ ITCZ_SEARCH_DEG = 35`, `w = max(Ts − (the column's max WITHIN that band − ITCZ_WARM_BAND_C 2.0), 0)`; clamped to `± lerp(ITCZ_MAX_OCEAN_DEG 8, ITCZ_MAX_LAND_DEG 22, land share of the column within |φ| ≤ 25°)`; then a circular window mean of radius `ITCZ_ZONAL_SMOOTH_DEG = 20` along x. A centroid, not an argmax: the tropical profile is flat-topped.
   - **Belt coordinate** `η = (φ − φ_I) / (90 − φ_I)` north of `φ_I`, `(φ_I − φ) / (90 + φ_I)` south of it. **Zonal pressure** `p_z = −P_BELT_HPA (8.0) · cos(3π · η)`: low at the thermal equator, high at η = 1/3, low at 2/3, high at the pole. With `φ_I` 8° into the summer hemisphere the highs sit at 35° there and 25° in the winter hemisphere — Pasta's seasonal rule falls out of one formula.
   - **Thermal anomaly** `p_t = clamp(−P_THERMAL_HPA_PER_C (0.8) · (Ts − zonal mean of Ts), ± P_THERMAL_MAX_HPA 12)`, smoothed over `THERMAL_SMOOTH_DEG = 10`: winter highs over cold land, summer lows over hot land (the monsoon low). `p = p_z + p_t`, minus its map mean (`exact_sum`; the zonal mean of `Ts` likewise).
   - **Wind** = `g = −∇p` (hPa per degree; central differences, x wraps; at rows 0 and `MODEL_NZ − 1` the z difference is ONE-SIDED over one cell, not a clamped central one) rotated by `θ(φ) = sign(φ) · (90° − FRICTION_TURN_DEG 25) · min(1, |φ| / CORIOLIS_RAMP_DEG 12)` — clockwise on the map (north up) in the north: `(east, south) = (g_e · cos θ − g_s · sin θ, g_e · sin θ + g_s · cos θ)`; the sign is the GEOGRAPHIC hemisphere's and the turn fades to straight down-gradient flow at the equator. Speed `= WIND_MS_PER_HPA_DEG (10.0) · |g|`, capped at `WIND_MAX_MS = 20`. Check by hand: high at 30° N, low at the equator → `g` points south → wind toward the south-west: the north-east trades.
   - This replaces `westerly_weight`, `wind`, `Wind`, `BAND_BLEND_DEG`, the two band-edge knobs and the meander (bands now wander because continents heat and cool, not by a noise). **Stored** as Phase 1b's two `i8` planes (4 per m/s, 12 months): the month's mean vector, bilinear from the model grid to the climate grid.
   — rejected: a shallow-water step (the note: fragile on a flat compressed map); latitude-fitted wind curves (no land–sea effect: no monsoon) — cost if wrong: belts are rigid functions of one coordinate, so storm tracks do not tilt and there is no jet; calm cells give a direction of no meaning (the moisture step floors the speed, `WIND_CALM_MS`).
4. **Ocean currents are a RULE on basin sides, applied as a wind-carried temperature anomaly AFTER the energy balance; the stream-function solve is not built.** The design's open question ("by rule or by a stream-function solve") is closed for the rule: a stream function needs a β per degree on a planet that has none, a sparse solve that breaks the roll rule unless it too is red–black, and has no more evidence than the rule (both are the researcher's proposals; the rule at least mechanises a published hand method).
   - **Coasts that count**: the land fraction smoothed by a direct box of radius `CURRENT_LAND_SMOOTH_DEG = 4`; a model cell is "coast land" when that is ≥ 0.5 (islands a few degrees across steer no current). Per sea cell, along its row: `west_gap_deg` / `east_gap_deg` = degrees to the nearest coast land to the west / east (integer cell counts; a row with no coast land has neither: a circumpolar belt gets no anomaly).
   - **Target anomaly** (°C, constant through the year): `key.ocean_currents · [ sub(|φ|) · (CURRENT_WARM_SUB_C 3 · f(west_gap) − CURRENT_COLD_SUB_C 5 · f(east_gap)) + high(|φ|) · (CURRENT_WARM_HIGH_C 8 · f(east_gap) − CURRENT_COLD_HIGH_C 6 · f(west_gap)) ]`, `f(d) = exp(−d / CURRENT_REACH_DEG 12)`, `sub = smoothstep(8, 15) · (1 − smoothstep(35, 45))`, `high = smoothstep(40, 50) · (1 − smoothstep(70, 80))`. A sea cell with land close to its WEST is on the western side of its basin: warm in the subtropics (a poleward boundary current), cold at high latitude; with land close to its EAST it is on the eastern side: cold in the subtropics (an equatorward current), warm at high latitude. Plus **upwelling** on subtropical eastern sides: `− UPWELLING_C (3) · sub · f(east_gap · 3) · clamp(equatorward / 8 m/s, 0, 1)`, `equatorward = max(0, sign(φ) · south)` (in the north, `φ > 0`, the wind blows equatorward when `south > 0`; in the south when `south < 0` — the builder check's `−sign(φ)` contradicts its own gloss and is not taken). Magnitudes are scaled down from Pasta's AIR-temperature rules of thumb (+15 / −10 °C at 50–70°, "minor" near 20°): untested.
   - **Carried onshore**: per month the anomaly field `a` is the target over sea and over land obeys `d a / d s = −a / CURRENT_INLAND_REACH_DEG (10)` along the wind (the moisture step's Jacobi upstream iteration, `CURRENT_CARRY_ITERS = 48`). `t_sea[m] += a`. Onshore wind brings it inland (a mild west coast at 50°); offshore wind leaves the land alone.
   - It is added after the energy balance (not as a heat flux inside it) so that `ocean_currents = 0` removes it exactly and the solve for `A` does not fight it; the moisture stage reads the temperature WITH the anomaly (a cold coast evaporates less and is capped: Ruling 5).
   - **Its own tuning task (12) with before / after evidence, and a fallback:** the phase's evidence is run a second time with `--set ocean_currents=0`; the Köppen main-class ranges must hold there too (the west-coast check need not).
   — cost if wrong: gyres do not close, there is no circumpolar current, a strait or an inland sea is treated as an ocean basin; a world whose model is wrong here ships with `ocean_currents` lowered and the miss recorded.
5. **Moisture is a steady transport along the wind's DIRECTION with lengths in degrees; precipitation is what it rains out, times four rules; the planetary stage rains on flat ground.** Per month on the model grid, `W` relative to saturation at 15 °C (untested design; the structure is the note's recommendation):
   - `W_sat = exp(CC_PER_C (0.067) · (T − 15))`; sea-ice share `ice = 1 − smoothstep(−4, SEA_FREEZE_C −1.8, T)`.
   - One Jacobi iteration, every cell from the PREVIOUS iterate: `W'` = `W` read bilinearly at the offset `−d` cells (`d = wind / max(|wind|, WIND_CALM_MS 1.0)` in cells east and south, each component in −1 ..= 1; the read by Global Constraints' rule 6), then `W* = (W' + Δ · s · W_sat / SEA_RECHARGE_DEG) / (1 + Δ · [s / SEA_RECHARGE_DEG + M · (1 − ℓ · rec) / key.inland_reach_deg])`, `Δ = MODEL_DEG`, `s = (1 − ℓ)(1 − ice)`, `SEA_RECHARGE_DEG = 9.0` (today's value), then the mix `W ← (1 − MOIST_MIX) · W* + MOIST_MIX · mean of W*'s four neighbours` (`MOIST_MIX = 0.1`; a missing polar neighbour is the cell itself). `MOIST_ITERS = 128` from `W = 0.5 · W_sat` (information crosses 180°). Speed does not enter: a length in degrees must not depend on a rule's metres per second. In a calm cell the read is less than one cell upwind while the step still rains out `Δ` degrees' worth: rain-out is overstated where the air barely moves (accepted; calm cells sit at the pressure centres).
   - **Land recycling** `rec = RECYCLE_MAX (0.6) · smoothstep(0, 20 °C, T)`: warm land gives rain back, frozen land none. This and the summer thermal low are the two things the research names as missing in the model that lost Asia's interior to cold desert.
   - **Rain multiplier** `M = max(M_MIN 0.05, (1 + ITCZ_RAIN 3.0 · exp(−((φ − φ_I) / ITCZ_HALF_WIDTH_DEG 7)²) + FRONTAL_RAIN 1.2 · front · (1 + FRONTAL_WINTER_BOOST 0.5 · winter) + CONVERGENCE_RAIN 1.5 · clamp(−p_t / P_BELT_HPA, 0, 1)) · (1 − SUBSIDENCE_DRY 0.85 · clamp(p / P_BELT_HPA, 0, 1)) · (1 − COLD_CURRENT_DRY 0.5 · clamp(−a / 5, 0, 1)))` — convergence at the thermal equator; the thermal low's convergence (the monsoon); frontal rain between the subtropical high and the subpolar low (`front = max(0, sin(3π · (η − 1/3)))` for η in 1/3 ..= 2/3, `winter = clamp((T̄ − T_m) / max(T̄ − T_min, 1 °C), 0, 1)` with `T̄` the cell's annual mean and `T_min` its coldest month); suppression under ANY high (`p` is the total anomaly, so a summer land low cancels the belt's high: east coasts stay wet; the polar high and winter continental highs dry); a cap over cold currents.
   - **Flat precipitation** `P0_m = RAIN_UNIT_MM (60.0) · key.rainfall · W · M · (INLAND_REACH_REF_DEG 18 / key.inland_reach_deg)` mm per month. `RAIN_UNIT_MM` is tuned (Task 6) so the all-ocean equatorial belt reaches 200–300 mm in its wettest month and land averages 550–950 mm a year on forged worlds (Earth's 700–800 is recalled).
   - **Why a Mediterranean west coast should appear** (the phase's check, argued before it is measured): at 38° in summer `η` is 0.37 — under the high, suppressed, and the eastern-side cold current caps what is left; in winter `η` is 0.47 — the high has moved equatorward and `front` is near 1.
   - **Wet-day fraction** (per climate cell and month; the runtime note's inference, untested): `clamp(P_m / (28 · amount), 0, fine::WET_SHARE_MAX 0.9)`, `amount = lerp(fine::WET_AMOUNT_COOL_MM 5, fine::WET_AMOUNT_HOT_MM 14, clamp(T_m / 25, 0, 1))` — Phase 1b's rule under NEW constant names in `fine.rs` (the placeholder's `WET_DAY_*` are untouched until Task 11 deletes them). `P_m` is per twelfth of a year and the divisor counts 28 game days: a game day rains 8 % more than an Earth day would, by the vocabulary's month rule. **Snow fraction** is a derived field (Ruling 9), not part of the transport.
   — rejected: Smith & Barstad's linear model (real metres and seconds and one wind per FFT window; the note itself prefers the upslope term on a compressed planet); a pure wind march with no subsidence term (both seasonal hobby generators needed "encourage Mediterranean / rainforest / savanna" patches) — cost if wrong: regimes that need a jet or a storm track (double rainy seasons, east-coast cyclones) are absent; the multiplier's five constants are the tuning surface of Tasks 9 and 13.
6. **The fine stage is today's sweep, four ways: wind from W, E, N and S, blended by the model's wind; the monthly values on the climate grid come from block means of the four factor planes.** `fine::rain_factor` re-implements today's `orographic` sweep along rows (from W, from E; wrapping) and along columns (from N, from S; no wrap, starting at the polar sea) with ONE change of meaning: the air's moisture relaxes toward 1.0 over land AND sea (over `LEE_RECOVERY_DEG = 16.0` on land — today's `land_depletion_deg` default — and `SEA_RECOVERY_DEG = 9.0` over sea and lakes): the coast-to-interior drying is now the planetary stage's, so flat ground gets exactly factor 1. The rise still wrings (`moisture −= moisture · min(rise / orographic_scale_m, 1)`) and still rains on the windward side: a land cell's raw factor is `moisture · (1 + rise · lift)`, `lift = OROGRAPHIC_RUN_M / orographic_scale_m / dx` — today's line WITHOUT its `/ CONTINENTAL_MOISTURE`; a sea cell's factor is 1.0. Then, per direction, in this order: clamp to `ORO_FACTOR_MIN 0.25 ..= ORO_FACTOR_MAX 4.0`; the land-only blur — today's `blur(rain · land) / blur(land)` with rain 0 at sea, ONE radius `RAIN_BLUR_DEG = 1.5` along z then along x ("cross" and "along" mean nothing with four directions), sea cells and cells with no land in the window 1.0; blend with 1 by `climate_strength`. **The blurs are a new function, `fine::blur_exact`** (the ground blur of `TERRAIN_BLUR_M` too): each value is turned into `round(v · 2²⁰)` as `i64`, the window is a running INTEGER sum (exact, so it does not matter where a wrapping row starts), the result is `sum as f64 / (count · 2²⁰)` as `f32`; windows and counts are `box_blur_rows`' (the full `2r + 1` box round a wrapping row, shrinking boxes at the z edges). `box_blur_rows` itself is not called and not edited: it is a float running sum from column 0 (rule 3's forbidden case), and the old model and Phase 2's rebound keep it as it is. **N / S sweeps** walk the grid row by row carrying one moisture value per column (a state vector of `nx`), never column by column (one cache miss per cell). A wrapping W / E sweep laps until the moisture entering a lap equals the moisture that entered the previous one BIT FOR BIT (at most `SWEEP_LAPS_MAX = 6`; typically the third lap), and records that last lap: a lap contracts by about e⁻²² over land and e⁻⁴⁰ over sea at EVERY size (the lengths are degrees), so the fixed point is reached below one unit in the last place and the rolled sweep reaches the same one — except that two start columns could in principle settle on rounding orbits one ulp apart (about 10⁻¹⁰ per row; `climate.roll_identical` would show it).
   - **Direction weights** per model cell and month from the wind: `w_W = max(east, 0) / (|east| + |south|)`, `w_E = max(−east, 0) / …`, `w_N = max(south, 0) / …`, `w_S = max(−south, 0) / …` (calm: a quarter each). A diagonal wind is a mix of two axis sweeps — a wall is felt by the share of the wind that crosses it.

## Known limits (what the stylised model deliberately does not do)

- **It is not a circulation model.** Pressure is one cosine of a belt coordinate plus a thermal anomaly; there is no jet, no storm track, no weather. Wind is a rule's turn of the pressure gradient; Coriolis is a sign and a ramp.
- **The planetary stage reads no height.** No snow albedo over plateaus, no blocking or steering of the wind by ranges, no föhn warmth: relief acts through the lapse (at read time) and the fine stage's four axis sweeps. A range oblique to the wind casts a mixed, blurred shadow.
- **The map is flat for the air and round for the sun.** Polar rows carry as much area as equatorial ones in the diffusion and in every share; the 15 °C mean alone is cos-weighted.
- **Ocean currents are a rule on basin sides**, added after the energy balance: gyres do not close, there is no circumpolar current, straits and inland seas count as basins, the anomaly is the same all year but for upwelling. `ocean_currents = 0` is a supported world.
- **Rain regimes that need dynamics are absent**: double rainy seasons at the equator come only from the thermal equator crossing twice; no tropical cyclones, no east-coast winter storms; the monsoon is a thermal low's convergence term.
- **Inside one climate cell the months share one split** (windward and lee slopes differ in annual total, not in seasonality), and the diurnal range ignores elevation.
- **Small worlds**: mountains of a few hundred metres give weak rain shadows and no alpine `ET` / `EF`; the polar class there is latitude's alone, and the polar seas leave little land for it at any size.
- **Derived fields are forge-side functions, not stored**; Thornthwaite underestimates evapotranspiration in arid heat (capped at 26.5 °C: the corrected branch was not read). Phase 5 owns the dryland share, Phase 6 the runtime's snow.
- **Circular orbit, fixed tilt**: no eccentricity, and the tilt is a constant, not a knob.
- **No real-Earth regression** (Ruling 12) and no first-hand reading of the energy-balance literature behind the starting constants: every one of them was a starting value for Tasks 3–13.
- **No version bump**: a world forged before the switch still loads with the placeholder's climate.
- **Today's biome classifier reads the model's annual fields** until Phase 5; `game::biome`'s thresholds were set for the latitude table.
